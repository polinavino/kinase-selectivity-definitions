B. Use additional kinase selectivity datasets (Gao; Metz; Patricelli;
   Anastassiadis), or motivate why not
--------------------------------------------------------------------

I have substantially expanded the dataset coverage. The analysis now spans
four datasets across three assay technologies and two readout types. The
two-family clustering replicates on all four (new Results subsection "The
Two-Family Structure Replicates Across Assay Technologies", and new Figure 2),
as do the key instability-predictor relationships, most robustly the ratio
family's dependence on the top-1 vs top-2 affinity gap. The full per-feature
predictor structure is reported for the two pKd datasets (Klaeger and Davis) in
Table 2; the ratio/gap relationship replicates strongly across both, while some
family-specific predictors (notably the S-score's) are assay-dependent, which I
now note explicitly.

  - Anastassiadis et al. (Nat. Biotechnol. 2011): ADDED. 178 inhibitors x 300
    kinases, single-concentration radiometric activity assay (percent residual
    activity, converted to percent inhibition). See Methods, "Datasets".

  - Metz et al. (Nat. Chem. Biol. 2011): ADDED. 704 compounds profiled against
    >=50 of 172 kinases, pKi affinities. See Methods, "Datasets".

  - Gao et al. (Biochem. J. 2013): NOT ADDED, with stated reason. It is a
    single-concentration functional inhibition assay of the same readout type
    as the already-included Anastassiadis dataset, and its raw data are not
    openly redistributable. Including it would add a second instance of an
    existing assay type rather than a new modality. See Methods, "Datasets".

  - Patricelli et al. (Chem. Biol. 2011): NOT ADDED, with stated reason. It
    profiles only a small number of inhibitors against native kinases as a
    proof of concept for the KiNativ platform. A ranking-stability analysis
    requires many compounds to produce a meaningful ranking, so this dataset is
    not suited to the present comparison. See Methods, "Datasets".

The four datasets together span competition binding (Davis), chemoproteomics
(Klaeger), and functional activity (Anastassiadis, Metz/affinity), giving the
cross-technology robustness the reviewer's comment was aimed at.

C. Equations presented without context; explain their derivation from the
   original publications and what they mean
--------------------------------------------------------------------

I agree this was a gap, since the definitions are the foundation of the paper.
The "Selectivity Definitions and Parameterization" section has been rewritten so
that each definition is now (i) traced to its original source, (ii) related to
the form in which it is implemented here, and (iii) explained in words for the
non-specialist:

  - S-score: I now state Karaman et al.'s original definition (fraction of the
    panel inhibited below a percent-of-control cutoff, reported as S(35), S(10),
    S(1)) and explain that, working on the pKd scale, the equivalent "bound"
    criterion is an affinity threshold tau, and that sweeping tau reproduces
    their family of cutoffs.

  - Selectivity entropy: I now give Uitdehaag and Zaman's original
    association-constant weighting (K_a,i = 1/K_d,i, p_i = K_a,i / sum_j K_a,j)
    and explain that I implement the entropy on the same log-affinity scale as
    the other metrics, with the baseline beta made an explicit parameter. I note
    that the two weightings can rank compounds differently and that this is
    itself an instance of the definitional instability the paper characterizes.

  - Gini coefficient: I now explain the Lorenz-curve construction (twice the
    area between the cumulative-binding curve and the line of equal
    distribution), note that Graczyk computed it from percent-inhibition values,
    and state that I apply the standard discrete Gini formula to the same
    baseline-subtracted log-affinities used for the entropy, with an
    interpretation of the G->1 and G->0 limits.

  - Ratio: I now present it as the prototypical member of the ratio family
    (which includes Klaeger's CATDS), written as a fold-selectivity in log
    units, and explain why I use this simpler form (the reference off-target
    index k becomes an explicit parameter whose effect can be studied directly).

In addition, a new subsection "Faithfulness to the original definitions" states
explicitly which metrics are computed exactly as in their sources (S-score,
ratio) and which were re-operationalized onto the common log-affinity scale
(entropy, Gini). To show that this choice does not silently drive the
conclusions, I recomputed the entropy and Gini in forms close to the originals
and compared rankings. The two operationalizations differ substantially
(Figure 1), which I report transparently as further evidence that the
operationalization of a named metric is itself a source of instability rather
than a neutral implementation detail.

ADDITIONAL CORRECTIONS (not requested by the reviewer)
====================================================================

While revising, I addressed three further items:

1. Candidate-measure property arguments. The motivations for D3 and D4 of the
   candidate include a brief analytic argument alongside the empirical check.
   D4 follows because the gated weight is zero at and below the assay floor and
   monotone above it, so adding a sub-threshold target cannot raise the score.
   D3 holds because the active/inactive boundary is the fixed assay detection
   floor rather than a tunable baseline, so the ranking does not depend on an
   arbitrary baseline choice. (The D3 argument was corrected after resubmission.
   See the correction note below.)

2. Sensitivity to the smoothing width T. I now note explicitly that as T -> 0
   the softplus hinge converges to the hard cutoff max(x_i - x_0, 0), recovering
   the standard entropy and reintroducing its D3 baseline sensitivity. A
   strictly positive T is therefore what buys distributional consistency
   (candidate-measure section).

3. Cross-references. Two in-text cross-references to unnumbered sections
   rendered as blank section numbers in the previous PDF. These have been
   rewritten as named references, consistent with the rest of the manuscript
   and with the journal's convention for sections that are not numbered.

CORRECTION MADE AFTER RESUBMISSION
================================================================

After resubmitting, I re-audited the candidate measure and found two related
errors.

First, the measure was inverted. As originally written, it weighted every kinase
with a softplus of its affinity above the assay floor. The softplus does not
vanish at the floor, so each of the several hundred non-binding kinases kept a
small weight. Their combined weight dominated the normalized profile, which scored
broadly promiscuous compounds as selective and single-target compounds as
non-selective. I corrected this by gating the weight at the fixed assay detection
floor, so non-binders now carry zero weight. The corrected measure is correctly
oriented.

Second, the earlier D3 justification was mistaken. It credited the smooth hinge
with baseline robustness. In fact the smoothing is robust only to a secondary
emphasis parameter (worst-case pairwise Spearman rho = 0.998). D3 holds because
the active/inactive boundary is pinned to the fixed assay detection floor, a
physical constant rather than a tunable choice, not because of smoothness. If the
boundary itself is varied, the ranking moves about as much as it does for the
existing entropy (about -0.93 versus -0.95), which is inherent to thresholded
assays. I have reframed the D3 argument accordingly.

The corrected measure still satisfies all four properties. It converges to its
full-panel ranking at 110 kinases rather than 170, so it is recoverable from an
even smaller panel. The corrected numbers and framing appear in the Candidate
Measure section, Figure 6, the abstract, the introduction, and the conclusion. The
two-family result, the instability taxonomy, and the four required properties are
unaffected.


I believe the manuscript is significantly improved and hope it is now suitable
for publication in Molecular Informatics. Thank you again for the constructive
review.
