Figure 1. Operationalization sensitivity of the entropy and Gini coefficient.
For Davis and Klaeger, the compound selectivity ranking computed in a form close
to the original publication (association-constant weighting) is plotted against
the baseline-subtracted log-affinity form used in the main text. Top row:
selectivity entropy; bottom row: Gini coefficient. The two operationalizations
of the same named metric differ substantially, and for entropy the relationship
between them even changes sign on Klaeger (r = 0.42 Davis, -0.34 Klaeger),
confirming that the operationalization of a named metric is a source of the
definitional instability.

Figure 2. Spearman correlations between the median rankings of the four
selectivity definitions, computed independently on each of the four datasets
(compound x kinase panel sizes shown). In every dataset the S-score (S), entropy
(Ent), and Gini cluster together while the ratio is the consistent outlier (the
ratio row and column are the least green throughout), confirming the two-family
structure across competition-binding, chemoproteomic, and functional-activity
assays.

Figure 3. Best predictor of rank instability within each definition family
(Klaeger dataset, active compounds, n = 206). Ratio instability is best
predicted by the top1-top2 affinity gap; entropy, Gini, and S-score instability
are best predicted by features of the active affinity distribution.

Figure 4. Binding profiles of the most stable, most unstable, and most
ratio-sensitive Davis compounds. Davis has no zero-active (Type 1) compounds;
the right panel illustrates Type 2. Left: Drug 64, most stable (sigma = 1.8),
two active kinases. Center: Drug 42, most unstable (sigma = 19.3), 22 active
kinases with no dominant target. Right: Drug 23, most ratio-sensitive, five
active kinases including two near-tied top targets (delta pKd = 0.023). Dashed
line: pKd = 6 activity threshold; dotted line: pKd = 5 assay floor.

Figure 5. Mean Spearman correlation between rankings computed on random kinase
subpanels and rankings on the full panel, as a function of subpanel size, on
all four datasets. Shaded bands indicate +/-1 standard deviation across 50
subsamples; the dashed line marks r = 0.90, and p* (legend) is the smallest
panel reaching mean r > 0.90.

Figure 6. The two failure modes that motivate D1 and D3 (Klaeger). Left (D1):
rank instability (std across the existing definitions' parameter sweeps) for
compounds with no binding above the activity threshold versus compounds with at
least one active kinase. The contrast comes mostly from the Gini and depends on
the convention used for undefined scores (Methods). When instability is computed
only over the configurations in which each compound's score is defined,
zero-active compounds are more stable than active ones (text). Right (D3): per-compound rank instability
against the number of active kinases, with each parameter swept over
B = [5.0, 6.0]: the entropy's activity baseline (orange); the candidate's gate
x0, with its emphasis baseline pinned to x0 at each point (green); and the
candidate's emphasis baseline with x0 fixed at the assay floor (red). The
candidate's gate and the entropy's baseline produce nearly the same mean
instability (7.2 and 7.5). Only the emphasis sweep, with the gate held fixed,
is stable (0.4).

Figure 7. Panel-size convergence of the candidate measure compared with the four
existing definitions, on all four datasets (50 random subpanels per size).
Curves show the mean Spearman correlation between the ranking computed on a
random subpanel and the full-panel ranking; p* (legend) is the smallest panel
reaching mean rho > 0.90.
