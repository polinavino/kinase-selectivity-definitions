Figure 1. Operationalization sensitivity of the entropy and Gini coefficient.
For each dataset, the compound selectivity ranking computed in a form close to
the original publication (association-constant weighting) is plotted against the
baseline-subtracted log-affinity form used in the main text. Top row: selectivity
entropy; bottom row: Gini coefficient. The two operationalizations of the same
named metric differ substantially, and the entropy ranking even reverses on
Klaeger (entropy r = 0.42 Davis, -0.32 Klaeger; Gini r = 0.11, -0.46), confirming
that the operationalization of a named metric is itself a source of the
definitional instability we report.

Figure 2. Spearman correlations between the median rankings of the four
selectivity definitions, computed independently on each of the four datasets
(compound x kinase panel sizes shown). In every dataset the S-score (S),
entropy (Ent), and Gini cluster together while the ratio is the consistent
outlier (the ratio row and column are the least green throughout), confirming
the two-family structure across competition-binding, chemoproteomic, and
functional-activity assays.

Figure 3. Best predictor of rank instability within each definition family
(Klaeger dataset, active compounds, n = 206). Ratio instability is best
predicted by the top1-top2 affinity gap; entropy, Gini, and S-score instability
are best predicted by features of the active affinity distribution.

Figure 4. Representative binding profiles illustrating the three instability
types. Left: Drug 64 (Davis), most stable (sigma = 1.6), two active kinases.
Center: Drug 42 (Davis), most unstable (sigma = 19.3), 22 active kinases with
no dominant target. Right: Drug 23 (Davis), most ratio-sensitive, five
active kinases including two near-tied top targets (delta pKd = 0.023). Dashed
line: pKd = 6 activity threshold; dotted line: pKd = 5 assay floor.

Figure 5. Mean Spearman correlation between rankings computed on random kinase
subpanels and rankings on the full panel, as a function of subpanel size, for
the Klaeger (343 kinases) and Metz (172 kinases) datasets. Shaded bands
indicate +/-1 standard deviation across 50 subsamples. The dashed line marks
r = 0.90. On Klaeger, entropy and S-score rankings stabilize at approximately
110 kinases, Gini requires approximately 290 kinases, and ratio does not
reach r = 0.90 within the practical panel range. On Metz, entropy, Gini, and
S-score all stabilize by 50-80 kinases, while ratio requires approximately 170
kinases, close to the full panel.

Figure 6. Panel-size convergence of the candidate measure compared with the four
existing definitions, on the Klaeger and Metz datasets (50 random subpanels per
size). Curves show the mean Spearman correlation between the ranking computed
on a random subpanel and the full-panel ranking; p* (legend) is the smallest
panel reaching mean rho > 0.90. On Klaeger the candidate converges in the fast
regime (p* = 110), matching entropy and the S-score (both p* = 110), and well
ahead of Gini (p* = 290) and ratio (p* = 343). On Metz the candidate converges
at p* = 80, matching entropy and Gini and close behind the S-score (p* = 50),
well ahead of ratio (p* = 170).

Figure 7. The two failure modes the candidate measure repairs (Klaeger
dataset). Left (D1): rank standard deviation, pooled across the four existing
definitions' parameter sweeps, for compounds with no detected binding above
the assay floor (sigma = 72.9, n = 16) versus compounds with at least one
active kinase (sigma = 31.3, n = 206); the candidate assigns the first group
no score at all (D1 gate) rather than an unstable one. Right (D3):
per-compound rank instability under the free activity baseline, plotted
against the number of active kinases, for the entropy (r = -0.41) and for the
candidate (r = +0.20); the entropy's instability grows sharply as the active
set shrinks, while the candidate's stays near zero (17x smaller mean
instability) because it has no free activity baseline.
