\section*{Table S4: Candidate measure cross-dataset validation}

Table~S4 reports three checks for each dataset. Orientation is the correlation
with the number of active kinases, where more negative means more correctly
oriented toward ``fewer active targets = more selective''. D3 is reported as in
Methods, as the percentage of pairs of scored compounds whose order reverses
somewhere in $B = [x_0, \tau^*]$. It is given for the emphasis parameter $\beta$
with the gate $x_0$ fixed (the featured measure), for the gate $x_0$ itself swept
as a free parameter (the like-for-like comparison with the entropy's baseline,
Figure~6 of the main text), and for the entropy's own baseline. D4 is the
percentage of compounds for which the score does not rise when one kinase is
added at the activity threshold $\tau^*$, the strongest binding D4 allows
and so the addition most likely to raise the score. $B$ is
$[5.0, 6.0]$ on Davis and Klaeger, $[4.0, 6.0]$ on Metz, and $[20, 50]\%$
inhibition on Anastassiadis, and the smoothing width is $T = \tau^* - x_0$ on
every dataset (Methods). D3 passes at $\varepsilon = 1\%$.
(\texttt{candidate\_validation.py}, \texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py})

The ``gate swept'' and ``entropy'' columns confirm, on every dataset, that the
candidate's D3 satisfaction, like the entropy's, rests on fixing its activity
boundary at $x_0$.
Swept over the same interval, the candidate's gate reverses about as many pairs
as the entropy's baseline does, and the emphasis parameter, with the gate fixed,
reverses fewer than one pair in a hundred.
Removing the gate inverts the measure's orientation entirely (gated $r =
-0.951$ vs.\ ungated $r = +0.93$ against $n_\mathrm{active}$ on Klaeger),
which is why the gate is required for correct orientation.

\section*{S5: Variants of the candidate measure}

The featured candidate is one member of a family of measures. Three axes of
variation are reported here: the floor, the R\'enyi order, and the smoothing
width $T$. Each variant is checked against D1--D4 as in the main text. D4 is
proved only for the featured measure (Shannon order, $\beta = x_0$,
$T \geq 0.58\,(\tau^* - x_0)$), and is otherwise checked by adding one kinase at
$\tau^*$ to every compound. (\texttt{family\_construction.py},
\texttt{family\_panelsize.py}, \texttt{candidate\_validation.py})

\paragraph{Quantile floor.} Replacing the fixed floor $x_0$ with a per-compound
low quantile of the profile (the 5th to 15th percentile) gives a variant that
is invariant to shifts of the readout, but on these data it is not a separate
test: on Klaeger every such percentile of every compound's profile equals the
floor, and on Davis it does for all but at most two compounds, so the variant ranks
compounds exactly as the featured measure does (Spearman $1.000$).

\paragraph{R\'enyi order.} Generalizing the Shannon functional to R\'enyi order
$\alpha > 0$,
\[
  -H_\alpha(p) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1}\log_2 \sum_i p_i^\alpha,
\]
gives a family that recovers the featured measure at $\alpha = 1$. D1 holds for every
order, since the gate is applied before the entropy is computed, and D2 holds
vacuously. D4 holds on every compound with one kinase added at $\tau^*$ for
each order tested. D3 separates the orders
(Table~S5): the orders $0.5$ and $1$ pass on both datasets, $\alpha = 2$ is borderline
(it passes on Davis and narrowly fails on Klaeger), and $\alpha = 3$ fails on
both.

The order does not affect panel recoverability. Every order tested, from
$0.01$ to $3$, reaches $p^* = 80$ on both Klaeger and Metz, and the mean
convergence curves differ by at most $0.003$ between orders at every panel size.
We feature $\alpha = 1$ because it is the Shannon entropy, for which
D4 is proved.

\paragraph{Smoothing width.} The featured measure uses $T = \tau^* - x_0$. On
Klaeger, over $T = 0.5$, $0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, and $2$ (in units of
$\tau^* - x_0 = 1$), orientation is unchanged ($r \approx -0.95$) and the
rankings agree closely across all five values (worst-case pairwise Spearman
$0.999$). D3 passes for $T \geq 0.75$ (pairs reversing as the emphasis baseline
is swept: $0.93\%$, $0.54\%$, $0.17\%$, $0.04\%$ for $T = 0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, $2$)
and narrowly fails at $T = 0.5$ ($1.94\%$), which also falls below the D4 bound
$T \geq 0.58$. As $T \to 0$ the softplus converges to the hard cutoff,
recovering the entropy together with its D3 and D4 failures.

\section*{S6: Additional analyses}

The analyses below are summarized in one sentence in the main text, and are
given here in the order the main text cites them.

\paragraph{Klaeger restricted to protein kinases.} The 343 Klaeger targets are
the proteins the Kinobeads pulldown quantified, and 120 of them are not protein
kinases: 46 cyclins and other subunits or adaptors of kinase complexes (for
example CCNA2--CCNT2, CKS1B, the AMPK and PKA regulatory subunits, and the AP2
adaptor subunits), 10 small-molecule and lipid kinases, 20 other enzymes (for
example COMT, NQO2, TOP2B, PYGB, PYGL and PTPN1), and 44 other proteins,
including ribosomal proteins and SLC transporters. Kinobeads co-capture complex
partners with their kinases, so a CDK hit can also count its cyclin. These
columns hold 519 of the 2{,}763 active entries ($18.8\%$), in 117 of the 222
compounds, and five compounds (AZD-2014, AZD-8186, SGX-523, TG-100115,
volitinib) are active only on them. Table~S6 recomputes the main Klaeger
results on the 223 protein kinases. Among compounds with an active kinase, the
two-family structure, the D3 verdicts and all but one of the instability
relations are essentially unchanged, and the candidate and the entropy still rank compounds
almost identically and reach the same $p^*$. Three results move. With all
compounds included, the five new zero-active compounds pull the Gini toward the
ratio ($0.655$ against $0.515$ with the S-score), so, as in the main text, its
placement on Klaeger depends on the zero-active compounds. The skewness--Gini
association weakens from $0.35$ to $0.24$ (still $p < 0.001$). The S-score
and Gini reach $p^*$ at fewer kinases (80 and 200), partly because the full panel
is smaller, and the ratio still needs the full panel.

\paragraph{Klaeger confidence filter.} The high-confidence filter removes 793
of the 5{,}916 drug--target rows in the table ($13.4\%$), one of which has no
numeric $K_d^\mathrm{app}$. A removed pair is treated identically to one with
no detected binding and assigned the same $\mathrm{p}K_d = 5.0$ background
value. Of the 792 low-confidence pairs with a numeric $K_d^\mathrm{app}$, 561
fall within the $222 \times 343$ high-confidence matrix. The other 231 involve
7 compounds and 177 kinases with no high-confidence measurement. Adding the 561
pairs to the same matrix changes only $0.74\%$ of its entries, shifts every cell
of the Klaeger triangle of main-text Table~1 by at most $0.05$, and leaves
every Klaeger panel-size $p^*$ value unchanged. Retaining all 792 pairs also
adds those compounds and kinases, which changes the panel itself. It shifts the
Klaeger cells of Table~1 by up to $0.12$ (S-score against ratio, from $0.22$ to
$0.34$) and leaves the two-family structure intact.
(\texttt{klaeger\_filter\_sensitivity.py})

\paragraph{Skewness and Gini instability.} Among the 161 Klaeger compounds with
at least three active kinases, skewness correlates with the share of a
compound's weight carried by kinases that the $\beta$ sweep crosses
($r = 0.45$), and that share predicts Gini instability with the number of
active kinases held fixed (partial $r = 0.71$). Holding it fixed as well lowers
the skewness association from $0.57$ to $0.31$, so this mechanism accounts for
part of the association. (\texttt{claim\_checks.py}, section 16)

\paragraph{Type~3 compounds.} Five of the ten largest entropy--ratio
disagreements among active Klaeger compounds have a top$_1$--top$_2$ gap above
$1$, for example dovitinib ($n_\mathrm{active} = 26$, gap $= 1.935$) and
BMS-690514 ($n_\mathrm{active} = 20$, gap $= 1.985$). The ratio ranks these
five 22nd to 60th of 222, and the entropy and Gini rank them 157th to 191st.
(\texttt{claim\_checks.py}, section 5;
\texttt{klaeger\_\allowbreak{}selectivity\_\allowbreak{}results.csv})

\paragraph{Most stable compounds.} On Klaeger the most stable compounds are
among the most promiscuous. XL-228 ($\sigma = 7.0$) and PF-03814735
($\sigma = 7.8$), with 55--57 active kinases, have median ranks of 208--220 of
222 under every definition, and mean instability falls from about 31--33 for
compounds with 1 to 40 active kinases to 16 above 40. On Davis, compounds with
few active kinases are the most stable on average (mean $\sigma = 3.1$ for one
or two, $4.4$ for three to five). The most stable, neflamapimod (main-text
Figure~4, left, $\sigma = 1.8$), has two, with median ranks of 3--5.5 under the
four definitions. The 57 Davis compounds with more than 20 active kinases are
less stable (mean $\sigma = 9.1$, against $5.3$ for the other 11). The S-score,
entropy, and Gini give the five broadest median ranks of 63--68 of 68, and the
ratio gives them 29--52. Without the ratio's configurations, the mean
instability of the 57 broad compounds falls from $9.1$ to $5.1$.
(\texttt{claim\_checks.py}, sections 7 and 18)
