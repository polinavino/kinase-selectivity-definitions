This document gives the full evidence for results summarized in the main text,
in the order of its argument: dataset construction (Section~S1), score
implementations and agreement (S2), ranking sensitivity and profile
associations (S3), the criteria D3 and D4, the gated entropy and its
uniqueness, with the proofs of Propositions 1--3 (S4),
recovery from subpanels (S5), cross-dataset and supplementary comparisons
(S6), the clinical analysis (S7), and the script behind every result (S8).
All analyses use the curated panels of the main text, one reference target per
human protein kinase, except where Section~S1 compares them with the released
panels. The code repository
(\url{https://github.com/polinavino/kinase-selectivity-definitions})
reproduces every number, and the script behind each table and paragraph is
named next to it.

\section*{S1: Dataset construction and target selection}

\paragraph{Released and curated panels.} Table~S1 compares the main Davis and
Klaeger results on the released and curated panels. The released Davis panel
lists 433 constructs, 12 of them for EGFR. For 26 of the 68 compounds the two
best-bound constructs are forms of one gene. The most ratio-unstable compound
on the released panel, GDC-0879, owes its $0.023$ top$_1$--top$_2$ gap to
BRAF(V600E) and BRAF, and its gap to the next gene (RAF1) is $1.54$. On Davis,
the best affinity per gene is also given as an alternative to the reference
construct.

Klaeger et al.\ annotate 225 of the 343 released Klaeger targets as protein or
lipid kinases. The curated panel keeps 217 of them and leaves out three
lipid-kinase columns (PIP4K2A, PIP4K2C and PIP4K2A;\allowbreak PIP4K2B), four
protein groups (CSNK2A1;\allowbreak CSNK2A3, PDPK1;\allowbreak PDPK2P,
PRKCE;\allowbreak PRKCH and PRKX;\allowbreak PRKY), and Q6ZSR9, which UniProt
describes as an uncharacterized protein with no kinase domain, encoded in the
3'-UTR of AAK1. The other 118 targets are not annotated as kinases: 46 subunits
or adaptors of kinase complexes (for example the cyclins CCNA2--CCNT2, CKS1B,
the AMPK and PKA regulatory subunits, and the AP2 adaptor subunits), 7
small-molecule kinases, 20 other enzymes (for example COMT, NQO2, TOP2B, PYGB,
PYGL and PTPN1), 43 other proteins, including ribosomal proteins and SLC
transporters, and BCR and TRRAP. The source flags 88 of the 118 as possibly
co-enriched as complex members, among them BCR (with ABL1) and TRRAP (with
CDK8). The 122 targets outside the curated panel, other than the four protein
groups, hold 556 of the 2{,}763 active entries ($20.1\%$), in 122 of the 222
compounds. Six compounds are active only on them (AZD-2014, AZD-8186,
NMS-1286937, SGX-523, TG-100115 and volitinib). The code repository lists every
target of each dataset with the reason it is kept or removed. Most results
change little between the panels. The largest changes are on Davis, where the
ratio agrees more with the other definitions on the curated panel, and on
Klaeger, where with all compounds included the Gini correlates more with the
ratio than with the S-score on the curated panel. The $p^*$ rows of Table~S1
are from the analysis without the D1 gate (Section~S5), not from the main-text
analysis.

\paragraph{Klaeger confidence filter.} The high-confidence filter removes 793 of
the 5{,}916 drug--target rows ($13.4\%$), one of which has no numeric
$K_d^\mathrm{app}$. A removed pair is coded at the floor by convention; a
low-confidence fit does not establish weak binding. Of the 792 low-confidence
pairs with a numeric $K_d^\mathrm{app}$, 405 involve curated targets (375 with
a fitted $\mathrm{p}K_d$ above 5, and 208 above 6), and 402 of these fall within
the $222 \times 217$ matrix. Adding them changes $0.77\%$ of its entries (some
added values lie below the floor and are set to it). It shifts the all-compound
Klaeger correlations of main-text Table~3 by at most $0.08$, and those among the
200 compounds with an active kinase under both filters by at most $0.006$. It
leaves every $p^*$ of the main-text recovery analysis unchanged and raises coverage
at 50 targets from $70.6\%$ to $72.9\%$. Retaining all low-confidence pairs also
adds 7 compounds and shifts the all-compound correlations by at most $0.06$.
(\texttt{klaeger\_filter\_sensitivity.py})

\paragraph{Metz coverage.} The 704 retained Metz compounds are measured against
50 to 143 of the 172 kinases ($29.1\%$ to $83.1\%$; mean $40.9\%$, median
$37.2\%$, 75th percentile $47.1\%$). For the median compound, most entries are
therefore floor-imputed. Twenty-three compounds have exactly 50 measured
kinases and 170 have 55 or fewer. Only 7 kinases are measured for at least
$80\%$ of the compounds, so no fully measured subset of useful size exists.
(\texttt{metz\_coverage.py}, \texttt{claim\_checks.py} section 10)

\paragraph{Activity threshold on Davis.} Active entries ($\mathrm{p}K_d > 6$)
make up $16.7\%$ of the Davis matrix and $4.5\%$ of the Klaeger matrix; the 46
compounds profiled in both are active against $15.0\%$ of the Davis panel and
$3.9\%$ of the Klaeger panel on average. With the S-score's threshold sweep
shifted up by one log unit on Davis (from $[5.5, 8.0]$ to $[6.5, 9.0]$), the
S-score still correlates more with the entropy and Gini ($0.88$) than with the
ratio ($0.54$). (\texttt{dataset\_descriptive\_stats.py}, \texttt{claim\_checks.py}
section 9)

\paragraph{Datasets not used.} The screen of Gao et al.\ (2013) has the same
readout type as Anastassiadis, so it would add no new assay technology, and its
raw data are not openly redistributable. The study of Patricelli et al.\ (2011)
profiles too few inhibitors for a ranking analysis.

In every case the published entropy correlates more with the ratio than with
the S-score or the Gini, so it would not be grouped with them on any of the
three affinity datasets. How far it is from the S-score and Gini depends on the
treatment of floor-coded pairs. On Klaeger, a third treatment keeps every
measured high-confidence affinity at its measured value, including the 194
weaker than the floor (4{,}000 measured cells on the curated panel, in 84
compounds for the weak ones), and omits unmeasured pairs. The published entropy
then correlates with the S-score, Gini and ratio at $0.558$, $0.531$ and $0.663$
over all compounds and $0.558$, $0.778$ and $0.918$ over the 200 with an
active kinase, against $0.571$, $0.530$, $0.657$ and $0.564$, $0.782$, $0.916$
with floor-coded pairs given zero weight. The two rankings correlate at $0.9987$
over all compounds and $0.9996$ over active ones. On the subpanels of the
recovery analysis, $p^*$ stays at 200 targets, and the mean Spearman
correlation at 50 targets is $0.6025$ against $0.6014$.

\section*{S3: Ranking sensitivity and profile associations}

\paragraph{Compounds with no active kinase.} Compounds with no active kinase
have higher aggregated rank instability than active compounds on every dataset where they occur ($77.9$
against $32.2$ on Klaeger, $36.6$ against $21.8$ on Anastassiadis, and $178.5$
against $108.1$ on Metz, which has five such compounds). The contrast comes
mainly from the definitions disagreeing. Within a single definition's sweep on
Klaeger, it is significant only for the Gini ($65.0$ against $16.3$,
$p < 0.001$). It is small and not significant for the entropy ($11.9$ against
$12.6$, $p = 0.91$) and the ratio ($17.5$ against $19.1$, $p = 0.51$), and
reverses for the S-score ($19.0$ against $25.7$, $p = 0.01$), whose score is $0$
for every such compound at thresholds of 6 or above. On Anastassiadis it is
significant for the Gini, the entropy and the ratio. On Metz it reverses for
the S-score, the entropy and the Gini ($7.8$ against $71.2$, $11.0$ against
$24.5$, and $5.4$ against $24.2$; all $p < 0.05$). On Klaeger, the aggregated
contrast persists with the undefined entropy and Gini configurations dropped
($66.2$ against $31.7$) and with only the S-score and ratio aggregated ($70.6$
against $37.7$, both $p < 0.001$). Computed only over each compound's defined
configurations, the 16 Klaeger compounds with no active kinase that have at
least two defined configurations have lower mean instability than active ones
within the entropy ($7.0$ against $13.5$) and the Gini ($6.7$ against $13.6$,
both $p = 0.002$). This is a group result: three of the 16 (brivanib, brivanib
alaninate and NMS-1286937) are less stable than the active mean, and the
compounds contribute different numbers of configurations. (\texttt{claim\_checks.py} sections 13 and 20,
\texttt{undefined\_\allowbreak{}score\_\allowbreak{}convention.py})

\paragraph{Six Klaeger compounds with no detected binding.} AZD-2014, AZD-8055,
AZD-8186, BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib show no binding to any curated
target, although they are established inhibitors of mTOR, mTOR, PI3K$\beta$,
JAK2, MET and MET. mTOR and PI3K$\beta$ were not quantified for any of the 229
compounds with a fitted target in the Kinobeads table, consistent with low
expression or poor capture in the lysate; PI3K$\beta$, a lipid kinase, is also
outside the curated panel. JAK2 and MET were quantified for 7 and 32 other
compounds (up to $\mathrm{p}K_d = 8.3$ and $9.5$), so absence from the lysate
does not explain the result for BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib. These data
cannot tell whether compound-specific capture, competition or curve fitting
does. (\texttt{claim\_checks.py} section 1)

\paragraph{Boundary sensitivity.} Table~S3 gives the share of pairs of scored
compounds whose order reverses as each parameter moves across
$B = [x_0, \tau^*]$. $B$ is $[5.0, 6.0]$ on Davis and Klaeger, $[4.0, 6.0]$ on
Metz and $[20, 50]\%$ inhibition on Anastassiadis. For the ratio, the parameter
is the level $b$ in $R_b(k) = x_{(1)} - \max(x_{(k+1)}, b)$. The gated
entropy's gate is swept with its emphasis baseline moved with it and $T$ held
at its value for the assay floor. A score satisfies D2 only if it stays within
$\varepsilon = 1\%$ on every dataset and, for the ratio, for every $k$. Every
swept parameter except the gated entropy's emphasis baseline exceeds $13\%$ on
at least one dataset (the ratio's level only at $k = 5$). Figure~S2 shows the per-compound instability behind the
Klaeger values. (\texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py},

\paragraph{Profile features and instability.} Table~S4 gives the correlations
between profile features and within-definition instability, with the compounds
that have an active kinase ranked among themselves, and, for Klaeger, ranked
among all 222 compounds. The ranking population changes the Gini column most:
ranked among all compounds, the skewness of the active affinities is the Gini's
strongest correlate ($+0.262$), and ranked among active compounds it is not
significant ($+0.072$), while the number of active kinases becomes significant
($-0.254$). Restricted to the 189 active compounds whose entropy and Gini are
defined throughout the baseline sweep, and re-ranked, the number of active
kinases correlates with entropy and Gini instability at $-0.274$ and $-0.272$
(both $p < 0.001$). Figure~S3 plots each definition's strongest correlate. With a
Bonferroni correction for the 24 tests per dataset, 8 of the 13 nominally
significant Klaeger correlations survive, and on Davis only the ratio--gap
correlation. The Holm procedure gives the same result; the Benjamini--Hochberg
procedure keeps 13 Klaeger correlations and, on Davis, again only the
ratio--gap correlation. On Anastassiadis and Metz, with active compounds ranked among
themselves, the number of active kinases correlates with entropy instability at
$-0.38$ and $-0.42$ (both $p < 0.001$), and the top$_1$--top$_2$ gap with ratio
instability at $0.00$ ($p = 0.97$) and $-0.26$ ($p < 0.001$). Without the eight
Anastassiadis compounds whose two best targets are both at $99.5\%$ inhibition
or more, the gap--ratio correlation there is $-0.15$ ($p = 0.10$).
(\texttt{instability\_by\_family.py}, \texttt{additional\_\allowbreak{}datasets\_\allowbreak{}analysis.py},

\paragraph{Undefined features.} The skewness of the active affinities is
undefined with fewer than three active kinases, and their standard deviation
and range with fewer than two; Table~S4 sets these to $0$. Over only the
compounds for which each feature is defined, the Klaeger range associations
strengthen (S-score, entropy and Gini $-0.373$, $-0.378$ and $-0.349$, all
$p < 0.001$), the Gini--skewness association stays non-significant ($+0.145$,
$p = 0.08$), and no Davis correlation changes significance.
(\texttt{feature\_\allowbreak{}placeholder\_\allowbreak{}sensitivity.py})

\paragraph{Range and skewness.} Range rises with the number of active kinases
(Spearman $0.76$ on Klaeger). Holding that number fixed, range remains
associated with S-score instability (partial $r = -0.22$, $p = 0.002$) but not
with entropy or Gini instability ($+0.08$ and $+0.01$). On the 46 compounds
profiled in both Davis and Klaeger, the range--S-score association is weak on
both (Klaeger $r = -0.10$, $p = 0.56$, for the 37 with at least two active
kinases; Davis $-0.16$, $p = 0.30$); the same compounds have a median of 4
active kinases on Klaeger and 32 on Davis. Among the 148 Klaeger compounds
with at least three active kinases, skewness correlates with the share of a
compound's weight carried by kinases that the $\beta$ sweep crosses ($0.38$),
and that share with Gini instability with the number of active kinases held
fixed (partial $r = 0.43$). Ranked among active compounds, skewness has no
marginal association with Gini instability, but with the number of active
kinases held fixed it does (partial $r = 0.35$, $p < 0.001$); holding the
weight share fixed as well lowers this to $0.19$ ($p = 0.02$). The share of a compound's above-floor kinases that some baseline

\section*{S4: Monotonicity under weak-target addition, the gated entropy and its uniqueness}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition 1.} Every gated kinase, the added one
included, has weight above $m = T\ln 2$. Since the softplus increases with
affinity, an added kinase with value at most $\tau^*$ has weight
$a \leq T\ln(1 + e^{(\tau^* - x_0)/T})$, and by the assumption on $T$ this is at
most $e\,m$. Let the original profile have $n \geq 1$ gated kinases ($n \geq 1$
because D1 scores it only if some kinase lies above $\tau^* > x_0$), total
weight $W > nm$, and entropy $H$ (in nats), and let $q = a/(W + a)$ be the
added kinase's share of the total weight. After the addition the entropy is
$(1 - q)H + h(q)$, where $h(q) = -q\ln q - (1 - q)\ln(1 - q)$ is the binary
entropy, so the entropy does not decrease if and only if $H \leq h(q)/q$.
Because $h$ is concave with $h(0) = 0$, $h(q)/q$ decreases in $q$. Since
$q < r/(n + r)$ with $r = a/m \leq e$, this gives
$h(q)/q > \ln\frac{n + r}{r} + \frac{n}{r}\ln\frac{n + r}{n}$. As $H \leq \ln n$,
it suffices that $\ln n$ is at most this bound, which rearranges to
$\ln r \leq (1 + \frac{n}{r})\ln(1 + \frac{r}{n})$. The right-hand side is at
least $1$ for all $n, r > 0$, because $\ln(1 + t) \geq t/(1 + t)$, and
$\ln r \leq 1$. The entropy therefore does not decrease, so the score does not
increase. $\square$ With $T = \tau^* - x_0$ the same argument covers every
$\beta \in B$: the lightest gated kinase then has weight above
$m = T\ln(1 + e^{(x_0 - \beta)/T})$, and $a/m < 2.213 < e$. The argument uses
only that every gated kinase has weight above some $m > 0$ and that an added
kinase with value at most $\tau^*$ has weight at most $e\,m$, so it applies to
any weight function that increases with affinity and has these two
properties.

\paragraph{Sharpness.} The bound cannot be lowered. With $\beta = x_0$ and
$T < (\tau^* - x_0)/\ln(2^e - 1)$, a kinase added at $\tau^*$ has weight
$r\,m$ with $r = \ln(1 + e^{(\tau^* - x_0)/T})/\ln 2 > e$, in units of the
limiting weight $m = T\ln 2$ just above the floor. Take one active target with
weight $s > r$ and $n$ kinases just above the floor, with weights close to 1.
For the limiting weights the entropy is $H_n = \ln(n + s) - s\ln s/(n + s)$,
and after the addition $H'_n = \ln(n + s + r) - (s\ln s + r\ln r)/(n + s + r)$.
Then $n(H'_n - H_n) \to r(1 - \ln r) < 0$ as $n \to \infty$, so for large
enough $n$ the entropy falls and the score rises; weights close to, but above,
1 keep this true with the gate strict. Numerically, profiles of this kind
violate D3 by $3.1 \times 10^{-5}$ bits on Klaeger at $T = 0.564$, and at
$T = 1$ on Metz and $T = 10$ on Anastassiadis by up to $0.0014$ and $0.014$
bits. A random search over 20{,}000 profiles per dataset found no violation with
$T = \tau^* - x_0$, and adding one kinase at $\tau^*$ never raises the score of
a real compound. (\texttt{d4\_\allowbreak{}entropy\_\allowbreak{}vs\_\allowbreak{}candidate.py})

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition 2.} With $x_0$, $\beta$ and $T$ fixed, the gated
weights, and so their shares $\mathbf{p}$, are fixed functions of the profile.
Shannon (1948, Theorem 2) showed that a function $F$ of a probability vector
that is continuous, increases with $n$ when all $n$ entries equal $1/n$, and
has the grouping property is $F(\mathbf{p}) = -K\sum_i p_i \log p_i$ for some
$K > 0$. A score $-F(\mathbf{p})$ is therefore $K$ times the gated entropy and
ranks compounds identically. The R\'enyi entropy of order $\alpha \neq 1$ is
continuous and increases with $n$ on equal shares but lacks the grouping
property. $\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition 3.} Let $W = \sum_i w_i$ and sort the
original positive weights in decreasing order. Because $a \leq \min_i w_i$, the
added weight comes last among the positive weights. For $j \leq r$ the sum of
the $j$ largest new shares, $\sum_{i \leq j} w_{(i)}/(W + a)$, is at most the
corresponding old sum, $\sum_{i \leq j} w_{(i)}/W$, and for $j > r$ both sums
equal 1. The new shares are therefore more even than the old ones in the
sense of Proposition 3 (the old shares majorize the new ones), so a score that
never increases when the shares become more even does not increase. $\square$
Functions with this property are called Schur-convex. The negative Shannon and
R\'enyi entropies have it because the entropies are Schur-concave, the largest
share has it and here falls to $w_{(1)}/(W + a)$, and the Gini coefficient has
it on a fixed panel. For the Shannon
entropy, a direct calculation also gives, in nats,
$H' - H = \ln(1 + a/W) + \frac{a}{W + a}(L - \ln a)$, where
$L = \sum_i (w_i/W)\ln w_i \geq \ln a$ because every $w_i \geq a$; the first
term is positive and the second non-negative. The condition on $a$ is
needed: the weights $10$ and fifty $1$s have $H = 5.353$ bits, and adding
another $10$, tied with the strongest, lowers it to $5.180$ bits. Without the
condition, a concentration score can also rank a profile with more active
targets as more selective, as it should when binding is more concentrated:
$H(1, 1) = 1$ bit, but $H(1000, 1, 1) = 0.023$ bits.

\paragraph{Checks of D4 and Proposition 3.} On reference profiles
$\mathbf{v}_m$ with $a = 6.5$, $7$, $8$ and $9$ on a 30-kinase panel, every
score in Table~4 of the main text, $P_{\max}$ and the published entropy
satisfy D4. The active count with its sign reversed and the gated entropy
without its non-binder gate fail it. A random check of Proposition 3 over
20{,}000 weight vectors found no violation. Adding one kinase at
$\mathrm{p}K_d = 12$ to a profile with one target at $9$ and fifty at $5.1$
raises the gated entropy's score from $-5.555$ to $-5.346$, the entropy's from
$-4.127$ to $-3.310$ and $P_{\max}$ from $0.994$ to $0.999$, and lowers the
active count's from $-1$ to $-2$: adding an active target is not constrained by
D4. Such an addition need not raise a concentration score either: adding a
weight of $2$ to a profile whose only positive weight is $1$ lowers $-H$ from
$0$ to $-0.918$ bits. D4 also depends on the floor treatment: if floor-coded
pairs keep weight $1$ and a target has weight $2$, the change from
$(2, 1, 1, 1)$ to $(2, 2, 1, 1)$ raises $-H$ from $-1.922$ to $-1.918$ bits, so
a broader profile is not then a more even one. The published entropy with
floor-coded pairs kept at the floor fails D4 in this way: with targets at

(\texttt{d4\_\allowbreak{}entropy\_\allowbreak{}vs\_\allowbreak{}candidate.py} sections

\paragraph{Variants.} D3 is proved only for the Shannon order, either with

$\tau^*$ to every scored compound, and it held for all of them.
\emph{Quantile floor.} Replacing the fixed floor with a per-compound low
quantile of the profile (the 5th to 15th percentile) gives a variant invariant
to shifts of the readout, but on these data it is not a separate test. On Davis
every such percentile equals the floor for all but at most two compounds, and on
Klaeger for every compound. At the 10th percentile the variant ranks compounds
as the gated entropy does (Spearman $1.000$), and across the five
percentiles $0.04\%$ (Davis) and $0\%$ (Klaeger) of pairs reverse.

$0.01$ to $3$ reaches $p^* = 80$ on Klaeger and Metz, and the mean Spearman
curves differ by at most $0.002$ between orders.
\emph{Smoothing width.} On Klaeger, over $T = 0.5$, $0.75$, $1$, $1.5$ and $2$,
the correlation with the number of active kinases is unchanged

$T \to 0$ the softplus approaches the hard cutoff, giving back the entropy.

\section*{S5: Recovery from subpanels}

\paragraph{Without the D1 gate.} Karaman et al.\ scored each compound's S-score
on random subpanels and found that it varies more as panels shrink. Repeating
that design for the four definitions, scoring every compound on every subpanel,
mixes the ranking with each definition's convention for compounds that have no
active target on the subpanel. For each panel size, in steps of 30 from 50
targets up to the full panel, we drew 50 random subsets, computed each
definition on the subpanel with the dataset's floor $x_0$ as its baseline
(entropy, Gini) or level (ratio, $k = 1$) and the activity threshold as the
S-score cutoff, and correlated its ranking with the full-panel ranking. The
S-score recovers the full-panel ranking fastest on every dataset, and the
entropy as fast on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis (Figure~S4; $p^*$ of 50
targets on Davis and Anastassiadis and 80 on Klaeger; on Metz 50 for the
S-score and 80 for the entropy). The ratio is the slowest, needing 350 of 363
targets on Davis, 260 of 291 on Anastassiadis, 170 of 172 on Metz, and more than
the largest subpanel tested on Klaeger ($r = 0.887$ at 200 targets).
The Gini needs 110 targets on Davis and 200 on Anastassiadis and Klaeger, but
matches the entropy on Metz (80). Its slower recovery comes from compounds whose
Gini is undefined on the subpanel, which the convention ranks least selective.
Excluding them gives the Gini $p^*$ values of 50, 80, 50 and 80 targets.
Changing the bar within $[0.80, 0.95]$ changes the $p^*$ values but never
reverses the order of two definitions on any dataset (Table~S7).

\section*{S7: Clinical analysis}

For the FDA-approved Klaeger compounds, we correlated each definition's median
selectivity rank with the proportions of FAERS reports that were serious, fatal
or involved hospitalization ($n = 46$) and with discontinuation rates reported
in FDA labels ($n = 33$). FAERS counts were pulled from the openFDA
adverse-event interface (\texttt{faers\_pull.py}); discontinuation rates were
extracted by hand from label text (\texttt{clinical\_safety\_data.csv}). No
correlation was significant (all $|r| \leq 0.20$, all $p > 0.19$). Raw report
counts correlate weakly with the ratio ranking ($r = 0.30$, $p = 0.04$), which
may reflect prescription volume. Grade 3/4 adverse-event rates were available
for too few drugs. FAERS proportions describe reports, not incidence among
treated patients; label discontinuation rates come from trials with different
populations and doses; and both depend on indication and prescription volume.
The analysis therefore cannot isolate an effect of selectivity on toxicity.
