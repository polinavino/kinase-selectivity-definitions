This document gives the full evidence for results summarized in the main text,
in the order of the main text: dataset construction (Section~S1), score
implementations and agreement (S2), ranking sensitivity and profile
associations (S3), the criteria D2 to D4, the gated entropy and its
characterization, with the proofs of Propositions 1--3 (S4),
recovery from subpanels (S5), cross-dataset and supplementary comparisons
(S6), the clinical analysis (S7), and the script for every result (S8).
All analyses use the curated panels of the main text, one reference target per
human protein kinase, except where Section~S1 compares them with the released
panels. The scripts in the code repository
(\url{https://github.com/polinavino/kinase-selectivity-definitions})
reproduce every number, and the script for each table and paragraph is
named next to it.

\section*{S1: Dataset construction and target selection}

\paragraph{Released and curated panels.} Table~S1 compares the main Davis and
Klaeger results on the released and curated panels. The released Davis panel
lists 433 constructs, 12 of them for EGFR. For 26 of the 68 compounds the two
best-bound constructs are forms of one gene. The most ratio-unstable compound
on the released panel, GDC-0879, has a top$_1$--top$_2$ gap of $0.023$, between
BRAF(V600E) and BRAF, and a gap of $1.54$ to the next gene (RAF1). On Davis,
the best affinity per gene is also given as an alternative to the reference
construct.

Klaeger et al.\cite{Klaeger2017} annotate 225 of the 343 released Klaeger targets as protein or
lipid kinases. The curated panel keeps 217 of them and leaves out three
lipid-kinase columns (PIP4K2A, PIP4K2C and PIP4K2A;\allowbreak PIP4K2B), four
protein groups (CSNK2A1;\allowbreak CSNK2A3, PDPK1;\allowbreak PDPK2P,
PRKCE;\allowbreak PRKCH and PRKX;\allowbreak PRKY), and Q6ZSR9, which UniProt
describes as an uncharacterized protein with no kinase domain, encoded in the
3'-UTR of AAK1. The other 118 targets are not annotated as kinases: 46 subunits
or adaptors of kinase complexes (for example the cyclins CCNA2--CCNT2, CKS1B,
the AMPK and PKA regulatory subunits, and the AP2 adaptor subunits), 7
small-molecule kinases, 20 other enzymes (for example COMT, NQO2, TOP2B, PYGB,
PYGL and PTPN1), 43 other proteins, including ribosomal proteins and SLC
transporters, and BCR and TRRAP. The source flags 88 of the 118 as possibly
co-enriched as complex members, among them BCR (with ABL1) and TRRAP (with
CDK8). The 122 targets outside the curated panel, other than the four protein
groups, hold 556 of the 2{,}763 active entries ($20.1\%$), in 122 of the 222
compounds. Six compounds are active only on them (AZD-2014, AZD-8186,
NMS-1286937, SGX-523, TG-100115 and volitinib). The code repository lists every
target of each dataset with the reason it is kept or removed. Most results
change little between the panels. The largest changes are on Davis, where the
ratio agrees more with the other definitions on the curated panel, and on
Klaeger, where, over all compounds, the Gini correlates more with the
ratio than with the S-score on the curated panel.

\paragraph{Klaeger confidence filter.} The high-confidence filter removes 793 of
the 5{,}916 drug--target rows ($13.4\%$), one of which has no numeric
$K_d^\mathrm{app}$. A removed pair is coded at the floor by convention. A
low-confidence fit does not establish weak binding. Of the 792 low-confidence
pairs with a numeric $K_d^\mathrm{app}$, 405 involve curated targets (375 with
a fitted $\mathrm{p}K_d$ above 5, and 208 above 6), and 402 of these fall within
the $222 \times 217$ matrix. Adding them changes $0.77\%$ of its entries (some
added values lie below the floor and are set to it). It shifts the all-compound
Klaeger correlations of main-text Table~3 by at most $0.08$, and those among the
200 compounds with an active kinase under both filters by about $0.006$.
The relaxed filter makes Brivanib\_alaninate and TG-100115 newly eligible,
increasing the active population to 202. It
leaves every $p^*$ of the main-text recovery analysis unchanged and raises coverage
at 50 targets from $70.6\%$ to $72.9\%$. Retaining all low-confidence pairs also
adds 7 compounds and shifts the all-compound correlations by at most $0.06$.
(\texttt{klaeger\_filter\_sensitivity.py})

\paragraph{Metz coverage.} The 704 retained Metz compounds are measured against
50 to 143 of the 172 kinases ($29.1\%$ to $83.1\%$, with mean $40.9\%$, median
$37.2\%$, 75th percentile $47.1\%$). For the median compound, most entries are
therefore floor-imputed. Twenty-three compounds have exactly 50 measured
kinases and 170 have 55 or fewer. Only 7 kinases are measured for at least
$80\%$ of the compounds, so no fully measured subset of useful size exists.
(\texttt{metz\_coverage.py}, \texttt{claim\_checks.py} section 10)

\paragraph{Activity threshold on Davis.} Active entries ($\mathrm{p}K_d > 6$)
make up $16.7\%$ of the Davis matrix and $4.5\%$ of the Klaeger matrix. The 46
compounds profiled in both are active against $15.0\%$ of the Davis panel and
$3.9\%$ of the Klaeger panel on average. With the S-score's threshold sweep
shifted up by one log unit on Davis (from $[5.5, 8.0]$ to $[6.5, 9.0]$), the
S-score still correlates more with the entropy and Gini ($0.88$) than with the
ratio ($0.54$). (\texttt{dataset\_descriptive\_stats.py}, \texttt{claim\_checks.py}
section 9)

\paragraph{Datasets not used.} The screen of Gao et al.\cite{Gao2013} has the same
readout type as Anastassiadis, so it would add no new assay technology, and its
raw data are not openly redistributable. The study of Patricelli et al.\cite{Patricelli2011}
profiles too few inhibitors for a ranking analysis.

the S-score or the Gini, so it would not be grouped with them on any of the

over all compounds and $0.9996$ over active ones. On the subpanels of the

\section*{S3: Ranking sensitivity and profile associations}

\paragraph{Compounds with no active kinase.} Compounds with no active kinase
have higher aggregated rank instability than active compounds on every dataset where they occur ($77.9$
against $32.2$ on Klaeger, $36.6$ against $21.8$ on Anastassiadis, and $178.5$
against $108.1$ on Metz, which has five such compounds). The contrast comes
mainly from the definitions disagreeing. Within a single definition's sweep on
Klaeger, it is significant only for the Gini ($65.0$ against $16.3$,
$p < 0.001$). It is small and not significant for the entropy ($11.9$ against
$12.6$, $p = 0.91$) and the ratio ($17.5$ against $19.1$, $p = 0.51$), and
reverses for the S-score ($19.0$ against $25.7$, $p = 0.01$), whose score is $0$
for every such compound at thresholds of 6 or above. On Anastassiadis it is
significant for the Gini, the entropy and the ratio. On Metz it reverses for
the S-score, the entropy and the Gini ($7.8$ against $71.2$, $11.0$ against
$24.5$, and $5.4$ against $24.2$, all with $p < 0.05$). On Klaeger, the aggregated
contrast persists with the undefined entropy and Gini configurations dropped
($66.2$ against $31.7$) and with only the S-score and ratio aggregated ($70.6$
against $37.7$, both $p < 0.001$). With instability computed only over each compound's defined
configurations, the 16 Klaeger compounds with no active kinase that have at
least two defined configurations have lower mean instability than active ones
within the entropy ($7.0$ against $13.5$) and the Gini ($6.7$ against $13.6$,
both $p = 0.002$). This is a group result: three of the 16 (brivanib, brivanib
alaninate and NMS-1286937) are less stable than the active mean, and the
compounds contribute different numbers of configurations. (\texttt{claim\_checks.py} sections 13 and 20,
\texttt{undefined\_\allowbreak{}score\_\allowbreak{}convention.py})

\paragraph{Six Klaeger compounds with no detected binding.} AZD-2014, AZD-8055,
AZD-8186, BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib show no binding to any curated
target, although they are established inhibitors of mTOR, mTOR, PI3K$\beta$,
JAK2, MET and MET. mTOR and PI3K$\beta$ were not quantified for any of the 229
compounds with a fitted target in the Kinobeads table, consistent with low
expression or poor capture in the lysate. PI3K$\beta$, a lipid kinase, is also
outside the curated panel. JAK2 and MET were quantified for 7 and 32 other
compounds (up to $\mathrm{p}K_d = 8.3$ and $9.5$), so absence from the lysate
does not explain the result for BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib. These data
do not show whether compound-specific capture, competition or curve fitting
explains it. (\texttt{claim\_checks.py} section 1)

\paragraph{Boundary sensitivity.} Table~S3 gives the share of pairs of scored
compounds whose order reverses as each parameter moves across
$B = [x_0, \tau^*]$. $B$ is $[5.0, 6.0]$ on Davis and Klaeger, $[4.0, 6.0]$ on
Metz and $[20, 50]\%$ inhibition on Anastassiadis. For the ratio, the parameter
is the level $b$ in $R_b(k) = x_{(1)} - \max(x_{(k+1)}, b)$. The gated
entropy's gate is swept with $T$ fixed at $\tau^* - x_0$. Every parameter
reverses more than $13\%$ of pairs on at least one dataset (the ratio's level only at $k = 5$). Figure~S2 shows the per-compound rank instability
on Klaeger. The S-score's reversal counts are exact. For the other scores,
we evaluate the boundary at 201 equally spaced values. For the gated entropy,
we also evaluate the gate at each observed value in $B$ and just below it.
Using 101 grid points instead of 201 changes no reversal
percentage by more than $0.01$ percentage point.
(\texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py},
\texttt{failure\_mode\_illustration.py})

\paragraph{Profile features and instability.} Table~S4 gives the correlations
between profile features and within-definition instability, with the compounds
that have an active kinase ranked among themselves, and, for Klaeger, ranked
among all 222 compounds. The ranking population changes the Gini column most:
ranked among all compounds, the skewness of the active affinities is the Gini's
strongest correlate ($+0.262$), and ranked among active compounds it is not
significant ($+0.072$), while the number of active kinases becomes significant
($-0.254$). Over the 189 active compounds whose entropy and Gini are
defined throughout the baseline sweep, re-ranked among themselves, the number of active
kinases correlates with entropy and Gini instability at $-0.274$ and $-0.272$
(both $p < 0.001$). Figure~S3 plots each definition's strongest correlate. With a
Bonferroni correction for the 24 tests per dataset, 8 of the 13 nominally
significant Klaeger correlations survive, and on Davis only the ratio--gap
correlation. The Holm procedure gives the same result. The Benjamini--Hochberg
procedure keeps all 13 Klaeger correlations and, on Davis, again only the
ratio--gap correlation. On Anastassiadis and Metz, with active compounds ranked among
themselves, the number of active kinases correlates with entropy instability at
$-0.38$ and $-0.42$ (both $p < 0.001$), and the top$_1$--top$_2$ gap with ratio
instability at $0.00$ ($p = 0.97$) and $-0.26$ ($p < 0.001$). Without the eight
Anastassiadis compounds whose two best targets are both at $99.5\%$ inhibition
or more, the gap--ratio correlation there is $-0.15$ ($p = 0.10$).
(\texttt{instability\_by\_family.py}, \texttt{additional\_\allowbreak{}datasets\_\allowbreak{}analysis.py},
\texttt{claim\_checks.py} sections 4 and 21)

\paragraph{Undefined features.} The skewness of the active affinities is
undefined with fewer than three active kinases, and their standard deviation
and range with fewer than two. Table~S4 sets these to $0$. Over only the
compounds for which each feature is defined, the Klaeger range associations
strengthen (S-score, entropy and Gini $-0.373$, $-0.378$ and $-0.349$, all
$p < 0.001$), the Gini--skewness association stays non-significant ($+0.145$,
$p = 0.08$), and no Davis correlation changes significance.
(\texttt{feature\_\allowbreak{}placeholder\_\allowbreak{}sensitivity.py})

\paragraph{Range and skewness.} Range rises with the number of active kinases
(Spearman $0.76$ on Klaeger). With that number held fixed, range remains
associated with S-score instability (partial $r = -0.22$, $p = 0.002$) but not
with entropy or Gini instability ($+0.08$ and $+0.01$). On the 46 compounds
profiled in both Davis and Klaeger, the range--S-score association is weak on
both (Klaeger $r = -0.10$, $p = 0.56$, for the 37 with at least two active
kinases, and Davis $-0.16$, $p = 0.30$). These compounds have a median of 4
active kinases on Klaeger and 32 on Davis. Among the 148 Klaeger compounds
with at least three active kinases, skewness correlates with the share of a
compound's weight on kinases that the $\beta$ sweep crosses ($0.38$),
and that share with Gini instability with the number of active kinases held
fixed (partial $r = 0.43$). Ranked among active compounds, skewness has no
marginal association with Gini instability, but with the number of active
kinases held fixed it does (partial $r = 0.35$, $p < 0.001$). Holding the

\section*{S4: The criteria D2 to D4, the gated entropy and its characterization}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition 1.} With the gate at $g$, every gated kinase has
weight $w_i = e^{(x_i - g)/T} > 1$. A kinase added at or below $g$ has weight
$0$ and leaves the score unchanged. A kinase added with value in $(g, \tau^*]$
has weight $a = e^{(x - g)/T} \leq e^{(\tau^* - g)/T}$, which is at most $e$ when
$T \geq \tau^* - g$. Let the original profile have total weight
$W = \sum_i w_i$ and $L = \sum_i w_i \ln w_i$, so that its entropy in nats is
$H = \ln W - L/W$. After the addition,
\[
  H' - H = \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{a}{W}\right) + \frac{a}{W + a}\left(\frac{L}{W} - \ln a\right).
\]
Every $w_i > 1$, so $L > 0$, and $a \leq e$, so $\ln a \leq 1$. With
$t = a/W > 0$ this gives $H' - H > \ln(1 + t) - t/(1 + t) > 0$. The entropy
therefore rises, so the score does not. $\square$ With $T = \tau^* - x_0$ and
$g \in B$, $\tau^* - g \leq T$, so the condition holds for every gate in $B$.
A random check over 20{,}000 weight vectors with every weight at least $1$
found no case in which appending a weight of at most $e$ lowers the entropy,
and $1{,}537$ cases when the appended weight is $1.05\,e$.

\paragraph{Sharpness (\emph{only if} part).} The bound cannot be lowered. If $T < \tau^* - g$, a
kinase added at $\tau^*$ has weight $r = e^{(\tau^* - g)/T} > e$. Take one active target with weight
$s$ and $n$ kinases just above the gate, with weights close to $1$. For the
limiting weights the entropy is $H_n = \ln(n + s) - s\ln s/(n + s)$, and after
the addition $H'_n = \ln(n + s + r) - (s\ln s + r\ln r)/(n + s + r)$. Then
$n(H'_n - H_n) \to r(1 - \ln r) < 0$ as $n \to \infty$, so for large enough $n$
the entropy falls and the score rises. By continuity, this also holds for
weights slightly above $1$, as the strict gate requires. Numerically, with $T$ $3\%$ below
$\tau^* - x_0$, a profile with one target just above $\tau^*$ and 280 kinases
just above the floor violates D3 by $2.2 \times 10^{-4}$ bits on Klaeger, Metz
and Anastassiadis, and with $T$ $1\%$ above the bound no such profile does. A
random search over 20{,}000 profiles per dataset found no violation with
$T = \tau^* - x_0$, and adding one kinase at $\tau^*$ never raises the score of
a real compound.
(\texttt{d4\_\allowbreak{}entropy\_\allowbreak{}vs\_\allowbreak{}candidate.py}
sections 1--5 and 14)

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition 2.} \emph{D4 and grouping fix the aggregate.}
This step does not use $h$. On the profiles $\mathbf{v}_m$, the positive
weights are $m$ equal weights, so D4 requires $A_F(m) = F(1/m, \ldots, 1/m)$ to
be nondecreasing in $m$ and $A_F(N) > A_F(1)$. Shannon\cite{Shannon1948} (Theorem 2) showed
that a continuous $F$ with the grouping property and increasing $A_F$ is
$F(\mathbf{p}) = -K\sum_i p_i \log p_i$. His argument uses the monotonicity of
$A_F$ only to show that $A_F(n) = K\log n$, for which nondecreasing $A_F$
suffices, and $A_F(N) > A_F(1)$ gives $K > 0$. \emph{D2 fixes the weights.}
Take a profile with two targets above $\tau^*$, at distances $u \geq v > 0$
above the gate placed at $\tau^*$, and lower the gate by $d$ with
$0 < d \leq \tau^* - x_0$. The gate passes no value, so D2 requires the score,
and therefore the Shannon entropy of the two shares, to be unchanged. For two
shares, this entropy falls strictly as the ratio of the larger weight to the
smaller rises from $1$, and $h$ is nondecreasing, so the ratio is unchanged:
$h(u + d)/h(v + d) = h(u)/h(v)$. Hence $A(d) = h(u + d)/h(u)$ does not depend
on $u$. Then $A(d_1 + d_2) = A(d_1)\,A(d_2)$ whenever $d_1 + d_2 \leq \tau^* - x_0$,
and $A$ is continuous, so $A(d) = e^{cd}$ with $c \geq 0$, because $h$ is
nondecreasing. Repeated shifts give $h(t) = C e^{ct}$ for every $t > 0$. With
$c = 1/T$ these are the gated entropy's weights, and $c = 0$ gives equal
weights. The score is therefore $K$ times the gated entropy with this $T$. The
R\'enyi entropy of order $\alpha \neq 1$ is continuous and increases with $n$
on equal shares but lacks the grouping property. $\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition 3.} Let $W = \sum_i w_i$ and sort the
original positive weights in decreasing order. Because $a \leq \min_i w_i$, the
added weight comes last among the positive weights. For $j \leq r$ the sum of
the $j$ largest new shares, $\sum_{i \leq j} w_{(i)}/(W + a)$, is at most the
corresponding old sum, $\sum_{i \leq j} w_{(i)}/W$, and for $j > r$ both sums
equal 1. The new shares are therefore more even than the old ones (the old
shares majorize the new ones), so $f$ does not increase. $\square$
Functions $f$ that never increase when the shares become more even are called
Schur-convex. The negative Shannon and
R\'enyi entropies have it because the entropies are Schur-concave. The largest
share has it, and here falls to $w_{(1)}/(W + a)$. The Gini coefficient has
it on a fixed panel. For the Shannon
entropy, the formula for $H' - H$ in the proof of Proposition 1 also gives the
result directly: $L/W \geq \ln a$ because every $w_i \geq a$, so the first term
is positive and the second non-negative. The condition on $a$ is
needed: the weights $10$ and fifty $1$s have $H = 5.353$ bits, and adding
another $10$, tied with the strongest, lowers it to $5.180$ bits. Without the
condition, a concentration score can also rank a profile with more active
targets as more selective, as it should when binding is more concentrated:
$H(1, 1) = 1$ bit, but $H(1000, 1, 1) = 0.023$ bits.

\paragraph{Checks of D4 and Proposition 3.} On reference profiles
$\mathbf{v}_m$ with $a = 6.5$, $7$, $8$ and $9$ on a 30-kinase panel, every
score in Table~4 of the main text, $P_{\max}$ and the published entropy
with floor-coded pairs given zero weight satisfy D4. The active count with its sign reversed and the gated entropy
without its non-binder gate fail it. A random check of Proposition 3 over
20{,}000 weight vectors found no violation. Adding one kinase at
$\mathrm{p}K_d = 12$ to a profile with one target at $9$ and fifty at $5.1$

\paragraph{Variants.} D3 is proved for the Shannon order with
$T \geq \tau^* - x_0$. For the other variants it is checked by adding one kinase
at $\tau^*$ to every scored compound, and it held for all of them.
\emph{Quantile floor.} Using a per-compound low quantile of the profile (the
5th to 15th percentile) as the gate gives a variant invariant to shifts of the
whole profile, but on these data it coincides with the gated entropy. On Davis every such
percentile equals the floor for all but at most two compounds, and on Klaeger
for every compound. At the 10th percentile the variant ranks compounds as the
gated entropy does (Spearman $1.000$), and across the five percentiles no pair
reverses.
\emph{R\'enyi order.} Replacing the Shannon entropy by the R\'enyi entropy of
order $\alpha$, $-H_\alpha(p) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1}\log_2 \sum_i p_i^\alpha$,
gives a score that still satisfies D1 and D2. Orders $0.5$ to $3$ rank compounds as the Shannon
order does at Spearman $0.93$ or higher (Table~S6). Without D1
(Section~S5), orders from $0.01$ to $1$ reach $p^* = 80$ on Klaeger and Metz,
and orders $2$ and $3$ reach it at 110 on Klaeger and 80 on Metz.
\emph{Scale.} On Klaeger, over $T = 0.5$, $0.75$, $1$, $1.5$ and $2$, the
correlation with the number of active kinases ranges from $-0.776$ ($T = 0.5$)
to $-0.945$, and D3 held for every scored compound at every scale, including
the two below the bound of Proposition 1. Across these scales the rankings
agree at Spearman $0.79$ or higher, and for $T \geq 1$ at $0.985$ or higher.
\emph{Other weights.} Equal weights (the limit $T \to \infty$) and the
association-constant weights of the published entropy ($T = 1/\ln 10$) rank
compounds with Spearman $0.963$--$0.978$ and $0.773$--$0.825$ against the gated
entropy on Davis and Klaeger (Table~S6).
D3 held for every scored compound in the measured profiles under every
variant in Table~S6.
(\texttt{family\_construction.py}, \texttt{family\_panelsize.py},
\texttt{candidate\_validation.py})

\paragraph{What the criteria determine.} Proposition 2 characterizes the scores in one class:
each target above the gate is weighted by the same function of its distance
above the gate, and the score is an aggregate of the weight shares with
Shannon's grouping property. Within this class, D2 and D4 leave the gated
entropies, with any $T > 0$, and the equal-weight limit, and D3 leaves those
with $T \geq \tau^* - x_0$ (Proposition 1). The class assumption is needed. If
each weight is a function of the distance above a fixed reference and only the
gate moves, D2 holds for every weight function and does not determine the
weights. The criteria also leave $T$ free above
$\tau^* - x_0$. We choose the smallest admissible value, $T = \tau^* - x_0$,
which makes the score depend most on affinity differences.
The published entropy, with floor-coded pairs given zero weight, is the member
with $T = 1/\ln 10 \approx 0.43$ $\mathrm{p}K$ units, below the bound on all
three $\mathrm{p}K$ datasets, which is why it violates D3.

\section*{S7: Clinical analysis}

For the 46 FDA-approved Klaeger compounds with FAERS records, we correlated
each definition's median selectivity rank with the proportions of FAERS
reports that were serious, fatal or involved hospitalization. FAERS counts
were retrieved from the openFDA adverse-event interface (\texttt{faers\_pull.py}).
No correlation was statistically significant in this exploratory analysis
(all $|r| \leq 0.20$, all $p > 0.19$); this does not establish the absence of
clinically relevant associations. Raw
report counts correlate weakly with the ratio ranking ($r = 0.30$, $p = 0.04$),
which may reflect prescription volume. FAERS proportions describe reports, not
incidence among treated patients, and they depend on indication and
prescription volume. The analysis therefore cannot isolate an effect of
selectivity on toxicity.
(\texttt{clinical\_\allowbreak{}outcomes\_\allowbreak{}correlation.py})
