\section*{Table S2: Bosc et al.\ window/ranking score and the CATDS proxy}

Bosc et al.'s window score (WS) and ranking score (RS) and a CATDS-style
proxy (Discussion) were computed on all four datasets to check whether they
behave as additional members of the ratio family (WS, RS) or the
distribution-based family (CATDS), as claimed in the Discussion.
(\texttt{bosc\_catds\_comparison.py})

WS and RS correlate strongly with the ratio family on every dataset ($r =

distributional construction despite its historical classification as a ratio
measure.

\section*{Table S4: Candidate measure cross-dataset validation}

Table~S4 reports three checks for each dataset. Orientation is the correlation
with the number of active kinases, where more negative means more correctly
oriented toward ``fewer active targets = more selective''. D3 is reported as in
Methods, as the percentage of pairs of scored compounds whose order reverses
somewhere in $B = [x_0, \tau^*]$. It is given for the emphasis parameter $\beta$
with the gate $x_0$ fixed (the featured measure), for the gate $x_0$ itself swept
as a free parameter (the like-for-like comparison with the entropy's baseline,
Figure~6 of the main text), and for the entropy's own baseline. D4 is the
percentage of compounds for which the score does not rise when one kinase is
added at the reliability threshold $\tau^*$, the strongest binding D4 allows
and so the addition most likely to raise the score. $B$ is
$[5.0, 6.0]$ on Davis and Klaeger, $[4.0, 6.0]$ on Metz, and $[20, 50]\%$
inhibition on Anastassiadis, and the smoothing width is $T = \tau^* - x_0$ on
every dataset (Methods). D3 passes at $\varepsilon = 1\%$.
(\texttt{candidate\_validation.py}, \texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py})

D4, across all four datasets. Orientation and D3 are computed over the

The ``gate swept'' and ``entropy'' columns confirm, on every dataset, that the
candidate's D3 satisfaction rests on fixing the gate $x_0$ by declaration.
Swept over the same interval, the candidate's gate reverses about as many pairs
as the entropy's baseline does, and the emphasis parameter, with the gate fixed,
reverses fewer than one pair in a hundred.
Removing the gate inverts the measure's orientation entirely (gated $r =
-0.951$ vs.\ ungated $r = +0.930$ against $n_\mathrm{active}$ on Klaeger),
which is why the gate is required for correct orientation.

\section*{S5: Variants of the candidate measure}

The featured candidate is one member of a family of measures. Three axes of
variation are reported here: the floor, the R\'enyi order, and the smoothing
width $T$. Each variant is checked against D1--D4 as in the main text. D4 is
proved only for the featured measure (Shannon order, $\beta = x_0$,
$T \geq 0.58\,(\tau^* - x_0)$), and is otherwise checked by adding one kinase at
$\tau^*$ to every compound. (\texttt{family\_construction.py},
\texttt{family\_panelsize.py}, \texttt{candidate\_validation.py})

\paragraph{Quantile floor.} Replacing the fixed floor $x_0$ with a per-compound
low quantile of the profile (the 10th percentile) gives a variant that is
invariant to shifts of the readout. On Davis and Klaeger it ranks compounds
exactly as the featured measure does (Spearman $1.000$), no pair of scored
compounds reverses as the quantile is varied from the 5th to the 15th
percentile, and D4 holds for every compound.

\paragraph{R\'enyi order.} Generalizing the Shannon functional to R\'enyi order
$\alpha > 0$,
\[
  -H_\alpha(p) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1}\log_2 \sum_i p_i^\alpha,
\]
gives a family that recovers the featured measure at $\alpha = 1$. D1 holds for every
order, since the gate is applied before the entropy is computed, and D2 holds
vacuously. D4 holds for additions at or below the floor, because
$p_i^\alpha = 0$ whenever $p_i = 0$, and holds on every compound with one kinase
added at $\tau^*$ for each order tested. D3 separates the orders
(Table~S5): the orders $0.5$ and $1$ pass on both datasets, $\alpha = 2$ is borderline
(it passes on Davis and narrowly fails on Klaeger), and $\alpha = 3$ fails on
both.

The order does not affect panel recoverability. Every order tested, from
$0.01$ to $3$, reaches $p^* = 80$ on both Klaeger and Metz, and the mean
convergence curves differ by at most $0.003$ between orders at every panel size.
We feature $\alpha = 1$ because it is the Shannon entropy, for which
D4 is proved.

\paragraph{Smoothing width.} The featured measure uses $T = \tau^* - x_0$. On
Klaeger, over $T = 0.5$, $0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, and $2$ (in units of
$\tau^* - x_0 = 1$), orientation is unchanged ($r \approx -0.95$) and the
rankings agree closely across all five values (worst-case pairwise Spearman
$0.999$). D3 passes for $T \geq 0.75$ (pairs reversing as the emphasis baseline
is swept: $0.93\%$, $0.54\%$, $0.17\%$, $0.04\%$ for $T = 0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, $2$)
and narrowly fails at $T = 0.5$ ($1.94\%$), which also falls below the D4 bound
$T \geq 0.58$. As $T \to 0$ the softplus converges to the hard cutoff,
recovering the entropy together with its D3 and D4 failures.
