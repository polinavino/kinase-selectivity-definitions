(Section~S1), three robustness checks summarized in the main text, the variants
of the candidate measure (Section~S5), and further analyses summarized briefly

\section*{S1: Panel size without the D1 gate}

Karaman et al.\ scored each compound's S-score on 500 random subpanels of each
size and found that it varies more as panels shrink. This section repeats that
design for all four definitions on all four datasets and, like Karaman et al.,
scores every compound on every subpanel. The main text (Recovery from Small
Panels) applies the D1 gate instead.

For each panel size, in steps of 30 from $p = 50$ targets up to the full panel
(363 targets on Davis, 217 on Klaeger, 291 on Anastassiadis, and 172 on Metz),
we drew 50 random subsets of $p$ targets, computed each definition on the
subpanel, and measured agreement with the full-panel ranking by Spearman
correlation. Each definition uses the dataset's floor $x_0$ ($20\%$ on
Anastassiadis) as its baseline (entropy, Gini) or clipping level (ratio,
$k = 1$), and the activity threshold as the S-score cutoff. $p^*$ is the
smallest panel size at which the mean correlation exceeds $0.90$ (main text,
Recovery from Small Panels). Changing the bar within $[0.80, 0.95]$ changes the
$p^*$ values but never reverses the order of two definitions on any dataset
(Table~S1).

The four definitions differ substantially in their panel size requirements
(Figure~S1). Selectivity entropy and the S-score recover the
full-panel ranking fastest on every dataset: at 50 targets, the smallest panel
in this analysis, on Davis and Anastassiadis, at 80 on Klaeger, and at 80
(entropy) and 50 (S-score) on Metz. The ratio is the slowest everywhere,
needing 350 of 363 targets on Davis, 260 of 291 on Anastassiadis, 170 of 172 on
Metz, and the complete panel on Klaeger, where it reaches only $r = 0.887$ at
200 targets. The Gini coefficient falls in between: it needs 110 targets on
Davis and 200 on Anastassiadis and Klaeger, but matches the entropy on Metz
(80). Its slower recovery comes from compounds whose Gini is undefined on the
subpanel, which the convention ranks least selective (main text, Methods). Excluding
them gives the Gini $p^*$ values of 50, 80, 50, and 80 targets.

On a 50-target subpanel, entropy- and S-score-based rankings are reasonably
stable on every dataset ($r = 0.84$--$0.97$). Ratio rankings are nearly
uncorrelated with the full-panel ranking ($r \leq 0.17$), and Gini rankings
range from close agreement on Metz ($r = 0.87$) to almost none on Klaeger
($r = 0.09$). Without the D1 gate, the smaller the panel, the more the choice
of definition determines the ranking.

with the number of active kinases, where more negative means more correctly
oriented toward ``fewer active targets = more selective''. D2 is the
percentage of pairs of scored compounds whose order reverses somewhere in
$B = [x_0, \tau^*]$ (Methods). It is given for the emphasis parameter $\beta$
with the gate $x_0$ fixed (the featured measure), for the gate $x_0$ itself

\section*{S5: Variants of the candidate measure}

Three variations of the featured candidate are reported here: the floor, the
R\'enyi order, and the smoothing width $T$. D3 is proved only for the Shannon
order, either with $\beta = x_0$ and $T \geq 0.58\,(\tau^* - x_0)$, or with any
$\beta$ in $B$ and $T = \tau^* - x_0$ (Methods). Otherwise it is checked by
adding one kinase at $\tau^*$ to every scored compound. (\texttt{family\_construction.py},
\texttt{family\_panelsize.py}, \texttt{candidate\_validation.py})

\paragraph{Quantile floor.} Replacing the fixed floor $x_0$ with a per-compound
low quantile of the profile (the 5th to 15th percentile) gives a variant that
is invariant to shifts of the readout, but on these data it is not a separate
test: on Davis every such percentile equals the floor for all but at most two
compounds, and on Klaeger for every compound. At the 10th percentile the
variant ranks compounds as the featured measure does (Spearman $1.000$), and
across the five percentiles $0.04\%$ (Davis) and $0\%$ (Klaeger) of pairs
reverse.

\paragraph{R\'enyi order.} Generalizing the Shannon functional to R\'enyi order
$\alpha > 0$,
\[
  -H_\alpha(p) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1}\log_2 \sum_i p_i^\alpha,
\]
gives a family that recovers the featured measure at $\alpha = 1$. D1 holds for
every order, since the gate is applied before the entropy is computed. D3 holds
for every scored compound with one kinase added at $\tau^*$, for each order
tested. Swept over $B$, the emphasis baseline reverses at most $\varepsilon$ of
pairs for the orders $0.5$ and $1$ on both datasets and for $\alpha = 2$ on
Davis, and more for $\alpha = 2$ on Klaeger and for $\alpha = 3$ (Table~S5).

The order does not affect panel recoverability. Every order tested, from
$0.01$ to $3$, reaches $p^* = 80$ on both Klaeger and Metz, and the mean
Spearman curves differ by at most $0.002$ between orders at every panel size.
We feature $\alpha = 1$ because it is the Shannon entropy, for which
D3 is proved.

\paragraph{Smoothing width.} The featured measure uses $T = \tau^* - x_0$. On
Klaeger, over $T = 0.5$, $0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, and $2$ (in units of
$\tau^* - x_0 = 1$), orientation is unchanged ($r \approx -0.95$) and the
rankings agree closely across all five values (worst-case pairwise Spearman
$0.999$). The emphasis baseline reverses $0.86\%$, $0.53\%$, $0.11\%$ and
$0.04\%$ of pairs for $T = 0.75$, $1$, $1.5$ and $2$, within $\varepsilon$, and
$1.94\%$ at $T = 0.5$, which also falls below the D3 bound $T \geq 0.58$. As $T \to 0$ the softplus converges to the hard cutoff,
recovering the entropy, with its baseline sensitivity and D3 failure.

\section*{S6: Additional analyses}

The analyses below are summarized briefly in the main text, and are given here
in the order the main text cites them.

\paragraph{Released panels.} The main analyses use one reference target per
human protein kinase (Methods). Table~S6 compares the main Davis and Klaeger
results with the released panels: Davis's 433 constructs, including 12 of
EGFR, and Klaeger's 343 Kinobeads targets. On Davis, the best affinity per gene is also given as an
alternative to the reference construct. On the released Davis panel, 26 of the
68 compounds have their two best-bound constructs from the same gene. The
most ratio-unstable compound there, GDC-0879, owes its $0.023$ top$_1$--top$_2$
gap to BRAF(V600E) and BRAF, and its gap to the next gene (RAF1) is $1.54$.

Klaeger et al.\ annotate 225 of the 343 released Klaeger targets as protein or
lipid kinases. The curated panel keeps 217 of them and leaves out three
lipid-kinase columns (PIP4K2A, PIP4K2C and PIP4K2A;\allowbreak PIP4K2B), four protein
groups (CSNK2A1;\allowbreak CSNK2A3, PDPK1;\allowbreak PDPK2P, PRKCE;\allowbreak PRKCH and
PRKX;\allowbreak PRKY), and Q6ZSR9,
which UniProt describes as an uncharacterized protein with no kinase domain,
encoded in the 3'-UTR of AAK1. The other 118 targets are not annotated as
kinases: 46 subunits or adaptors of kinase complexes (for example the cyclins
CCNA2--CCNT2, CKS1B, the AMPK and PKA regulatory subunits, and the AP2 adaptor
subunits), 7 small-molecule kinases, 20 other enzymes (for example COMT, NQO2,
TOP2B, PYGB, PYGL, and PTPN1), 43 other proteins, including ribosomal proteins
and SLC transporters, and BCR and TRRAP. The source flags 88 of the 118 as
possibly co-enriched as complex members, among them BCR (with ABL1) and TRRAP
(with CDK8). Kinobeads co-capture complex partners with their kinases, so a
CDK hit can also count its cyclin. The 122 targets outside the curated panel,
other than the four protein groups, hold 556 of the 2{,}763 active entries
($20.1\%$), in 122 of the 222 compounds. Six compounds are active only on them
(AZD-2014, AZD-8186, NMS-1286937, SGX-523, TG-100115, and volitinib) and have
no active target on the curated panel. The code repository lists every target
with the reason it is kept or removed. Most results change little
between the panels. The largest changes are on Davis, where the ratio agrees
more with the other definitions on the curated panel and its dependence on the
number of active targets and on the second-ranked affinity disappears, and on
Klaeger, where with all compounds included the Gini sits closer to the ratio on
the curated panel.

\paragraph{Klaeger confidence filter.} The high-confidence filter removes 793
of the 5{,}916 drug--target rows in the table ($13.4\%$), one of which has no
numeric $K_d^\mathrm{app}$. A removed pair is treated identically to one with
no detected binding and assigned the same $\mathrm{p}K_d = 5.0$ background
value. Of the 792 low-confidence pairs with a numeric $K_d^\mathrm{app}$, 405
involve curated targets, and 402 of these fall within the $222 \times 217$
matrix. Adding the 402 pairs changes $0.83\%$ of its entries and shifts every
Klaeger cell of main-text Table~1 by at most $0.08$. It leaves the four
definitions' panel-size $p^*$ values (Section~S1) unchanged and lowers the
candidate's from 80 to 50 targets. Retaining all low-confidence
pairs also adds 7 compounds and shifts the Klaeger cells of Table~1 by at most
$0.06$. (\texttt{klaeger\_filter\_sensitivity.py})

\paragraph{Skewness and Gini instability.} Among the 148 Klaeger compounds with
at least three active kinases, skewness correlates with the share of a
compound's weight carried by kinases that the $\beta$ sweep crosses
($r = 0.38$), and that share is associated with Gini instability with the
number of active kinases held fixed (partial $r = 0.70$). Holding it fixed as
well lowers the skewness association from $0.50$ to $0.28$, so this mechanism
accounts for part of the association. (\texttt{claim\_checks.py}, section 16)

\paragraph{Further profile features.} Five further features were tested
against within-family instability on Klaeger and Davis: two concentration
measures from industrial-organization economics, the field the Gini
coefficient comes from (the top-target concentration ratio CR$_1$ and the
Herfindahl--Hirschman Index, HHI), the primary-target affinity, a near-tie count
generalizing the top$_1$--top$_2$ gap, and the active-affinity coefficient of
variation. CR$_1$ and HHI are near-collinear with $n_\mathrm{active}$
($r = -0.98$ to $-0.99$ for CR$_1$ and $-0.997$ to $-0.998$ for HHI) and so
carry the same information. The other three were weaker than the strongest feature of main-text
Table~2 in every family and on both datasets.
(\texttt{instability\_\allowbreak{}predictor\_\allowbreak{}candidates.py})

\paragraph{Type~3 compounds.} Seven of the ten largest entropy--ratio
disagreements among active Klaeger compounds have a top$_1$--top$_2$ gap above
$1$, for example dovitinib ($n_\mathrm{active} = 22$, gap $= 1.93$) and
BMS-690514 ($n_\mathrm{active} = 20$, gap $= 1.98$). The ratio ranks these
seven 14th to 56th of 222, and the entropy and Gini rank them 121st to 195th.
(\texttt{claim\_checks.py}, section 5;
\texttt{klaeger\_\allowbreak{}selectivity\_\allowbreak{}results.csv})

\paragraph{Most stable compounds.} On Klaeger the most stable compounds are
among the most promiscuous. XL-228 ($\sigma = 8.7$) and PF-03814735
($\sigma = 9.9$), with 40--49 active kinases, have median ranks of 199--218.5
of 222 under every definition, and mean instability falls from about 29--36 for
compounds with 1 to 40 active kinases to 19 above 40. On Davis, compounds with
few active kinases are the most stable on average (mean $\sigma = 3.9$ for one
