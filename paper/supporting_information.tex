\section*{Table S4: Candidate measure cross-dataset validation}

Orientation (correlation with the number of active kinases; more negative is
more correctly oriented toward ``fewer active targets = more selective''); D3
as defined in Methods, i.e.\ the percentage of pairs of scored compounds whose
order reverses somewhere in $B = [x_0, \tau^*]$, for the emphasis parameter
$\beta$ with the gate $x_0$ fixed (the featured measure), for the gate $x_0$
itself swept as a free parameter (the like-for-like comparison with the
entropy's baseline; Figure~6 of the main text), and for the entropy's own
baseline; and the percentage of compounds for which D4 holds when one kinase is
added at the reliability threshold $\tau^*$, the hardest case. $B$ is
$[5.0, 6.0]$ on Davis and Klaeger, $[4.0, 6.0]$ on Metz, and $[20, 50]\%$
inhibition on Anastassiadis; the smoothing width is $T = \tau^* - x_0$ on every
dataset (Methods). D3 passes at $\varepsilon = 1\%$.
(\texttt{candidate\_validation.py}, \texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py})

D4, across all four datasets.}

The ``gate swept'' and ``entropy'' columns confirm, on every dataset, that the
candidate's D3 satisfaction rests on fixing the gate $x_0$ by declaration
rather than on the measure being inherently insensitive to its placement: swept
over the same interval, the candidate's gate reverses about as many pairs as
the entropy's baseline does, while the emphasis parameter, with the gate fixed,
reverses fewer than one pair in a hundred.
Removing the gate inverts the measure's orientation entirely (gated $r =
-0.936$ vs.\ ungated $r = +0.943$ against $n_\mathrm{active}$ on Klaeger),
which is why the gate, not merely the smooth emphasis, is required for D1 and
correct orientation jointly.

\section*{S5: Variants of the candidate measure}

The featured candidate is one member of a family of measures. Three axes of
variation are reported here: the floor, the R\'enyi order, and the smoothing
width $T$. Each variant is checked against D1--D4 as in the main text; D4 is
proved only for the featured measure (Shannon order, $\beta = x_0$,
$T \geq 0.58\,(\tau^* - x_0)$), and is otherwise checked by adding one kinase at
$\tau^*$ to every compound. (\texttt{family\_construction.py},
\texttt{family\_panelsize.py}, \texttt{candidate\_validation.py})

\paragraph{Quantile floor.} Replacing the fixed floor $x_0$ with a per-compound
low quantile of the profile (the 10th percentile) gives a variant that is
invariant to shifts of the readout. On Davis and Klaeger it ranks compounds
exactly as the featured measure does (Spearman $1.000$), no pair of scored
compounds reverses as the quantile is varied from the 5th to the 15th
percentile, and D4 holds for every compound.

\paragraph{R\'enyi order.} Generalizing the Shannon functional to R\'enyi order
$\alpha > 0$,
\[
  -H_\alpha(p) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1}\log_2 \sum_i p_i^\alpha,
\]
gives a family that recovers the featured measure at $\alpha = 1$. D1 holds for every
order, since the gate is applied before the entropy is computed, and D2 holds
vacuously. D4 holds for additions at or below the floor, because
$p_i^\alpha = 0$ whenever $p_i = 0$, and holds on every compound with one kinase
added at $\tau^*$ for each order tested. D3 separates the orders
(Table~S5): $\alpha \leq 1$ passes on both datasets, $\alpha = 2$ is borderline
(it passes on Davis and narrowly fails on Klaeger), and $\alpha = 3$ fails on
both.

The order affects panel recoverability on the sparser Klaeger panel. Every
order below $1$ tested ($0.01$, $0.25$, $0.5$, $0.75$) reaches $p^* = 80$, ahead
of the Shannon order's $110$, while $\alpha \in \{2, 3\}$ needs $290$, matching
the Gini. On Metz every order from $0.01$ to $3$ reaches $p^* = 80$. This
speed does not come from discarding information: the number of distinct scores
and the size of the largest tied group are identical across every order tested,
including $\alpha \to \infty$, and the only tied compounds are those with at
most one active kinase. It reflects how each limit behaves under subsampling.
As $\alpha \to 0$ the score approaches a count of the active set, which a random
subpanel estimates with low variance; as $\alpha \to \infty$ it depends on the
single largest weight, and the estimate is far off whenever the subpanel misses
the kinase carrying it. Agreement with the Shannon ranking is as high on the
sparsest compounds ($n_\mathrm{active} < 5$) as on the rest for
$\alpha \leq 0.5$ ($\geq 0.98$ in both subsets, on Klaeger and Metz), whereas
$\alpha \to \infty$ loses agreement most sharply among the sparsest Metz
compounds ($0.62$, against $0.92$ for the rest; on Klaeger, $0.98$ and $0.95$).
We feature $\alpha = 1$ because it is the Shannon entropy, for which D4 is
proved; a lower order is a documented alternative with faster panel
recoverability on Klaeger.

\paragraph{Smoothing width.} The featured measure uses $T = \tau^* - x_0$. On
Klaeger, over $T = 0.5$, $0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, and $2$ (in units of
$\tau^* - x_0 = 1$), orientation is unchanged ($r \approx -0.94$) and the
rankings agree closely across all five values (worst-case pairwise Spearman
$0.999$). D3 passes for $T \geq 0.75$ (pairs reversing as the emphasis baseline
is swept: $0.93\%$, $0.54\%$, $0.17\%$, $0.04\%$ for $T = 0.75$, $1$, $1.5$, $2$)
and narrowly fails at $T = 0.5$ ($1.94\%$), which also falls below the D4 bound
$T \geq 0.58$. As $T \to 0$ the softplus converges to the hard cutoff,
recovering the entropy together with its D3 and D4 failures.
