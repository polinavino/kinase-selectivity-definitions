Dear Editor and Reviewer 1,

Thank you for a careful reading. In summary:

  - The analyses now use one reference target per human protein kinase. The
    released Davis panel lists mutant and domain constructs of the same gene
    (for 26 of the 68 compounds the two best-bound constructs are forms of one
    gene), and the released Klaeger panel includes 120 proteins that are not
    kinases. Every Davis and Klaeger number changed with this curation. The
    released panels are compared in Supporting Information Section S6.
  - The properties are revised (Major 4, 6 and 7). Fixing a parameter is now
    treated as a protocol decision, not as meeting distributional consistency,
    which no measure examined meets. The former D2 (bounded gap sensitivity)
    is removed, because different reference off-targets answer different
    questions; the ratio's dependence on its reference is reported as a
    result. Three properties remain: D1 (reliability threshold), D2
    (distributional consistency, formerly D3) and D3 (monotonicity, formerly
    D4). Property numbers below refer to the revised manuscript.
  - Two simple comparators, the active count and the entropy with its baseline
    at the activity threshold, are added. Both satisfy D1 and D3, so the
    candidate is presented more narrowly: it satisfies D3 while still
    weighting binding below the activity threshold.
  - Recovery from small panels is evaluated separately from the properties,
    for all measures on the same random subpanels, with the share of
    compounds that can still be scored (Results, "Recovery from Small
    Panels"; Figure 7).
  - Results that were only in the code repository are now in Supporting
    Information, and Table S7 lists the script behind every result.

Re-running every analysis after a review of the code against Methods also
found errors that change some reported numbers. They are listed under
"Further corrections" at the end. Reviewer text is abridged, and locations
are given by section name.

REVIEWER 1 — MAJOR COMMENTS
====================================================================

1. State that entropy and Gini are re-operationalized; show the correlation
matrix under the published forms.

The qualification now appears in every summary of the clustering result: the
Abstract, Introduction (contribution i), the opening of the Discussion,
Limitations and the Conclusion. Methods ("Faithfulness to the original
definitions") states that every entropy and Gini result concerns the
baseline-subtracted form.

The requested matrices are in Supporting Information Table S3 and summarized
in Methods. Under the published entropy the two-family pattern does not hold.
On Davis, Klaeger and Metz the association-constant entropy correlates more
with the ratio (r = 0.69, 0.66 and 0.76) than with the S-score (0.34, 0.57
and 0.29) or the Gini (0.37, 0.53 and 0.39). The native percent-inhibition
Gini on Anastassiadis correlates with the entropy (0.63) and weakly, about
equally, with the S-score (0.34) and the ratio (0.33).


2. Report what the Klaeger filter removes and the sensitivity to relaxing it;
restate the Metz threshold or report its coverage distribution.

Klaeger (Methods, "Datasets"; details in Supporting Information Section S6).
The filter removes 793 of 5,916 rows (13.4%). Restoring the 403 low-confidence
pairs that fall inside the analysed matrix changes 0.83% of its entries, moves
every Klaeger cell of Table 1 by at most 0.08, and leaves every Klaeger
panel-size p* unchanged. Retaining all low-confidence pairs also adds 7
compounds and moves the cells by at most 0.06. Checking the panel itself, I
found that 120 of its 343 targets are not protein kinases, including 46
kinase-complex subunits, such as cyclins, that Kinobeads co-capture. The
analyses now use the 219 protein kinases that are single genes.

Metz. The phrase "keeping profiles in which most entries reflect a real
measurement" has been removed. The threshold is stated as at least 50 of 172
kinases (>= 29% coverage), and Methods reports the coverage of the 704
retained compounds: 29.1% to 83.1% (mean 40.9%, median 37.2%, 75th percentile
47.1%), with 23 compounds at exactly 50 kinases and 170 at 55 or fewer.

This comment also led me to test the floor imputation on Metz. Nearly every
measured value lies above the floor, so a compound measured against more
kinases looks less selective: coverage correlates with median rank at 0.98
(entropy), 0.95 (Gini), 0.76 (S-score) and 0.11 (ratio). Methods, Results and
Limitations now qualify the Metz findings, and D2 percentages are given for
Metz separately.


3. State the convention for undefined scores, and report the per-definition
contrast under an alternative.

Methods ("Rank Stability Analysis") states the convention. An undefined
entropy or Gini is set to 0, which ranks the compound as maximally selective
under the entropy and minimally selective under the Gini. The ratio
substitutes the value assigned to non-detection for a missing off-target, and
the S-score is always defined. Methods ("Ratio") notes that this departs from
Bosc et al.'s convention, under which R(k) would meet D1.

The per-definition contrast is in Results ("Existing Definitions Against the
Properties", D1). On Klaeger it is significant within a single definition only
for the Gini, small and not significant for the entropy and the ratio, and
reversed for the S-score. As you noted, R(k) contributes to the pooled
contrast, which comes mainly from the definitions disagreeing about these
compounds and persists with the undefined configurations dropped (sigma 66.3
against 31.6). Under the alternative you proposed, zero-active compounds are
more stable than active ones (entropy 6.9 against 13.2, Gini 6.7 against
13.3, p = 0.002). The case for D1 therefore no longer rests on these scores'
instability, but on their being computed from sub-threshold values alone,
which each definition treats by its own convention. Results ("The Two-Family
Structure Across Assay Technologies") gives the per-definition contrast for
Anastassiadis and Metz.


4. Define rho ~ -0.93, and address whether the floor can be regarded as
fixed.

It was the lowest Spearman correlation between the rankings at any two points
of the sweep over [4.5, 6.5], negative because the two ends of that range give
nearly inverted rankings. It has been replaced by the D2 statistic (Major 6).

Your reading is correct. Swept over B = [x0, tau*], the candidate's gate
reverses about as many pairs as the entropy's baseline (Table S4). Fixing a
parameter is a protocol decision and does not make a ranking robust to it, so
D2 now reports each measure's boundary sensitivity, and no measure passes, the
candidate included (Table 3). The Discussion recommends fixing the boundary in
the analysis protocol and reporting its effect. With its gate fixed, the
candidate's remaining parameter reverses at most 0.72% of pairs. The activity
threshold tau*, also fixed in the candidate, barely affects its ranking
(Spearman >= 0.999 for tau* from 5.5 to 7). Limitations states that in none of
the four datasets is x0 a measured detection limit.


5. State which parameter was varied in Figure 6, and show the floor sweep.

The right panel of Figure 6 now shows three sweeps over B = [5.0, 6.0] on
Klaeger, and the caption names the parameter each varies: the entropy's
baseline, the candidate's gate x0, and the candidate's emphasis baseline with
x0 fixed. The 17-fold annotation has been removed. Mean instability is 7.3 for
the gate and 7.5 for the entropy's baseline, against 0.3 for the emphasis
baseline.


6. Restate D2 (formerly D3) so that the verdicts follow from its definition.

D2 is now defined over an interval (Methods, "Motivated Properties"). A pair of
compounds reverses in B if their score difference is positive at some value
of the analyst's parameter in B and negative at another, and D2 requires that
at most a fraction epsilon of the pairs of scored compounds reverse. The
S-score is evaluated at every observed affinity in B, and other parameters on
201 values. Swept over B, the S-score's threshold reverses 8.4-13.0% of pairs
on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis and 21.8% on Metz, and the entropy's and
Gini's baselines 7.9-12.5% and 22.5-22.8%. The verdicts are the same for any
epsilon from 1% to 13% (we use 1%).

Each property's statement now says how it is assessed: D2 empirically on all
four datasets, D1 from the measure's reporting rule, and D3 from its
functional form. D3 now concerns an added kinase bound above the floor and at
or below the activity threshold, since adding a non-binder can reasonably make
a compound look more selective; no verdict changes. The caption of Table 3
states how each row is assessed.


7. State the conclusion more directly; give vacuous satisfaction its own
symbol.

The Abstract and Conclusion now state the conclusions directly: no measure
examined satisfies D2, so the activity boundary must be fixed by protocol; D1
is a reporting rule any measure can adopt; and D3 separates the measures.
Table 3 now uses only satisfied and violated, for the four definitions, the
two comparators and the candidate. The vacuous verdicts came from the former
D2, which has been removed (summary above).

You asked for evidence that the candidate improves on the entropy. On these
data it does not, and the Abstract, Results and Conclusion say so: the two
rank compounds almost identically (Spearman 0.994-0.998), and the candidate
recovers rankings from subpanels no better than the entropy with its baseline
at the floor. What the candidate shows is that D3 can hold while binding below
the activity threshold still counts. The active count and the entropy with
its baseline at the activity threshold satisfy D3 by ignoring such binding.
The entropy with its baseline at the floor violates D3 by its functional form
("Why the Smooth Hinge Matters" gives a worked example); on real data this
happens only on Anastassiadis at baselines below the 20% floor. The
candidate's D3 is proved (Methods, Proposition).


8. Revise the Type 1 description.

Revised (Results, Type 1). The text names the compounds' established targets
and says that none appears as a high-confidence hit. I tested your explanation
against the Kinobeads table. For AZD-2014, AZD-8055 and AZD-8186, the target
was not quantified for any of the 229 compounds, consistent with poor
expression or capture. For BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib it does not hold:
JAK2 and MET were quantified for 7 and 32 other compounds (up to pKd 8.3 and
9.5).


9. Benchmark definitional instability against platform variability.

Added (Limitations). Matching PubChem CIDs to Klaeger drug names gives 46
compounds profiled in both Davis and Klaeger. On the 200 kinases both panels
profile, the same definition's Davis and Klaeger rankings correlate at
Spearman 0.33-0.84 (mean 0.60), against -0.09 to 1.00 between definitions on
one dataset (mean 0.56), so the choice of definition moves rankings about as
much as the change of dataset. Without matching the targets, the
cross-dataset correlations are lower (mean 0.52), so part of the difference
between datasets comes from which kinases each profiles. Practical
Recommendations adds that within-dataset rank instability is not associated
with how far a compound's rank moves between the datasets (|r| <= 0.23).


10. Discuss how these properties limit the use of any single measure.

Added (Limitations). All four definitions and the candidate treat a profile as
an unordered set of affinities, so they score inert and safety-relevant
off-targets alike, and none accounts for exposure. The paragraph concludes
that a selectivity score is at best a summary of binding breadth, to be
weighed alongside target-specific liability knowledge and exposure modeling.


11. Introduce the properties and candidate in Methods, evaluate them in
Results, and consolidate the qualifications.

Done. Methods now contains "Motivated Properties" (the formal statement,
motivation and assessment of each property, and the recoverability criterion)

with proof). Results evaluates them in "Existing Definitions Against the

The manuscript is about 840 words (7.5%) longer than the version you read.
The increase comes from the analyses requested in this review, the curated

FURTHER CORRECTIONS
====================================================================

All affected numbers, tables, figures and Supporting Information have been
updated.

1. Undefined entropy scores. The code added 1e-10 inside the logarithm, which
ranked compounds with an undefined entropy just below single-target profiles
instead of tied with them, as Methods states. The exact formula is now used.
Among other changes, the per-definition D1 contrast for the entropy is no
longer significant (Major 3).

2. Renyi order and panel size. The previous claim that orders below 1 recover
the Klaeger ranking faster than the Shannon order came from scoring compounds
with no gated weight on a subpanel differently at each order. Scored as in
the main analysis, every order reaches p* = 80 on Klaeger and Metz, and the
claim has been removed.

3. Panel size. The subsampling analysis, previously run on Klaeger and Metz
only, now covers all four datasets (Figure 5, Table S1). The panel-size
summary ("about a third of a panel") has been removed from the Abstract,
since 50 kinases was the smallest panel tested and the candidate's p* equals
the entropy's. Recovery is now evaluated separately, from 10 targets, with
coverage reported (Figure 7).

4. Gated panel size. The D1 gate brings the Gini to the entropy's p*, by
removing compounds whose Gini is undefined on a subpanel. The previous
attribution of part of the candidate's convergence to the gate has been
removed.

5. Smoothing width. The D3 proof requires T >= 0.58 (tau* - x0). The previous
widths on Metz and Anastassiadis were below this bound, and constructed
profiles violate D3 there. T is now tau* - x0 on every dataset, which changes
the Metz and Anastassiadis rankings negligibly (Spearman 0.999).

6. Two-family wording. The Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion no longer say
that the S-score, entropy and Gini "agree closely", only that they agree more
with one another than with the ratio. The Introduction and Limitations qualify
the grouping (clear only on the pKd datasets, and on Klaeger only among
compounds with an active kinase). Results states that on Anastassiadis the
separation comes mainly from the 45 compounds with no active kinase: among
the other 133, the ratio correlates 0.79-0.84 with the family, against
0.87-0.999 within it. The third Practical Recommendation applies only to the
log-affinity forms.

7. D2 percentages. They were computed on 5 to 9 parameter values. They are now
exact for the S-score and computed on 201 values otherwise (101 values change
no percentage by more than 0.1 point).

8. Bosc et al.'s scores. R(k) equals Bosc et al.'s ranking score at rank k + 1,
apart from the floor fallback, so their agreement follows from the definition;
Methods and Discussion now say so. Background now states that Bosc et al.
computed several metrics at more than one parameter value. The CATDS proxy is
no longer used to draw conclusions about the published CATDS score.

9. Quantile-floor variant. On these data it coincides with the featured
measure (the 5th-15th percentiles of almost every profile equal the floor), so
it is no longer presented in the main text, and Section S5 says so.

10. References. The FDA count is updated to 94 approved agents (Roskoski, 2026
update), and Bajorath et al. is corrected to Miljkovic and Bajorath.


I hope the manuscript is now suitable for publication in Molecular
Informatics. Thank you again for a constructive review.
