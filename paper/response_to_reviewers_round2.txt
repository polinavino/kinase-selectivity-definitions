Dear Editor and Reviewer 1,

Thank you for a careful reading. In summary:

  - The manuscript is consolidated around one question: which analysis
    choices change selectivity rankings. The Introduction and Background are
    merged, Methods describes only what was done, and Results has four
    sections: how analysis choices change agreement between scores, parameter
    sensitivity and profile associations, the criteria and the gated
    entropy, and recovery from small panels. The main text is about 6,600 words, against about
    11,100 in the version you read, with three figures. Supporting
    Information follows the same order (Sections S1-S8) and holds the full
    evidence for each check. The Abstract is shortened to the journal's limit
    of 1,000 characters.
  - The title is changed to match this focus (both titles are given above).
    The previous title suggested that the paper characterizes a well-formed
    measure.
  - The analyses use one reference target per human protein kinase. The
    released Davis panel lists mutant and domain constructs of the same gene
    (for 26 of the 68 compounds the two best-bound constructs are forms of
    one gene). On Klaeger we keep the 217 single-gene protein kinases that
    the source annotates, except an uncharacterized protein without a kinase
    domain, which removes cyclins and other co-captured proteins, and on Anastassiadis one entry per kinase (291 of
    300). Most Davis and Klaeger numbers changed. The code repository lists
    every target with the reason it was kept or removed.
  - The published entropy is compared with the log-affinity form, under two
    treatments of pairs without detected binding, in the agreement and the
    recovery analyses (Major 1).
  - Analyses of compounds with an active kinase now rank them among
    themselves, as the eligibility rule (D1) implies. Ranked among all
    compounds, part of an active compound's instability came from how
    compounds without an active kinase are scored. This removed the marginal
    association between skewness and Gini instability that we had
    highlighted (Major 3).
  - The properties, now called criteria, are revised (Major 4, 6 and 7). D1
    is renamed activity eligibility. Fixing a parameter is treated as a
    protocol decision, not as meeting distributional consistency, which no
    boundary parameter we tested meets. The former D2 (bounded gap
    sensitivity) is removed, because different reference off-targets answer
    different questions. Three criteria remain: D1, D2 (distributional
    consistency, formerly D3) and D3 (monotonicity under weak-target
    addition, formerly D4). Criterion numbers below refer to the revised
    manuscript.
  - Simple comparators are added: the active count, the entropy with its
    baseline at the activity threshold, the S-score with its threshold at the
    floor, and the partition index at the most strongly bound target. Several
    satisfy D1 and D3, so the gated entropy is presented narrowly (Major 7).

Re-running every analysis after a review of the code against Methods also
found errors that change some reported numbers. They are listed under
"Further corrections" at the end. Reviewer text is abridged, and locations
are given by section name.

REVIEWER 1 — MAJOR COMMENTS
====================================================================

1. State that entropy and Gini are re-operationalized; show the correlation
matrix under the published forms.

Methods ("Selectivity Scores and Their Implementations") defines the
published and log-affinity forms together, states that the baseline is a
parameter we introduce, and lists every implementation in Table 2. The
Abstract, Results and Discussion state that the grouping of the S-score,
entropy and Gini holds for the log-affinity form and not for the published
entropy.

The published forms are compared in Results ("Analysis Choices Change
Agreement Between Scores"; Table 3, Figure 1), with full results in
Supporting Information Table S2. As you requested, pairs without a measured
affinity are excluded. On Davis such pairs are coded at the floor (71% of the
matrix), so we report both treatments, floor-coded pairs given zero weight and
kept at the floor. Under both, the published entropy correlates more with the
ratio than with the S-score or Gini on Davis, Klaeger and Metz. With
floor-coded pairs given zero weight, it correlates with the ratio at 0.86, 0.66
and 0.76, and with the S-score at 0.74, 0.57 and 0.29. With them at the floor,
its correlation with the S-score on Davis is 0.34. The published and
log-affinity entropies correlate at 0.37 (Davis) and -0.31 (Klaeger) with
floor-coded pairs at the floor, and at 0.74 and 0.79 with them given zero
weight. The native percent-inhibition Gini on Anastassiadis correlates with
the entropy at 0.63 and with the S-score and ratio at 0.34 and 0.33.

The published entropy is also in the recovery analysis (Figure 3). It needs
300, 200 and 150 targets on Davis, Klaeger and Metz to reach a mean Spearman
of 0.90 with its full-panel ranking, against 20, 50 and 65 for the
log-affinity entropy, under either floor treatment (Figure 3; Supporting
Information Section S5 for the at-floor treatment).

On Klaeger, the floor-excluded treatment also drops 194 measured high-confidence
affinities weaker than 10 uM. A third treatment keeps every measured affinity
at its measured value and omits only unmeasured pairs. It
changes the published entropy's ranking little (Spearman 0.9996 with the
floor-excluded ranking among active compounds), changes its correlations with
the other definitions by at most 0.013, and leaves its recovery p* at 200
targets (Supporting Information Section S2).


2. Report what the Klaeger filter removes and the sensitivity to relaxing it;
restate the Metz threshold or report its coverage distribution.

Klaeger (Methods; Supporting Information Section S1). The filter removes 793
of 5,916 rows (13.4%). Restoring the 402 low-confidence pairs that fall inside
the analysed matrix changes 0.77% of its entries, moves the all-compound
Klaeger correlations by at most 0.08 and those among compounds with an active
kinase by at most 0.006, and leaves every p* of the recovery analysis
unchanged. Checking the panel itself, we found that 118 of its 343 targets are
not annotated as kinases by the source, including kinase-complex subunits,
such as cyclins, that Kinobeads co-capture. The analyses now use the 217
single-gene protein kinases that the source annotates, except Q6ZSR9, an
uncharacterized protein without a kinase domain. This also removes BCR and
TRRAP, which the source flags as possibly co-enriched with
ABL1 and CDK8. Methods now distinguishes the pairs coded at the floor: no
detected binding, low-confidence fits (375 of the 405 on curated targets have
a fitted pKd above 5, so a low-confidence fit does not establish weak binding)
and 194 measured affinities weaker than 10 uM. Giving them all the floor value
is stated as an analysis convention.

Metz (Methods; Supporting Information Section S1). The threshold is stated as
at least 50 of 172 kinases, and Section S1 reports the coverage of the 704
retained compounds (29.1% to 83.1%; median 37.2%). Results ("Analysis Choices
Change Agreement Between Scores") presents Metz as a case of missing
measurements: coverage correlates with median rank at 0.98 (entropy), 0.95
(Gini), 0.76 (S-score) and 0.11 (ratio).


3. State the convention for undefined scores, and report the per-definition
contrast under an alternative.

Methods ("Conventions"; Table 2) states the conventions. Results gives the
main findings for the 22 Klaeger compounds with no active kinase, and
Supporting Information Section S3 the full per-definition contrasts. Within
one definition the contrast is significant only for the Gini, small and not
significant for the entropy and the ratio, and reversed for the S-score.
Under the alternative you proposed, these compounds are more stable than
active ones within the entropy (7.0 against 13.5) and the Gini (6.7 against
13.6, p = 0.002, for the 16 of them defined at two or more baselines). The
case for D1 therefore rests on these scores being computed from
sub-threshold values alone, not on their instability. Methods notes that our
ratio, like Bosc et al.'s ranking score, scores every compound.

Your comment also led us to the ranking population. Instability of active
compounds is now computed with only the active compounds ranked (Methods,
"Ranking Comparisons and Sensitivity Analysis"). Ranked among all 222 Klaeger compounds, skewness was
the strongest correlate of Gini instability (r = +0.26); ranked among the
active compounds it is not significant (r = +0.07, p = 0.31), and the number of
active kinases correlates with Gini instability on both pKd datasets. Results
reports this, and Supporting Information Table S4 gives both rankings.


4. Define rho ~ -0.93, and address whether the floor can be regarded as
fixed.

It was the lowest Spearman correlation between rankings at any two points of
the sweep over [4.5, 6.5]. It has been replaced by the D2 statistic (Major 6).
Your reading is correct: swept over B = [x0, tau*], the gated entropy's gate
reverses about as many pairs as the entropy's baseline (Results, "Parameter
Sensitivity and Profile Associations"). Fixing a parameter is a protocol decision, D2 reports each
score's boundary sensitivity, and no boundary parameter passes. With its gate
fixed, the gated entropy's remaining parameter reverses at most 0.72% of
pairs. The Limitations state that in none of the four datasets is x0 a
measured detection limit.


5. State which parameter was varied in Figure 6, and show the floor sweep.

This figure is now Supporting Information Figure S2. Its caption names the
parameter each sweep varies: the entropy's baseline, the gated entropy's gate
with its emphasis baseline moved with it, and the emphasis baseline with the
gate fixed. Mean instability on Klaeger is 6.8 for the gate and 7.0 for the
entropy's baseline, against 0.4 for the emphasis baseline.


6. Restate D2 (formerly D3) so that the verdicts follow from its definition.

D2 is defined over an interval (Methods, "Evaluation Criteria and the Gated
Entropy"). A pair of
compounds reverses in B if their score difference is positive at some value of
the parameter in B and negative at another, and D2 requires that at most 1% of
the pairs of scored compounds reverse, on every dataset and, for the ratio,
for every k. A score that fixes a parameter is assessed with it swept. For the
ratio, the parameter is the level b in R_b(k) = x(1) - max(x(k+1), b). The
S-score's threshold reverses 8.4-13.0% of pairs on Davis, Klaeger and
Anastassiadis and 21.8% on Metz, and the entropy's and Gini's baselines
7.9-12.4% and 22.5-22.8%. The ratio's level reverses 3.0-13.7% on Klaeger and
2.1-9.7% on Anastassiadis, 0-2.6% on Davis and 0.04-1.03% on Metz. Under this rule the
verdicts are the same for any tolerance up to 13% (Supporting Information
Table S3). Methods presents D2
as a boundary-sensitivity diagnostic: a score whose question changes with the
threshold can reasonably rank compounds differently at different thresholds,
and fixing the boundary defines the protocol without removing the
sensitivity. D1 and D3 are judged by construction, and D2 empirically.


7. State the conclusion more directly; give vacuous satisfaction its own
symbol.

The Abstract states the conclusions directly: no boundary parameter we tested
satisfies D2, so the boundary must be fixed by protocol, and simple measures
and the gated entropy satisfy D3. The Conclusion draws the reporting
implications, and Results adds that D1 is a reporting rule any score can adopt
and that D3 separates the scores. Table
4 uses only satisfied and violated. The vacuous verdicts came from the former
D2, which has been removed.

You asked for evidence that the candidate improves on the entropy. On these
data it does not, and the paper says so. The gated entropy ranks compounds
almost as the entropy does (Spearman 0.994-0.998), and almost as the S-score
with its threshold at the floor does (0.998-0.9997), which also satisfies D1
and D3, as does the partition index at the most strongly bound target. It
recovers rankings from subpanels no better than that S-score. What it adds is
narrow: the entropy meets D3 only when binding below the threshold gets zero
weight, whereas the gated entropy weights that binding and still meets D3, with
a proof. The proof is in
Supporting Information Section S4; Methods gives the proposition and the
reason it holds. The paper also states its limits: the score is discontinuous
at the floor, and strengthening a target already in the profile can raise it.


8. Revise the Type 1 description.

Revised (Results; Supporting Information Section S3). For AZD-2014, AZD-8055
and AZD-8186, the target was not quantified for any compound, consistent with
poor expression or capture, and PI3Kbeta, a lipid kinase, is outside the
curated panel. For BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib, JAK2 and MET were
quantified for other compounds, so absence from the lysate does not explain
the result; the text states that these data cannot tell whether
compound-specific capture, competition or curve fitting does.


9. Benchmark definitional instability against platform variability.

Added (Results, "Analysis Choices Change Agreement Between Scores"; full
results in Supporting Information Section S6). On 46 compounds profiled in
both Davis and Klaeger and the 201 kinases both panels share, the same
definition's rankings correlate at 0.34-0.84 between datasets (mean 0.61),
against -0.08 to 1.00 between definitions within a dataset (mean 0.57).
Kinases are matched by gene symbol with renamed symbols mapped to current ones
(Davis's MLCK is MYLK3).
Among the 40 compounds active in both, the means are 0.72 and 0.73. The
comparison is descriptive.


10. Discuss how these properties limit the use of any single measure.

Added (Discussion, Limitations). No score considered uses target identity or
exposure, so a selectivity score is at best a summary of binding breadth, to
be weighed with target-specific liability knowledge and exposure modeling.


11. Introduce the properties and candidate in Methods, evaluate them in
Results, and consolidate the qualifications.

Done, as described in the summary. Methods defines the criteria and the gated
entropy once ("Evaluation Criteria and the Gated Entropy"). Results gives the
D2 sweeps with the other parameter sensitivity ("Parameter Sensitivity and
Profile Associations") and evaluates the rest in one section ("Criteria and the
Gated Entropy"), which brings together the criteria table, the
comparators, the gated entropy and the entropy counterexample. Each finding has one main location in the
main text, with its qualification next to it, and the full evidence is in the
Supporting Information section that follows the same order.

FURTHER CORRECTIONS
====================================================================

All affected numbers, tables, figures and Supporting Information have been
updated.

1. Undefined entropy scores. The code added 1e-10 inside the logarithm, which
ranked compounds with an undefined entropy just below single-target profiles
instead of tied with them. The exact formula is now used.

2. Ranking population. Profile analyses of active compounds ranked them among
all compounds; they now rank them among themselves (Major 3).

3. Panel size. The analysis without the D1 gate, previously in Results, is in
Supporting Information Section S5. The earlier statement that weighting weak
binding explains fast recovery was wrong: the published entropy weights weak
binding and recovers slowly. Results now states that scores whose ranking
depends mostly on the strongest targets recover slowest, and that p* is a
threshold on the mean over subpanels.

4. Smoothing width. The D3 proof requires T >= 0.58 (tau* - x0). The previous
widths on Metz and Anastassiadis were below this bound. T is now tau* - x0
on every dataset.

5. Grouping wording. The paper no longer says that the S-score, entropy and
Gini "agree closely", only that they agree more with one another than with
the ratio among compounds with an active kinase. On Anastassiadis the
separation among active compounds is weak, and Results says so.

6. D2 percentages are exact for the S-score. The gated entropy's gate is
evaluated at the observed values, where its score jumps, and on 201 values,
which raises its upper value from 14.2% to 14.4%. The other parameters are
evaluated on the 201 values, and Methods calls these percentages grid
estimates.

7. Bosc et al. The earlier statement that Bosc et al.'s convention leaves
ratio-type scores undefined was wrong: they withheld ranks only for the
S-score, and their ranking score scores every compound. The Introduction now
also notes that they traced individual disagreements to profile shape, and
the novelty claim is narrowed accordingly. The Davis floor
is attributed to DeepDTA. The CATDS score is not reimplemented because it
scores one designated target from Kinobeads curves, which only Klaeger
provides.

8. Comparing studies. The recommendation on comparisons between studies
(Discussion, item 6) no longer advises restricting them to compounds on which
the definitions agree. It asks for a protocol fixed in
advance and for the compounds on which scores disagree to be reported.

9. References. The FDA count is updated (Roskoski, 2026), Miljkovic and
Bajorath is corrected to ACS Omega 3(3):3113-3119, and Cheng et al. (2010)
and Uitdehaag et al. (2012) are added. The description of Khinchin's axioms
was wrong and has been removed; the axiomatic background is reduced to one
sentence in the Introduction.

10. Measurement noise. The noise check reapplies D1 to the noisy profiles,
and Methods states its model: on the affinity datasets it perturbs measured
values and leaves floor-coded pairs fixed, and on Anastassiadis it perturbs
every entry.

11. Proposition. The SI now proves that the bound on T cannot be lowered
(previously shown only by examples), states the exact constant
1/ln(2^e - 1) = 0.5816, and corrects a bound to a/m < 2.213.

12. Recovery benchmark. The 0.90 bar is described as a chosen descriptive
benchmark, not as an intraclass-correlation standard, and Results reports
that the comparisons between scores hold with bars of 0.80, 0.85 and 0.95. Methods states that each score is
compared with its own full-panel ranking, and Results notes that the compounds
lost on small panels are mostly those with few active targets.

13. Wording. A group-level result about the 16 compounds with no active kinase
is no longer stated as holding for each compound, and the Anastassiadis floor
is described as an operational cutoff for weak inhibition, not a noise level.


I hope the manuscript is now suitable for publication in Molecular
Informatics. Thank you again for a constructive review.
