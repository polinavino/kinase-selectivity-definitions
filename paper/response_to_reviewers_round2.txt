1. State that entropy and Gini are re-operationalized; show the correlation
matrix under the published forms.

The qualification now appears in every summary of the clustering result: the
Abstract, Introduction (contribution i), the opening of the Discussion,
Limitations and the Conclusion. Methods ("Faithfulness to the original
definitions") states that every entropy and Gini result concerns the
baseline-subtracted form.

The requested matrices are in Supporting Information Table S3 and summarized
in Methods. Under the published entropy the two-family pattern does not hold.
On Klaeger and Metz the association-constant entropy correlates more with the
ratio (r = 0.79 and 0.76) than with the S-score (0.57 and 0.29) or the Gini
(0.69 and 0.39). On Davis it decouples from both families (0.42 with the
S-score and the Gini, 0.34 with the ratio). The native percent-inhibition Gini
on Anastassiadis correlates with the entropy (0.63) and weakly, about equally,
with the S-score (0.35) and the ratio (0.31). Figure 1's Klaeger entropy value
is now -0.34 (previously -0.32; Further corrections, item 1).


2. Report what the Klaeger filter removes and the sensitivity to relaxing it;
restate the Metz threshold or report its coverage distribution.

Klaeger (Methods, "Datasets"). The filter removes 793 of 5,916 rows (13.4%).
Restoring the 561 numeric low-confidence pairs that fall inside the
222 x 343 matrix changes 0.74% of its entries, moves every Klaeger cell of
Table 1 by at most 0.05, and leaves every Klaeger panel-size p* unchanged.
Restoring all 792 also adds 7 compounds and 177 kinases. The Klaeger cells of
Table 1 then move by up to 0.12 (S-score against ratio, 0.22 to 0.34), and the
two-family structure is unchanged.

Metz. The phrase "keeping profiles in which most entries reflect a real
measurement" has been removed. The threshold is stated as at least 50 of 172
kinases (>= 29% coverage), and Methods reports the coverage of the 704
retained compounds: 29.1% to 83.1% (mean 40.9%, median 37.2%, 75th percentile
47.1%), with 23 compounds at exactly 50 kinases and 170 at 55 or fewer.

This comment also led me to test the floor imputation on Metz. Nearly every
measured value lies above the floor, so a compound measured against more
kinases looks less selective: coverage correlates with median rank at 0.98
(entropy), 0.95 (Gini), 0.76 (S-score) and 0.11 (ratio). Methods, Results and
Limitations now qualify the Metz findings, and the candidate's panel-size
summary in the Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion no longer draws on Metz
(Further corrections, item 3).


3. State the convention for undefined scores, and report the per-definition
contrast under an alternative.

Methods ("Rank Stability Analysis") states the convention. An undefined
entropy or Gini is set to 0, which ranks the compound as maximally selective
under the entropy and minimally selective under the Gini. The ratio
substitutes the assay floor for a missing off-target, and the S-score is
always defined. Methods ("Ratio") notes that this fallback departs from Bosc et
al.'s convention and is why R(k) violates D1.

The per-definition contrast is in Results ("Existing Definitions Against the
Properties", D1). The pooled Klaeger contrast (sigma 74.0 against 31.2) is
significant within a single definition only for the Gini (86.1 against 15.9).
It is small and not significant for the entropy (14.1 against 11.9, p = 0.25)
and the ratio (24.0 against 20.7, p = 0.08), and reverses for the S-score
(18.5 against 24.3, p = 0.07). As you noted, R(k) contributes to the pooled
contrast. The contrast comes mainly from the definitions disagreeing about these
compounds, which the S-score and entropy rank among the most selective and
the ratio in the bottom third (median ranks 22, 25 and 156 of 222), and it
persists with the undefined configurations dropped (59.6 against 30.6).
Under the alternative you proposed, zero-active compounds are more stable
than active ones (entropy 9.8 against 12.8, p = 0.046; Gini 9.5 against 13.0,
p = 0.028; 14 of the 16 compounds, the other two being defined at fewer than
two baselines). The case for D1 therefore no longer rests on these scores'
instability, but on their being computed from sub-threshold values alone,
which each definition treats differently. Results ("The Two-Family Structure Replicates Across Assay
Technologies") gives the breakdown for Anastassiadis and Metz.


4. Define rho ~ -0.93, and address whether the floor can be regarded as
fixed.

It was the lowest Spearman correlation between the rankings at any two points
of the sweep over [4.5, 6.5], negative because the two ends of that range give
nearly inverted rankings. It has been replaced by the D3 statistic (Major 6).
Sweeping the candidate's gate x0 over B = [x0, tau*] reverses 8.5-22.5% of
pairs across the four datasets, against 8.8-22.6% for the entropy's baseline
(Results, "The Candidate Measure"; Table S4). Your reading is therefore
correct: the candidate's D3 verdict holds only with its activity boundary
fixed. Table 3 marks it with its own symbol, and the Abstract, Introduction
and Conclusion state the condition.

Limitations now states that in none of the four datasets is x0 a measured
detection limit, and that where a dataset's encoding of non-detection is
uncertain, the candidate's standing on D3 is no better than the entropy's.


5. State which parameter was varied in Figure 6, and show the floor sweep.

The right panel of Figure 6 now shows three sweeps over B = [5.0, 6.0] on
Klaeger, and the caption names the parameter each varies: the entropy's
baseline, the candidate's gate x0 (the equivalent comparison), and the
candidate's emphasis baseline with x0 fixed. The 17-fold annotation has been
removed. Mean instability is 7.2 for the gate and 7.5 for the entropy's
baseline, against 0.4 for the emphasis baseline.


6. Restate D3 so that the verdicts follow from its definition.

D3 is now defined over an interval (Methods, "Motivated Properties"). A pair of
compounds reverses in B = [x0, tau*] if their score difference is positive at
some value of the analyst's parameter in B and negative at another. D3
requires that at most a fraction epsilon of the pairs of scored compounds
reverse. This removes the local reading you identified. Computed on each
dataset, the S-score, entropy and Gini reverse 8-23% of pairs and the
candidate's emphasis baseline at most 0.72%, so every verdict is the same for
any epsilon from 1% to 7% (we use 1%). The ratio has no parameter on the
affinity scale and satisfies D3 vacuously.

Each property's statement now says how it is assessed: D3 empirically on all
four datasets, D1 from the measure's reporting rule, and D2 and D4 from its
functional form. D1 refers to the activity threshold itself, and D2 and D4
are stated for profiles with a kinase above it, which is how they are
assessed. The caption of Table 3 marks which verdicts are empirical.


7. State the conclusion more directly; give vacuous satisfaction its own
symbol.

The Abstract and Conclusion now open with the four properties and the finding
that none of the four definitions satisfies all of them. The full statement is
in the Introduction (contribution iii) and in "Existing Definitions Against
the Properties": none of the
four definitions satisfies all four properties, the one property all four
violate is D1, and any of them could satisfy D1 by withholding or flagging
such scores.

With D3 restated, D1 is no longer the entropy's only violation. The entropy
fails D3 (8.8-22.6% of pairs reverse), and it fails D4 by its functional form:
its hard cutoff gives a kinase just above the baseline an arbitrarily small
weight, so adding a sub-threshold kinase can raise its score ("Why the Smooth
Hinge Matters" gives a worked example). On Anastassiadis at a 0% baseline,
adding one kinase at the activity threshold raises the score of 34% of the
compounds with an active kinase. The candidate's D4 is now proved (Methods,
Proposition).

You are right that the candidate and the entropy rank these datasets almost
identically (Spearman 0.994-0.999). The Introduction, Results and Conclusion
now say so, and the Introduction, Methods and Conclusion present the
candidate as a demonstration that the four properties can be met together. The properties are the main contribution.

In Table 3, a check mark in parentheses denotes vacuous satisfaction (D2 for
the S-score, entropy, Gini and candidate; D3 for the ratio), and a check mark
with a dagger denotes the candidate's D3, which holds only with its activity
boundary fixed at the floor.


8. Revise the Type 1 description.

Revised (Results, Type 1). The text names the compounds' established targets
(mTOR, JAK2, VEGFR2, JAK1 and HER2) and says that none appears as a
high-confidence sub-micromolar hit. I tested your explanation against the
Kinobeads table. For AZD-8055, vatalanib and ARRY-380, the target was not
quantified for any of the 229 compounds, consistent with poor expression or
capture. For BMS-911543 and filgotinib it does not hold: JAK2 and JAK1 were
quantified for 7 and 12 other compounds (up to pKd 8.3).


9. Benchmark definitional instability against platform variability.

Added (Limitations). Matching PubChem CIDs to Klaeger drug names gives 46
compounds profiled in both Davis and Klaeger. On these, the platform effect
(same definition, Davis rank against Klaeger rank) is on average larger than
the definition effect: Spearman -0.06 to 0.69 (mean 0.44), against 0.14 to
1.00 (mean 0.60). For the ratio, the two platforms' rankings are uncorrelated
(r = -0.06, p = 0.67). Practical Recommendations adds that within-dataset rank
instability does not predict how far a compound's rank moves between
platforms (|r| <= 0.25).


10. Discuss how these properties limit the use of any single measure.

Added (Limitations). All four definitions and the candidate treat a profile as
an unordered set of affinities, so they score inert and safety-relevant
off-targets alike, and none accounts for exposure. The paragraph concludes
that a selectivity score is at best a summary of binding breadth, to be
weighed alongside target-specific liability knowledge and exposure modeling.

FURTHER CORRECTIONS
====================================================================

All affected numbers, tables, figures and Supporting Information have been
updated.

1. Undefined entropy scores. The code added 1e-10 inside the logarithm, which
ranked compounds with an undefined entropy just below single-target profiles
instead of tied with them, as Methods states. With the exact formula:
   - the Klaeger panel-size p* of the entropy and the candidate falls from
     110 to 80 kinases;
   - three Klaeger cells of Table 1 change (S-score-entropy 0.901 to 0.910,
     entropy-Gini 0.866 to 0.840, entropy-ratio 0.483 to 0.455), as does the
     Klaeger entropy column of Table 2 (largest change: number of active
     kinases, -0.409 to -0.381);
   - the per-definition D1 contrast for the entropy is no longer significant
     (Major 3);
   - Figure 1's Klaeger entropy value moves from -0.32 to -0.34.
Davis and Metz are unaffected.

2. Renyi order and panel size. The previous claim that orders below 1 recover
the Klaeger ranking faster than the Shannon order came from scoring compounds
with no gated weight on a subpanel differently at each order. Scored as in
the main analysis, every order reaches p* = 80 on Klaeger and Metz, and the
claim has been removed.

3. Panel size on all four datasets. The subsampling analysis, previously run
on Klaeger and Metz only, now covers all four (Figures 5 and 7, Table S1).
The candidate reaches p* = 50 kinases on Davis and Anastassiadis and 80 on
Klaeger and Metz. Leaving out Metz for the coverage reason in Major 2, the
Abstract's summary changes from "about a third of a panel" to "from about a
tenth to a quarter of a panel on three of the four datasets".

4. Gated panel size. After item 1, the D1 gate leaves the candidate's and the
entropy's p* unchanged, so the previous attribution of part of the
candidate's convergence to the gate has been removed. The gate does bring the
Gini to the entropy's p*, by removing compounds whose Gini is undefined on a
subpanel.

5. Smoothing width. The D4 proof requires T >= 0.58 (tau* - x0). The previous
widths on Metz and Anastassiadis were below this bound, and constructed
profiles violate D4 there. T is now tau* - x0 on every dataset, which leaves
Davis and Klaeger unchanged and changes the Metz and Anastassiadis rankings
negligibly (Spearman 0.999).

6. Two-family wording. The Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion no longer say
that the S-score, entropy and Gini "agree closely" (their lowest correlation is
0.72, Klaeger S-score-Gini), only that they agree more with one another than
with the ratio. Results now states that on Anastassiadis the separation comes
mainly from the 44 compounds with no active kinase: among the other 134, the
ratio correlates 0.79-0.83 with the family, against 0.87-0.999 within it.


I hope the manuscript is now suitable for publication in Molecular
Informatics. Thank you again for a constructive review.
