Dear Editor and Reviewer 1,

Thank you for a careful reading. In summary:

  - The manuscript is consolidated around one question: which analysis
    choices change selectivity rankings. Methods describes only what was
    done, and Results has four sections: agreement between scores, parameter
    sensitivity and profile associations, the criteria and the gated
    entropy, and recovery from small panels. The main text is about 9,000
    words, against about 11,100 in the version you read, with three figures.
    Supporting Information (Sections S1-S8) follows the same order. The title
    is changed to match this focus, and the Abstract is within the journal's
    limit of 1,000 characters.
  - The analyses use one reference target per human protein kinase. The
    released Davis panel lists mutant and domain constructs of the same gene
    (for 26 of the 68 compounds the two best-bound constructs are forms of
    one gene). Klaeger is curated as described under Major 2, and
    Anastassiadis keeps one entry per kinase (291 of 300). Most Davis and
    Klaeger numbers changed. The code repository lists every target with the
    reason it was kept or removed.
  - The properties, now called criteria, are revised (Major 4, 6 and 7).
    D1 is renamed activity eligibility. The former D2 (bounded gap
    sensitivity) is removed, because different reference off-targets answer
    different questions. D2 (distributional consistency) and D3
    (monotonicity under weak-target addition) were D3 and D4. A new D4
    (orientation) requires that, on a fixed panel, binding equally strongly
    to more targets never makes a compound look more selective. Without it,
    the active count with its sign reversed would meet the other criteria as
    the gated entropy does. Every score studied satisfies D4 when floor-coded
    pairs have zero weight. The entropy and Gini fail it at baselines below
    the floor, which only the Anastassiadis sweep includes (Table 4).
    Criterion numbers below refer to the revised manuscript.
  - Re-running every analysis after a review of the code against Methods
    found errors that change some numbers. Each is reported under the
    comment it concerns, and the others under "Further corrections".

Reviewer text is abridged, and locations are given by section name.

REVIEWER 1 — MAJOR COMMENTS
====================================================================

1. State that entropy and Gini are re-operationalized; show the correlation
matrix under the published forms.

Methods ("Selectivity Scores and Their Implementations") defines the
published and log-affinity forms together, states that the baseline is a
parameter we introduce, and lists every implementation in Table 2. The
Abstract states that agreement changes with affinity weighting. Results and
Discussion state that the grouping of the S-score, entropy and Gini holds for
the log-affinity form and not for the published entropy. The paper no longer says that these three "agree closely", only that
they agree more with one another than with the ratio among compounds with an
active kinase. On Anastassiadis this separation is weak, and Results says so.

The published forms are compared in Results ("Analysis Choices Change
Agreement Between Scores"; Table 3, Figure 1), with full results in
Supporting Information Table S2. As you requested, pairs without a measured
affinity are excluded. On Davis such pairs are coded at the floor (71% of the
matrix), so we report both treatments: floor-coded pairs given zero weight and
kept at the floor. Under both, the published entropy correlates more with the
ratio than with the S-score or Gini on Davis, Klaeger and Metz. With zero
weight, it correlates with the ratio at 0.86, 0.66 and 0.76, and with the
S-score at 0.74, 0.57 and 0.29. The published and log-affinity entropies
correlate at 0.74 (Davis) and 0.79 (Klaeger) with zero weight, and at 0.37 and
-0.31 at the floor. The native percent-inhibition Gini on Anastassiadis
correlates with the entropy at 0.63 and with the S-score and ratio at 0.34 and
0.33. In the recovery analysis (Figure 3), the published entropy needs 300,
200 and 150 targets on Davis, Klaeger and Metz to reach a mean Spearman of
0.90 with its full-panel ranking, against 20, 50 and 65 for the log-affinity
entropy, under either floor treatment.

On Klaeger, zero weight also drops 194 measured high-confidence affinities
weaker than 10 uM. Keeping them at their measured values changes the
published entropy's ranking little (Spearman 0.9996 among active compounds)
and its correlations with the other definitions by at most 0.013 (Supporting
Information Section S2).


2. Report what the Klaeger filter removes and the sensitivity to relaxing it;
restate the Metz threshold or report its coverage distribution.

Klaeger (Methods; Supporting Information Section S1). The filter removes 793
of 5,916 rows (13.4%). Restoring the 402 low-confidence pairs that fall inside
the analysed matrix changes 0.77% of its entries, moves the all-compound
Klaeger correlations by at most 0.08 and those among compounds with an active
kinase by at most 0.006, and leaves every p* of the recovery analysis
unchanged. Checking the panel itself, we found that 118 of its 343 targets are
not annotated as kinases by the source, including kinase-complex subunits,
such as cyclins, that Kinobeads co-capture. The analyses now use the 217
single-gene protein kinases that the source annotates, except Q6ZSR9, an
uncharacterized protein without a kinase domain. This also removes BCR and
TRRAP, which the source flags as possibly co-enriched with ABL1 and CDK8.
Methods now distinguishes the pairs coded at the floor: no detected binding,
low-confidence fits (375 of the 405 on curated targets have a fitted pKd above
5, so a low-confidence fit does not establish weak binding) and 194 measured
affinities weaker than 10 uM. Giving them all the floor value is stated as an
analysis convention.

Metz (Methods; Supporting Information Section S1). The threshold is stated as
at least 50 of 172 kinases, and Section S1 reports the coverage of the 704
retained compounds (29.1% to 83.1%; median 37.2%). Results ("Analysis Choices
Change Agreement Between Scores") presents Metz as a case of missing
measurements: coverage correlates with median rank at 0.98 (entropy), 0.95
(Gini), 0.76 (S-score) and 0.11 (ratio).


3. State the convention for undefined scores, and report the per-definition
contrast under an alternative.

Methods ("Conventions"; Table 2) states the conventions. Checking them, we
found that the code added 1e-10 inside the logarithm, which ranked compounds
with an undefined entropy just below single-target profiles instead of tied
with them. The exact formula is now used. Results gives the main findings for
the 22 Klaeger compounds with no active kinase, and Supporting Information
Section S3 the full per-definition contrasts. Within one definition the
contrast is significant only for the Gini, small and not significant for the
entropy and the ratio, and reversed for the S-score. Under the alternative you
proposed, these compounds are more stable than active ones within the entropy
(7.0 against 13.5) and the Gini (6.7 against 13.6, p = 0.002, for the 16 of
them defined at two or more baselines). This is a group-level result, and the
paper no longer states it for each compound. The case for D1 therefore rests
on these scores being computed from sub-threshold values alone, not on their
instability. Methods notes that our ratio, like Bosc et al.'s ranking score,
scores every compound.

Your comment also led us to the ranking population. Active compounds are now
ranked among themselves, as D1 implies (Methods, "Ranking Comparisons and
Sensitivity Analysis"). Ranked among all 222 Klaeger compounds, skewness was
the strongest correlate of Gini instability (r = +0.26). Ranked among the
active compounds it is not significant (r = +0.07, p = 0.31), and the number of
active kinases correlates with Gini instability on both pKd datasets. Results
reports this, and Supporting Information Table S4 gives both rankings.


4. Define rho ~ -0.93, and address whether the floor can be regarded as
fixed.

It was the lowest Spearman correlation between rankings at any two points of
the sweep over [4.5, 6.5]. It has been replaced by the D2 statistic (Major 6).
Your reading is correct: swept over B = [x0, tau*], the gated entropy's gate
reverses about as many pairs as the entropy's baseline (Results, "Parameter
Sensitivity and Profile Associations"). Fixing a parameter is a protocol
decision, D2 reports each score's boundary sensitivity, and no boundary
parameter passes. With its gate fixed, the gated entropy's remaining parameter
reverses at most 0.72% of pairs. The Limitations state that in none of the
four datasets is x0 a measured detection limit, and describe the Anastassiadis
floor as an operational cutoff for weak inhibition, not a noise level. The
noise check now reapplies D1 to the noisy profiles, and Methods states its
model: on the affinity datasets it perturbs measured values and leaves
floor-coded pairs fixed, and on Anastassiadis it perturbs every entry.


5. State which parameter was varied in Figure 6, and show the floor sweep.

This figure is now Supporting Information Figure S2. Its caption names the
parameter each sweep varies: the entropy's baseline, the gated entropy's gate
with its emphasis baseline moved with it, and the emphasis baseline with the
gate fixed. Mean instability on Klaeger is 6.8 for the gate and 7.0 for the
entropy's baseline, against 0.4 for the emphasis baseline.


6. Restate D2 (formerly D3) so that the verdicts follow from its definition.

D2 is defined over an interval (Methods, "Evaluation Criteria and the Gated
Entropy"). A pair of compounds reverses in B if their score difference is
positive at some value of the parameter in B and negative at another, and D2
requires that at most 1% of the pairs of scored compounds reverse, on every
dataset and, for the ratio, for every k. A score that fixes a parameter is
assessed with it swept. For the ratio, the parameter is the level b in
R_b(k) = x(1) - max(x(k+1), b). The S-score's threshold reverses 8.4-13.0% of
pairs on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis and 21.8% on Metz, and the entropy's
and Gini's baselines 7.9-12.4% and 22.5-22.8%. The ratio's level reverses
3.0-13.7% on Klaeger, 2.1-9.7% on Anastassiadis, 0-2.6% on Davis and
0.04-1.03% on Metz. The verdicts are the same for any tolerance up to 13%
(Supporting Information Table S3). The S-score's percentages are exact. The
gated entropy's gate is now also evaluated at the observed values, where its
score jumps, which raises its upper value from 14.2% to 14.4%. Methods calls
the other percentages estimates. Methods presents D2 as a boundary-sensitivity
diagnostic: a score whose question changes with the threshold can reasonably
rank compounds differently at different thresholds, and fixing the boundary
defines the protocol without removing the sensitivity. D1, D3 and D4 are
judged by construction, and D2 empirically.


7. State the conclusion more directly; give vacuous satisfaction its own
symbol.

The Abstract states the conclusions directly: no score satisfies D2, so the
boundary must be fixed by protocol, and the gated entropy satisfies D3. The Conclusion draws the reporting
implications, and Results adds that D1 is a reporting rule any score can adopt
and that D3 is the criterion on which the scores differ. Table 4 uses only
satisfied and violated. The vacuous verdicts came from the former D2, which
has been removed.

You asked for evidence that the candidate improves on the entropy. On these
data it does not, and the paper says so. We added three simple comparators
(the active count, the entropy with its baseline at the activity threshold and
the S-score with its threshold at the floor) and the partition index at the
most strongly bound target. The gated entropy ranks compounds almost as the
entropy does (Spearman 0.994-0.998), and almost as the S-score with its
threshold at the floor does (0.998-0.9997), which also satisfies D1, D3 and
D4. It recovers rankings from subpanels no better than that S-score. What it
adds is narrow: the entropy meets D3 only when binding below the threshold
gets zero weight, whereas the gated entropy weights that binding and still
meets D3, with a proof (Methods, Proposition 1; Supporting Information Section
S4). The proof requires T >= 0.58 (tau* - x0). The previous widths on Metz and
Anastassiadis were below this bound, and T is now tau* - x0 on every dataset.
Supporting Information now proves that the bound cannot be lowered and
corrects a bound to a/m < 2.213. The paper also states the score's limits: it
is discontinuous at the floor, and strengthening a target already in the
profile can raise it.

The criteria do not single out one score. Several comparators and a range of
gated entropies satisfy D1, D3 and D4, and variants rank compounds almost
identically. Proposition 2 (Methods) shows that once the weights are fixed,
Shannon's conditions make the gated entropy unique. Results states this in one
paragraph, and the details are in Supporting Information Section S4.


8. Revise the Type 1 description.

Revised (Results; Supporting Information Section S3). For AZD-2014, AZD-8055
and AZD-8186, the target was not quantified for any compound, consistent with
poor expression or capture, and PI3Kbeta, a lipid kinase, is outside the
curated panel. For BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib, JAK2 and MET were
quantified for other compounds, so absence from the lysate does not explain
the result. The text states that these data cannot tell whether
compound-specific capture, competition or curve fitting does.


9. Benchmark definitional instability against platform variability.

Added (Results, "Analysis Choices Change Agreement Between Scores"; full
results in Supporting Information Section S6). On 46 compounds profiled in
both Davis and Klaeger and the 201 kinases both panels share, the same
definition's rankings correlate at 0.34-0.84 between datasets (mean 0.61),
against -0.08 to 1.00 between definitions within a dataset (mean 0.57).
Kinases are matched by gene symbol with renamed symbols mapped to current ones
(Davis's MLCK is MYLK3). Among the 40 compounds active in both, the means are
0.72 and 0.73. The comparison is descriptive.


10. Discuss how these properties limit the use of any single measure.

Added (Discussion, Limitations). No score considered uses target identity or
exposure, so a selectivity score is at best a summary of the binding profile,
to be weighed with target-specific liability knowledge and exposure modeling.
The clinical analysis that you noted is now limited to FAERS report
proportions, which are pulled by script, and it remains null. We removed the
label discontinuation rates. An audit of the hand extraction against the
label text found two dose-reduction rates recorded as discontinuation rates
(ceritinib and pazopanib), three values that we could not trace to the stored
label text, and one rate for a combination therapy.

FURTHER CORRECTIONS
====================================================================

All affected numbers, tables, figures and Supporting Information have been
updated.

1. Recovery. The analysis without the D1 gate, previously in Results, is in
Supporting Information Section S5. The earlier statement that weighting weak
binding explains fast recovery was wrong: the published entropy weights weak
binding and recovers slowly. Results now states that scores whose ranking
depends mostly on the strongest targets recover slowest, and that the
compounds lost on small panels are mostly those with few active targets. The
0.90 bar is described as a chosen descriptive benchmark, and the comparisons
between scores hold with bars of 0.80, 0.85 and 0.95.

2. Bosc et al. The earlier statement that their convention leaves ratio-type
scores undefined was wrong: they withheld ranks only for the S-score, and
their ranking score scores every compound. The Introduction notes that they
traced individual disagreements to profile shape, and the novelty claim is
narrowed accordingly. The CATDS score is not reimplemented because it scores
one designated target from Kinobeads curves, which only Klaeger provides.

3. Comparing studies. The recommendation on comparisons between studies
(Discussion, item 6) no longer advises restricting them to compounds on which
the definitions agree. It asks for a protocol fixed in advance and for the
compounds on which scores disagree to be reported.

4. References. The FDA count is updated (Roskoski, 2026), Miljkovic and
Bajorath is corrected to ACS Omega 3(3):3113-3119, Cheng et al. (2010) and
Uitdehaag et al. (2012) are added, and the Davis floor is attributed to
DeepDTA. Saifudeen et al. (2026) now gives its DOI, and author lists longer
than seven names give the first three and et al. The description of Khinchin's axioms was wrong and is removed. The
IUPAC citation is removed, because its definition of selectivity does not
concern a ratio of binding constants.


I hope the manuscript is now suitable for publication in Molecular
Informatics. Thank you again for a constructive review.
