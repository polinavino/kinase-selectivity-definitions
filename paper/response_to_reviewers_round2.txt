Dear Editor and Reviewer 1,

Thank you for a careful reading. The revised manuscript focuses on which
analysis choices change selectivity rankings. It now uses one reference
target per human protein kinase, compares published and log-affinity score
forms, and evaluates ranking recovery together with compound coverage.
The criteria and gated entropy are defined in Methods and evaluated in
Results. Supporting Information (Sections S1-S8) contains the full evidence.

The gated entropy now uses exponential weights. Its contribution is a
mathematical guarantee under the proposed criteria; it has no recovery
advantage over simpler scores on these data. We also reviewed the code
against Methods and reran every analysis. Corrections are reported below.

Reviewer comments are abridged. Section names and criterion numbers refer
to the revised manuscript.

REVIEWER 1 — MAJOR COMMENTS
====================================================================

1. State that entropy and Gini are re-operationalized; show the correlation
matrix under the published forms.

Methods ("Selectivity Scores and Their Implementations"; Table 2) distinguishes
the published and log-affinity forms and identifies the baseline as a
parameter we introduce. Results and Discussion now limit the grouping of
the S-score, entropy and Gini to the log-affinity forms among active
compounds. The separation from the ratio is weak on Anastassiadis.

Table 3 and Supporting Information Table S2 compare the published entropy
and the native percent-inhibition Gini where their required readouts are
available. Figure 1 compares association-constant and baseline-subtracted
weights for entropy and Gini. Methods and its caption credit the
association-constant Gini variant to Uitdehaag and Zaman (2011), distinguishing
it from Graczyk's percent-inhibition Gini.
For the published entropy, we report both zero weight for floor-coded
pairs and their retention at the floor. Under both treatments, it correlates
more with the ratio than with the S-score or Gini on Davis, Klaeger and Metz.
Its correlation with the log-affinity entropy is 0.74 on Davis and 0.79 on
Klaeger with zero weight, but 0.37 and -0.31 when pairs stay at the floor.
The native percent-inhibition Gini on Anastassiadis correlates with the
entropy at 0.63 and with the S-score and ratio at 0.34 and 0.33.

Figure 3 also compares recovery: the published entropy needs 300, 200 and
150 targets on Davis, Klaeger and Metz to exceed mean Spearman 0.90, against
20, 50 and 65 for the log-affinity entropy. The floor treatment does not
change these sizes. On Klaeger, retaining the 194 measured affinities weaker
than 10 uM barely changes the published entropy's ranking (Spearman 0.9996
among active compounds; Supporting Information Section S2).


2. Report what the Klaeger filter removes and the sensitivity to relaxing it;
restate the Metz threshold or report its coverage distribution.

Klaeger (Methods; Supporting Information Section S1). The filter removes 793
of 5,916 rows (13.4%). Restoring low-confidence fits changes 0.77% of matrix
entries. Correlations change by at most 0.08 among all compounds and about
0.006 among the 200 active under both filters. Two more compounds become
eligible (202 total), and every recovery p* is unchanged.

The curated panel contains 217 single-gene protein kinases. It excludes
non-kinase proteins, lipid kinases, protein groups and Q6ZSR9, which lacks an
annotated kinase domain. Methods distinguishes three kinds of floor-coded
pairs: no detected binding, low-confidence fits and measured affinities
weaker than 10 uM. Of the 405 low-confidence fits on curated targets, 375 have
fitted pKd above 5. Assigning them the floor is therefore an analysis
convention, not evidence of weak binding. The repository lists each target
and why it was kept or removed.

Metz (Methods; Supporting Information Section S1). The inclusion threshold
is at least 50 of 172 kinases. Coverage among the 704 retained compounds is
29.1-83.1% (median 37.2%). Results presents Metz as a missing-measurement case:
coverage correlates with median rank at 0.98 for entropy, 0.95 for Gini,
0.76 for the S-score and 0.11 for the ratio.


3. State the convention for undefined scores, and report the per-definition
contrast under an alternative.

Methods ("Conventions"; Table 2) states that undefined entropy and Gini values
are set to zero, ranking these compounds at the top for entropy and bottom
for Gini. We removed an added 1e-10 inside the logarithm, which had placed
undefined entropy just below single-target profiles rather than tied with them.

Results and Supporting Information Section S3 give the contrasts for the
22 Klaeger compounds with no active kinase. Within a definition, their
instability is significantly higher only for Gini; the S-score contrast is
reversed. Under your proposed alternative, using only configurations with
defined scores, the 16 compounds defined at two or more baselines are more
stable than active compounds under entropy (7.0 versus 13.5) and Gini
(6.7 versus 13.6; both p = 0.002).
These are group comparisons, not statements about every compound. The case
for D1 now rests on eligibility to judge selectivity, rather than instability.
The ratio is defined for every compound.

Active compounds are now ranked among themselves. This also changes the
profile associations: the Gini-skewness correlation falls from 0.26 among all
222 Klaeger compounds to 0.07 among active compounds (p = 0.31). Supporting
Information Table S4 reports both ranking populations.


4. Define rho ~ -0.93, and address whether the floor can be regarded as
fixed.

This was the lowest Spearman correlation between rankings at two points of
the [4.5, 6.5] sweep. We replaced it with the share of compound pairs whose
order reverses over B = [x0, tau*] (Major 6). Over this interval, the gated
entropy's gate reverses about as many pairs as the entropy's baseline.
Fixing the boundary is a protocol choice; it does not remove sensitivity.

Limitations states that no dataset's floor is a measured detection limit.
The Anastassiadis floor is a chosen cutoff for weak inhibition. Exponential
weights remove the gated entropy's separate emphasis baseline because any
reference value cancels. The noise analysis now reapplies D1 after adding
noise. Methods specifies that floor-coded affinity pairs stay fixed, whereas
every Anastassiadis entry is perturbed.


5. State which parameter was varied in Figure 6, and show the floor sweep.

Supporting Information Figure S2 now compares the entropy's baseline sweep
with the gated entropy's gate sweep. The caption names both parameters.
Mean rank instability on Klaeger is 7.0 and 6.7, respectively.


6. Restate D2 (formerly D3) so that the verdicts follow from its definition.

D2 is now boundary locality: a score may change as its boundary moves only
if the set of targets above the boundary changes. All four criteria are
judged from the score definitions. The S-score, active count and gated
entropy satisfy D2; the baseline-subtracted entropy, Gini and ratio violate
it (Table 4).

The former reversal-based criterion is retained as a sensitivity measure.
Methods ("Evaluation Criteria") defines a reversal as a score difference
that is positive at one boundary value in B and negative at another.
Supporting Information Table S3 reports the share of D1-eligible pairs that
reverse on every dataset, including every k for the ratio. Its swept
parameter is b in R_b(k) = x(1) - max(x(k+1), b).

The S-score counts are exact. Entropy, Gini and ratio counts use a 201-point
grid; the gated entropy is also evaluated at and just below each observed
value, where its score jumps. No score stays within 1% reversals on every
dataset. We therefore no longer use that bound as a criterion. Rankings can
reasonably differ between thresholds that address different questions.


7. State the conclusion more directly; give vacuous satisfaction its own
symbol.

The Abstract now states the empirical findings directly: score agreement
depends on implementation and eligibility, and rank agreement on small
panels can remain high while compounds with few active targets become
unscorable. D1 is a reporting rule any score can adopt. The former D2, which
produced the vacuous verdicts, is removed; Table 4 now uses satisfied and
violated only.

The gated entropy has no recovery advantage over simpler scores on these
data. We added the active count, entropy with its baseline at the activity
threshold, the S-score with its threshold at the floor, and the partition
index at the strongest target. The S-score at the floor also meets all four
criteria. On Davis, Klaeger, Anastassiadis and Metz, it reaches p* at
20, 50, 30 and 65 targets, against 50, 65, 30 and 80 for the gated entropy.
Doubling the gated entropy's scale gives the same p* as that S-score, with
nearly identical rankings (Spearman 0.9968-0.9996; Results, "Scale of the
Gated Entropy and Recovery").

The gated entropy combines the four criteria with affinity weighting,
including weak binding above the floor. Proposition 2 characterizes it
within a stated class of weights and grouping assumptions. Proposition 1
proves that D3 holds exactly when T >= tau* - x0. We choose the smallest
allowed scale to make the score most sensitive to affinity differences. The
published entropy has T = 1/ln 10, below this bound, and violates D3.
The previous softplus weights met D3 but were not determined by the criteria.
The proofs and variants are in Supporting Information Section S4.

D4 is new: on a fixed panel, equally strong binding to more targets must not
increase apparent selectivity. The criteria do not select one score.
The manuscript also states the gated
entropy's limits: it jumps at the floor, strengthening an existing target
can raise its score, and changing the activity threshold changes both
eligibility and, through the default scale, rankings.


8. Revise the Type 1 description.

Results and Supporting Information Section S3 now distinguish missing targets
from compound-specific non-detection. For AZD-2014, AZD-8055 and AZD-8186, the
intended target was not quantified for any compound or was outside the
curated panel. For BMS-911543, SGX-523 and volitinib, JAK2 and MET were
quantified for other compounds. These data cannot distinguish effects of
compound-specific capture, competition and curve fitting.


9. Benchmark definitional instability against platform variability.

Added in Results ("Analysis Choices Change Agreement Between Scores") and
Supporting Information Section S6. Among 46 shared compounds and 201 shared
kinases, rankings correlate at 0.34-0.84 between Davis and Klaeger for the
same definition (mean 0.61), against -0.08 to 1.00 between definitions within
a dataset (mean 0.57). Among the 40 compounds active in both, the means are
0.72 and 0.73. Kinases are matched by gene symbol, with renamed symbols
updated. This is a descriptive comparison.

A separate exploratory diagnostic uses the full panels and original ranking
populations. Weak associations with rank displacement (|r| <= 0.18) concern
instability aggregated over all definitions. They do not show that each
definition's sensitivity is uninformative across datasets. Within-definition
instability gives r = 0.327 for the Davis S-score (nominal p = 0.027;
Bonferroni-adjusted p = 0.212). None of the eight tests remains significant
after correction (Supporting Information Section S6).


10. Discuss how these properties limit the use of any single measure.

Discussion ("Limitations") now explains that these scores use neither target
identity nor exposure. They summarize binding profiles and must be considered
with target-specific liabilities and exposure estimates.

No correlation with the scripted FAERS report proportions is statistically
significant. We removed the hand-extracted
label discontinuation rates after finding two dose-reduction rates recorded
as discontinuation rates, three untraceable values and one combination-therapy
rate. The FAERS results do not establish the absence of clinically relevant
associations.
Supporting Information Section S7 now identifies the frozen snapshot used
for the correlations, distinguishes it from the later count file, and states
the query scope and the unrecorded exact retrieval date.


11. Introduce the properties and candidate in Methods, evaluate them in
Results, and consolidate the qualifications.

Methods now defines the criteria ("Evaluation Criteria") and score ("The
Gated Entropy"). Results reports parameter sensitivity and profile associations,
then evaluates the criteria and score in "Criteria and the Gated Entropy".
Qualifications accompany the findings they concern, with full evidence in
Supporting Information. Repeated explanations have been removed.

FURTHER CORRECTIONS
====================================================================

Affected numbers, tables, figures and Supporting Information are updated.

1. Recovery. The analysis without D1 is now in Supporting Information
Section S5. Weighting weak binding does not explain fast recovery: the
published entropy weights it but recovers slowly. The 0.90 bar is
a chosen descriptive benchmark; comparisons were also checked at 0.80,
0.85 and 0.95.

2. Bosc et al. Their ranking score scores every compound; only their S-score
withholds ranks. The Introduction now credits their explanations of
individual score disagreements and narrows our novelty claim. CATDS is not
reimplemented across datasets because only Klaeger supplies the required
Kinobeads curves.

3. Comparing studies. Discussion item 6 now asks for a comparison protocol
fixed in advance and disclosure of score disagreements. It no longer advises
restricting comparisons to compounds on which definitions agree.

4. References. We updated the FDA count (Roskoski, 2026), corrected the
Miljkovic and Bajorath citation, added Cheng et al. (2010) and Uitdehaag et
al. (2012), attributed the Davis floor to DeepDTA, and added Saifudeen et
al.'s DOI. We corrected the author-list format and Zaman's given name (Guido).
The incorrect account of Khinchin's axioms and the inapplicable IUPAC
selectivity definition are removed.

5. Other corrections. The ratio's maximum reversal rate is 3.0% at k = 1 and
13.7% at k = 5. We fixed and regression-tested an Anastassiadis cache-rebuild
error; the committed 291-target analysis was unaffected. Figures and tables
were regenerated, and keywords checked against Wiley's catalogue.
