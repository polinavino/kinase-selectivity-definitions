Dear Editor and Reviewer 1,

Thank you for a second careful reading. The reviewer's requests have changed
the paper in four ways:

  (1) The motivated properties and the candidate measure are now defined in
      Methods ("Motivated Properties", "A Candidate Measure"), with a formal
      statement of each property and how it is assessed, and they are
      evaluated in Results ("Existing Definitions Against the Properties",
      "The Candidate Measure", "Why the Smooth Hinge Matters"). Variants of
      the candidate moved to Supporting Information. Repeated results and
      layered qualifications have been consolidated.

  (2) Every robustness check the reviewer asked for has been added: the
      correlation matrices under the published forms of the entropy and Gini,
      the Klaeger filter sensitivity, the Metz coverage distribution, the
      convention for undefined scores with the requested alternative, the
      placeholder values for undefined profile features, and a comparison of
      platform and definition effects on the compounds shared by Davis and
      Klaeger.

  (3) D3 has been restated so that each verdict follows from its definition.
      The properties table (now Table 3) gives vacuous satisfaction its own
      symbol and states how each row was determined.

  (4) A Supporting Information file (Tables S1-S4 and Section S5) now holds
      the results that were previously only in the code repository.

While preparing this revision I also had the code checked against Methods and
re-ran every analysis. This found errors that change some reported numbers,
including the candidate's panel-size result. They are listed under "Further
corrections" at the end.

A point-by-point response follows. Reviewer text is quoted in abridged form,
and the location of each change is given by section name.

REVIEWER 1 — MAJOR COMMENTS
====================================================================

1. "I recommend more clearly stating that the analysis concerns
re-operationalized forms of entropy and Gini, and to show the correlation
matrix under the published forms where it is computable."

The distinction is now stated wherever the clustering result is claimed: in
the Abstract ("with entropy and Gini computed on a common log-affinity
scale"), in contribution (i) of the Introduction, at the start of the
Discussion, and in Limitations. Methods ("Faithfulness to the original
definitions") states that every entropy and Gini result in the paper concerns
the baseline-subtracted form.

The requested matrices are in Supporting Information Table S3 and summarized
in Methods. They use the association-constant entropy on Davis (complete
matrix) and on Klaeger and Metz (measured entries only, per compound), and the
native percent-inhibition Gini on Anastassiadis. Under the published entropy
the two-family pattern does not hold. On Klaeger and Metz the
association-constant entropy correlates more strongly with the ratio
(r = 0.79 and 0.76) than with the S-score (0.57 and 0.29) or the Gini (0.69
and 0.39). On Davis it decouples from both families (0.42 with the S-score
and with the Gini, 0.34 with the ratio). The native Gini on Anastassiadis
correlates with the entropy (0.63) and weakly, about equally, with the S-score
(0.35) and the ratio (0.31). The Introduction now says that the two-family
grouping holds for the common-scale forms, and that under the
association-constant form the entropy does not cluster with the S-score and
Gini on any of the three affinity datasets.

Figure 1's Klaeger entropy value is now -0.34 (previously -0.32), after the
correction described in Further corrections, item 1.


2. "The author should report how many entries the filter removed, and the
sensitivity of Table 1 and the subsampling results to relaxing this filter.
[...] Please restate the [Metz] threshold or report the coverage distribution
across the retained Metz compounds."

Klaeger. Methods ("Datasets") now reports that the high-confidence filter
removes 793 of the 5,916 drug-target rows (13.4%), one of which has no
numeric Kd. Of the 792 numeric low-confidence pairs, 561 fall within the
222 x 343 high-confidence matrix. The other 231 involve 7 compounds and 177
kinases with no high-confidence measurement. Adding the 561 pairs changes
0.74% of the matrix, shifts every Klaeger cell of Table 1 by at most 0.05, and
leaves every Klaeger panel-size p* unchanged. Retaining all 792 pairs also
adds the 7 compounds and 177 kinases, which changes the panel itself. It
shifts the Klaeger cells of Table 1 by up to 0.12 (S-score against ratio,
0.22 to 0.34) and leaves the two-family structure intact
(klaeger_filter_sensitivity.py).

Metz. Both requests are met. The phrase "keeping profiles in which most
entries reflect a real measurement" has been removed. The threshold is now
stated as at least 50 of the 172 kinases (>= 29% coverage), chosen to exclude
the most sparsely profiled compounds while keeping enough compounds for the
panel-size and instability analyses. Methods reports the coverage
distribution of the 704 retained compounds: 29.1% to 83.1% (mean 40.9%,
median 37.2%, 75th percentile 47.1%), with 23 compounds at exactly 50 kinases
and 170 at 55 or fewer (metz_coverage.py).

This comment also led me to test what the imputation does to the Metz
rankings. Nearly every measured Metz value lies above the floor, so a
compound measured against more kinases looks less selective. The Spearman
correlation between a compound's coverage and its median rank is 0.98 for the
entropy, 0.95 for the Gini, 0.76 for the S-score and 0.11 for the ratio
(Methods). Results and Limitations now qualify the Metz findings accordingly.
I also removed a claim that rested on Metz. The previous summary of the
candidate's panel-size result, "from as little as a tenth to about a half of
a panel", took its upper end from Metz (80 of 172 kinases). The Abstract,
Introduction and Conclusion now say "from as little as a tenth of a panel".
The Metz value is still reported, with this caveat, in Results ("The
Candidate Measure").


3. "The convention by which these undefined scores were ranked across the
parameter sweep is not stated. Please state the convention and report the
per-definition contrast under an alternative, such as rank sigma computed
only over configurations in which score is defined."

The convention is stated in Methods ("Rank Stability Analysis"). An undefined
entropy or Gini is set to 0, which ranks the compound as maximally selective
under the negated entropy (tied with single-target profiles) and as minimally
selective under the Gini. The ratio substitutes the assay floor for a missing
off-target (pKd 5.0 on Davis and Klaeger, pKi 4.0 on Metz, 0% inhibition on
Anastassiadis), and the S-score is defined for every compound. Methods
("Ratio") states that this fallback departs from Bosc et al.'s convention of
leaving such ratio scores undefined, and that R(k)'s D1 violation comes from
this implementation choice.

The per-definition contrast is reported in Results ("Existing Definitions
Against the Properties", D1). Under the stated convention, the pooled Klaeger
contrast (sigma = 74.0 for zero-active compounds against 31.2 for active ones)
is significant within a single definition only for the Gini (86.1 against
15.9). It is small and not significant for the entropy (14.1 against 11.9,
p = 0.25) and the ratio (24.0 against 20.7, p = 0.08), and it reverses for the
S-score (18.5 against 24.3, p = 0.07). Under the alternative the reviewer
proposed (sigma computed only over the configurations in which a compound's
score is defined, with compounds at each baseline ranked only among those
defined there), zero-active compounds are more stable than active ones
(entropy 9.8 against 12.8, p = 0.046; Gini 9.5 against 13.0, p = 0.028;
undefined_score_convention.py). This uses 14 of the 16 compounds, since the
other two are defined at fewer than two baselines. The D1 paragraph therefore
rests the case for D1 on the arbitrariness of these scores, which D1 removes.
The same per-definition breakdown for Anastassiadis and Metz is given in
Results ("The Two-Family Structure Replicates Across Assay Technologies").


4. "The author should define the quantity reported as rho ~ -0.93 and address
whether the floor can be regarded as fixed across the four datasets."

The quantity was the lowest Spearman correlation between the rankings at any
two points of the sweep over [4.5, 6.5]. It was negative because the rankings
at the two ends of that range are close to inverted. It has been replaced by
the D3 statistic itself (comment 6): the share of pairs of scored compounds
whose order reverses somewhere in B = [x0, tau*]. Sweeping the candidate's
gate x0 over B reverses 8.5-22.5% of pairs across the four datasets, against
8.8-22.6% for the entropy's baseline (Results, "The Candidate Measure", and
Supporting Information Table S4). The reviewer's reading is therefore correct.
The candidate is as sensitive to the placement of its activity boundary as the
entropy is to its baseline, and its D3 verdict holds only with that boundary
fixed. Table 3 marks this verdict with its own symbol.

On whether the floor can be regarded as fixed, Limitations now states that in
none of the four datasets is x0 a measured detection limit. It is the value
assigned to non-detected pairs on Davis and Klaeger (pKd 5.0), the lowest
reported value on Metz (pKi 4.0), and a 20% inhibition noise level that I set
on Anastassiadis. Fixing x0 removes the analyst's choice only to the extent
that each dataset's encoding of non-detection is accepted as given. Where that
encoding is itself uncertain, the candidate's standing on D3 is no better than
the entropy's.


5. "Please state which parameter was varied for the candidate measure and
present the floor sweep as the equivalent comparison."

The right panel of Figure 6 now shows three sweeps over the same interval,
B = [5.0, 6.0] on Klaeger: the entropy's baseline, the candidate's gate x0
with its emphasis baseline pinned to x0 at each point (the equivalent
comparison), and the candidate's emphasis baseline with x0 fixed at the
floor. The caption states which parameter each series varies. The 17-fold
annotation has been removed. The panel reports the mean instability of each
sweep: 7.2 for the candidate's gate and 7.5 for the entropy's baseline,
against 0.4 for the emphasis baseline with the gate fixed. Results states
that the candidate's D3 standing comes entirely from declaring x0 fixed.


6. "The author should restate D3 so that the verdicts follow from its
definition and distinguish empirical verdicts in the properties table from
those derived from the functional form."

D3 is now defined in Methods ("Motivated Properties") as a condition on pairs
of compounds. Let B = [x0, tau*], the interval between the assay's detection
floor and the activity threshold. A pair of compounds reverses in B if the
difference of their scores is positive at some value of the analyst's
parameter in B and negative at another. D3 requires that at most a fraction
epsilon of the pairs of scored compounds reverse in B. Under the local
reading the reviewer identified, any pair with distinct scores satisfied the
condition. Reversal over a whole interval removes that reading.

The verdicts follow from this definition by computing the reversal fraction
on each dataset (d3_pair_reversals.py). The S-score, entropy and Gini reverse
8-23% of pairs on every dataset, and the candidate's emphasis baseline
reverses at most 0.72%. We use epsilon = 1%, and every verdict is the same for
any epsilon from 1% to 7%. The ratio has no parameter on the affinity scale
and satisfies D3 vacuously.

The caption of Table 3 now states how each row was determined: D3
empirically on all four datasets, D1 from each measure's reporting rule, and
D2 and D4 from each measure's functional form. The statement of each property
in Methods also says how it is assessed.


7. "The conclusion that none of the four satisfies all four properties
therefore depends largely on the absence of a reliability gate [...] Please
state the conclusion more directly, and I would suggest giving vacuous
satisfaction its own symbol in Table 2."

The conclusion is now stated directly in the Abstract ("None of the four
assessed satisfies all of them. The one property all four violate is a
reliability condition for compounds without binding above threshold."), in
contribution (iii) of the Introduction, and at the start of "Existing
Definitions Against the Properties". That section adds that any of the four
definitions could satisfy D1 by withholding or flagging such scores.

With D3 restated (comment 6), D1 is no longer the entropy's only violation.
The entropy fails D3 (8.8-22.6% of pairs reverse over B). It also fails D4 by
its functional form. Its hard cutoff gives a kinase just above the baseline an
arbitrarily small weight, so adding a kinase below the activity threshold can
raise its score. Results ("Why the Smooth Hinge Matters") gives a worked
example, and on Anastassiadis the violation occurs in the data for 34% of the
compounds with an active kinase at a 0% baseline. The candidate's D4 is now
proved (Methods, "A Candidate Measure", Proposition).

The reviewer is right that the candidate and the entropy rank these datasets
almost identically (Spearman 0.994-0.999 on the compounds both score). The
Introduction, Results and Conclusion say this, and the paper presents the
candidate as a demonstration that the four properties can be met together.
The properties themselves are the main contribution.

In Table 3, a check mark in parentheses now denotes vacuous satisfaction
(D2 for the S-score, entropy, Gini and candidate, and D3 for the ratio), and a
check mark with a dagger denotes the candidate's D3, which holds only with the
activity boundary fixed at the floor.


8. "Their zero-active status more plausibly reflects poor expression or
capture of these targets. Please revise the Type 1 description accordingly."

Revised (Results, "Three Mechanistically Distinct Sources of Instability",
Type 1). The text no longer says these compounds lack meaningful binding. It
names their established targets (mTOR, JAK2, VEGFR2, JAK1 and HER2) and states
that none appears as a high-confidence sub-micromolar hit in the Klaeger
screen. I also tested the reviewer's explanation against the Kinobeads table.
For three of the five compounds (AZD-8055, vatalanib and ARRY-380), the target
was not quantified for any of the 229 compounds with a fitted target, which is
consistent with poor expression or capture. For the other two the explanation
does not hold. JAK2 and JAK1 were quantified for 7 and 12 other compounds (up
to pKd 8.3), yet no binding was detected for BMS-911543 or filgotinib
(claim_checks.py). The Discussion now describes Type 1 compounds as having no
target captured above the activity threshold on the profiled panel.


9. "The manuscript would benefit from benchmarking definitional instability
against the ranking variability introduced by assay platform."

Added (Limitations). Matching PubChem CIDs against Klaeger's drug names
resolves 46 compounds profiled in both Davis and Klaeger
(davis_klaeger_overlap.py). On this fixed set, the platform effect (same
definition, Davis rank against Klaeger rank) is on average larger than the
definition effect: Spearman r = -0.06 to 0.69 across the four definitions
(mean 0.44), against 0.14 to 1.00 for cross-definition comparisons within a
platform (mean 0.60). The ratio is the extreme case. Its Davis and Klaeger
rankings of the same compounds are uncorrelated (r = -0.06, p = 0.67). Practical
Recommendations adds that within-dataset rank instability does not predict
how far a compound's rank moves between platforms (|r| <= 0.25).


10. "The author should discuss how these properties limit the use of any
single selectivity measure in the drug discovery decisions the Discussion
describes."

Added (Limitations). All four definitions and the candidate are symmetric
functions of the binding profile, so two compounds with identical affinity
distributions receive identical scores whether their off-targets are inert or
known safety-relevant kinases. None of the measures incorporates target
identity or exposure, and a measured affinity alone does not establish
whether a target is occupied at a tolerated dose. The paragraph concludes
that a selectivity score is at best a summary of binding breadth, and that
go/no-go decisions should weigh it alongside target-specific liability
knowledge and exposure modeling.


11. "The author should introduce the properties and candidate measure in
Methods, report their evaluation in Results, and consolidate the
qualifications so that the principal findings are stated plainly."

Done. Methods now contains "Motivated Properties" (the formal statement,
motivation and method of assessment of each property) and "A Candidate
Measure" (the definition, the parameter choices, and the D4 proposition with
its proof). Results evaluates them in "Existing Definitions Against the
Properties" (Table 3), "The Candidate Measure", and "Why the Smooth Hinge
Matters". The last of these opens with its purpose: to show that the
candidate and the entropy differ in principle, although they rank these
datasets almost identically. The variants of the candidate (quantile floor,
Renyi order, smoothing width) moved to Supporting Information Section S5. I
removed results repeated across sections and moved each qualification next to
the finding it qualifies.

FURTHER CORRECTIONS
====================================================================

These were found by re-running every analysis after a review of the code
against Methods. All affected numbers, tables, figures and Supporting
Information have been updated.

1. Undefined entropy scores were not tied with single-target profiles. The
code computed the entropy with a 1e-10 term inside the logarithm, which ranked
compounds with an undefined entropy just below single-target profiles. Methods
states that they are tied. With the exact formula:
   - the Klaeger panel-size p* of the entropy and the candidate falls from
     110 to 80 kinases;
   - three Klaeger cells of Table 1 change (S-score-entropy 0.901 to 0.910,
     entropy-Gini 0.866 to 0.840, entropy-ratio 0.483 to 0.455), as does the
     Klaeger entropy column of Table 2 (largest change: number of active
     kinases, -0.409 to -0.381);
   - the per-definition D1 contrast for the entropy is no longer significant
     (Major comment 3);
   - Figure 1's Klaeger entropy value moves from -0.32 to -0.34.
Davis and Metz are unaffected.

2. Renyi order and panel size. The previous version reported that Renyi
orders below 1 recover the Klaeger ranking faster than the Shannon order
(p* = 80 against 110) and orders 2 and 3 more slowly (290). This came from
scoring compounds with no gated weight on a subpanel differently at each
order. Scored as in the main analysis, every order reaches p* = 80 on Klaeger
and Metz, and the claim has been removed (Supporting Information, Section S5).

3. Panel size on all four datasets. The previous version ran the subsampling
analysis on Klaeger and Metz only. It now covers all four datasets (Figures 5
and 7, Table S1). The candidate reaches p* = 50 kinases on Davis and
Anastassiadis and 80 on Klaeger and Metz. Together with the Metz caveat
(Major comment 2), this changes the summary in the Abstract from "about a
third of a panel" to "as little as a tenth of a panel".

4. Gated panel size. After item 1, applying the D1 gate leaves the
candidate's and the entropy's p* unchanged on every dataset. The previous
version attributed part of the candidate's convergence to the gate. The gate
does bring the Gini to the entropy's p*, by removing compounds whose Gini is
undefined on a subpanel, which the convention ranks least selective
(Results, "The Candidate Measure").

5. Smoothing width. The proof of D4 (Major comment 7) requires
T >= 0.58 (tau* - x0). The previous widths on Metz (T = 1 pKi unit) and
Anastassiadis (T = 10 percentage points) were below this bound, and
constructed profiles violate D4 there. T is now tau* - x0 on every dataset.
This leaves Davis and Klaeger unchanged and changes the Metz and Anastassiadis
rankings negligibly (Spearman 0.999).


I believe these changes address each of the reviewer's comments, and I hope
the manuscript is now suitable for publication in Molecular Informatics.
Thank you again for the constructive review.
