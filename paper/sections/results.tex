\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Analysis Choices Change Agreement Between Scores}
\label{sec:results:families}

With the entropy and Gini in their log-affinity forms, and compounds with an
active kinase ranked among themselves, the S-score, entropy and Gini agree more
with one another than with the ratio (Table~\ref{tab:correlations}). On Davis,
where every compound has an active kinase, they correlate at
$0.963$--$0.999$, and the ratio with each at $0.51$--$0.56$. Among the 200
Klaeger compounds with an active kinase, they correlate at $0.89$--$0.997$,
and the ratio with each at $0.38$--$0.64$. With log-affinity weights, the entropy
and Gini, like the S-score, summarize how activity is spread over the panel,
while the ratio compares the primary target with one off-target.

\paragraph{Weighting.} The grouping of the S-score, entropy and Gini holds only
for the log-affinity forms of the entropy and Gini. The published entropy, with
association-constant weights, correlates more with the ratio than with the
S-score or Gini on Davis, Klaeger and Metz, under both treatments of
floor-coded pairs (Table~\ref{tab:correlations} and Supporting Information,
Table~S2). On Anastassiadis, the native percent-inhibition Gini correlates with
the entropy at $0.63$, and with the S-score and the ratio at only $0.34$ and
$0.33$.

How far the published entropy is from the S-score and Gini depends on the
treatment of floor-coded pairs. They make up $71\%$ of the Davis matrix, and
the published entropy's correlation with the S-score there is $0.34$ with them
at the floor and $0.74$ with them given zero weight. The published and
log-affinity entropies correlate at $0.37$ (Davis) and $-0.31$ (Klaeger) with
floor-coded pairs at the floor, and at $0.74$ and $0.79$ with them given zero
weight (Figure~\ref{fig:fidelity}). The negative Klaeger value comes from the
floor-coded pairs, with which the published entropy also fails D4
(``Criteria and the Gated Entropy''). Using every measured Klaeger affinity at its measured value,
including the 194 weaker than the floor, changes the published entropy's
ranking little (Spearman $0.9996$ with the floor-excluded ranking among active
compounds) and changes its correlations and recovery by at most $0.02$
(Supporting Information, Section~S2).

\paragraph{Compounds ranked.} Twenty-two Klaeger compounds have no target above
the activity threshold. With them included, the Gini correlates more with the
ratio ($0.66$) than with the S-score ($0.51$), although the difference is not
significant (bootstrap $95\%$ interval of the difference $[-0.35, 0.06]$,
against $[0.16, 0.36]$ among active compounds). Each definition scores these
compounds by its own convention. The
S-score and entropy rank these 22 compounds among the most selective (median
ranks 24 and 20 of 222) and the ratio near the bottom (182). 
The Gini ranks them near the top
where its score is defined and near the bottom where it is not (mean ranks 32
and 212). Their aggregated rank instability
is $\sigma = 77.9$, against $32.2$ for active compounds ($p < 0.001$,
Mann--Whitney $U$ test). 
Within a single definition's sweep, their rank instability is significantly
higher than that of active compounds only for the Gini ($65.0$ against
$16.3$). The difference reverses for the S-score ($19.0$ against
$25.7$, $p = 0.01$), whose score is $0$ for all of these compounds at
thresholds of 6 or above. Sixteen of the 22 bind some kinase above the floor.
Computed only over the configurations where their scores are defined, the
mean instability of these 16 is lower than that of the active compounds within
the entropy and the Gini ($7.0$ against $13.5$, and $6.7$ against $13.6$, both with
$p = 0.002$). The
other six show no binding to any curated target, although all are established
inhibitors. For three of them, the target was quantified for other compounds
(Supporting Information, Section~S3). Whether a compound is
eligible for a selectivity score therefore depends on the panel and the
threshold. Supporting Information (Section~S3) gives the per-definition
contrasts.

\paragraph{Agreement within each dataset.} Among compounds with an active kinase, the
S-score, entropy and Gini correlate at $0.81$--$0.999$ on all four datasets,
and the ratio less with each (Supporting Information, Figure~S1). On
Anastassiadis, the separation is weak: the ratio correlates with the other three
at $0.79$--$0.84$, against $0.87$--$0.999$ among them. With all compounds
included, this falls to $0.48$--$0.55$, because of the 45 compounds with no
active kinase. On Metz, the ratio correlates with the other three at only
$0.15$--$0.20$, because the other three largely follow how many kinases each
compound was tested against. Untested pairs are coded at the floor, and nearly
every tested value ($99.9\%$) lies above it. A compound tested against more
kinases therefore has more kinases with positive weight and looks less
selective under the S-score, entropy and Gini, while the ratio, which uses only
the two highest values, is barely affected. The
Spearman correlation between a compound's coverage and its median rank (rank 1
= most selective) is $0.98$ for the entropy, $0.95$ for the Gini, $0.76$ for
the S-score and $0.11$ for the ratio. Compounds measured against more kinases
are also active against a larger share of those measured, but that association
is much weaker ($r = 0.33$). The Metz correlations therefore show how
coding untested pairs at the floor affects rankings.

\paragraph{Target selection and filtering.} On the released Davis panel, the
ratio agrees less with the other definitions than on the curated panel
($0.34$--$0.46$ against $0.51$--$0.56$, Supporting Information,
Section~S1). Restoring the low-confidence Klaeger pairs shifts the
all-compound Klaeger correlations by at most $0.08$ and the active-compound
correlations by at most $0.01$. It leaves every $p^*$ of the recovery
analysis unchanged (Supporting Information, Section~S1).

\paragraph{Effects of scoring against a different dataset versus a different measure.}
On the 46 compounds profiled in both
Davis and Klaeger, the same definition's Davis and Klaeger rankings correlate,
on the 201 kinases both panels share, at $0.34$--$0.84$ (mean $0.61$), and
different definitions' rankings within one dataset at $-0.08$--$1.00$ (mean
$0.57$). Among the 40 compounds active in both datasets, the means are $0.72$
and $0.73$, and the Gini's cross-dataset correlation rises from $0.45$ to
$0.84$. On these compounds, the choice of definition moves rankings about as
much as the change of dataset. The 46 compounds are active against $15.0\%$
of the Davis panel and $3.9\%$ of the Klaeger panel on average, and without
restricting the panels to their shared kinases the cross-dataset correlations
are lower (mean $0.53$).

\subsection{Parameter Sensitivity and Profile Associations}
\label{sec:results:instability}

Moving each definition's activity boundary across $B$ reorders a substantial
share of compound pairs. The S-score's threshold reverses $8.4$--$13.0\%$ of
pairs of scored compounds on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis, and $21.8\%$ on
Metz. The entropy's and Gini's baselines reverse $7.9$--$12.4\%$ on the same three
datasets, and $22.5$--$22.8\%$ on Metz. The ratio's level $b$, which replaces any
off-target value below $b$, measured or not, reverses $3.0$--$13.7\%$ on
Klaeger and $2.1$--$9.7\%$ on Anastassiadis, rising with $k$, $0$--$2.6\%$ on
Davis and $0.04$--$1.03\%$ on Metz. Sweeping the gated entropy's gate, with $T$ fixed at $\tau^* - x_0$, reverses
$9.2$--$14.6\%$ of pairs on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis, and $23.8\%$ on
Metz. On Klaeger, the per-compound instability under the gate sweep is close
to that under the entropy's baseline sweep (mean rank $\sigma = 6.7$ and
$7.0$, Supporting Information, Figure~S2).
Supporting Information (Table~S3) gives every value.

\paragraph{Reference off-target.} When an active compound's two best-bound
kinases have near-identical affinities, the ratio depends heavily on $k$.
$R(1)$ compares the first kinase with the second, treating the second as an
off-target (a kinase the compound is not meant to bind). For near-tied kinases
this difference is close to zero, so the compound scores as non-selective.
$R(2)$ compares the first kinase with the third, treating the second as a
co-target (a second intended target), so the same compound can score as highly
selective. On the 22 Klaeger
compounds with an active kinase and a top$_1$--top$_2$ gap below $0.05$, mean
$R(1) = 0.02$ against $R(2) = 0.50$. Which reference is appropriate depends on
whether the second kinase is an intended target, which the profile does not
show. On Davis, fedratinib, a JAK2 inhibitor with JAK2 and GAK tied, has the
most unstable ratio ranking (Figure~\ref{fig:profiles}, left).

\paragraph{Profile associations.} Ranked among compounds with an active kinase,
three associations between profile features and instability are significant at
$p < 0.05$ on both $\mathrm{p}K_d$ datasets (Supporting Information, Table~S4). The number of
active kinases correlates negatively with entropy instability on every dataset
($r = -0.30$ to $-0.42$, on Davis only nominally, with $p = 0.01$), and with Gini
instability on Davis and Klaeger ($r = -0.31$ and $-0.25$). On
Klaeger, entropy instability correlates with the share of a compound's
above-floor kinases that lie within the baseline sweep ($r = 0.22$,
$p = 0.002$). The top$_1$--top$_2$ gap
correlates with ratio instability on Davis, Klaeger and Metz ($r = -0.44$,
$-0.28$ and $-0.26$, all $p < 0.001$), but not on Anastassiadis ($r = 0.00$, or
$-0.15$ with $p = 0.10$ without eight compounds whose two best targets are at the
$100\%$ inhibition ceiling). With a Bonferroni correction, only the ratio--gap
association remains significant on both $\mathrm{p}K_d$ datasets, as it does
with the Holm and Benjamini--Hochberg procedures. On Klaeger
(but not on Davis),
S-score instability also correlates with the range of active affinities
($r = -0.32$, and partial $r = -0.22$ with the number of active kinases held
fixed). Ranked among all 222 Klaeger compounds, the skewness
of the active affinities was the strongest correlate of Gini instability
($r = +0.26$). Ranked among active compounds, it is not significant ($r = +0.07$,
$p = 0.31$), so the marginal association came mainly from the scoring of
compounds with no active kinase. With the number of active kinases held fixed,
a weaker association remains (Supporting Information, Section~S3).

\paragraph{Broad profiles.} Among active compounds, seven of the ten largest
entropy--ratio disagreements on Klaeger involve profiles with 14--27 active
kinases, and six of these have one clearly dominant target (top$_1$--top$_2$
gap above $1$). The ratio ranks these seven 31st to 63rd of 200 and the
entropy and Gini 146th to 180th. This is because the ratio reflects the dominant target, and the
entropy and Gini reflect the number of active kinases. Bosc et al.\cite{Bosc2017}
attributed disagreements for individual compounds to such profiles (i.e. broad profiles with one
dominant target). On Davis, the most unstable
compound overall is a profile of this kind (Figure~\ref{fig:profiles},
right).

\subsection{Criteria and the Gated Entropy}
\label{sec:desiderata}

Table~\ref{tab:desiderata} gives each score's verdict on the four criteria,
D1 to D4, all judged by construction (Methods). None of the four definitions,
as published, withholds the score of a compound with no active kinase (so none
meets D1), but any score can meet it by adopting that rule. The comparators and
the gated entropy adopt it. D2 and D3
are the criteria on which the scores differ. The entropy, the Gini and the
ratio violate D2, and the other scores satisfy it. The published entropy and
$P_{\max}$ have no boundary parameter, so D2 does not apply to them. The
S-score with $\tau > x_0$, the entropy with $\beta = x_0$, the Gini and the
published entropy violate D3. The other scores, including $P_{\max}$, satisfy
it. Every score in the table satisfies D4, and so do $P_{\max}$ and the
published entropy with floor-coded pairs given zero weight. D2 does not make
a ranking independent of where the boundary is placed: for every score, moving
the boundary across $B$ reverses at least $13.7\%$ of pairs on at least one
dataset (Supporting Information, Table~S3).

\emph{By construction.} These verdicts follow from each score's definition,
without data. The S-score and the active count depend on the boundary only
through which targets lie above it, so they satisfy D2. The entropy and Gini
subtract $\beta$ from every readout above it, and $R_b(k)$ replaces a
reference off-target below $b$ by $b$. Moving the boundary therefore changes
these scores even when it passes no value, and they violate D2. Moving the
gated entropy's gate multiplies every weight above it by the same factor, so
it satisfies D2. The S-score violates D3 for any threshold above the floor,
because adding a kinase bound between the floor and the threshold increases
$N$ but not the number of hits, which makes the compound look more
selective. With the threshold at the floor, every addition that D3 allows is a
hit, and a compound with $C$ hits among $N$ kinases moves from $-C/N$ to
$-(C+1)/(N+1)$, which is not higher. The active count ranks compounds on any
one panel as the S-score at $\tau^*$ does, but it does not divide by $N$. A
kinase added at or below $\tau^*$ is not counted, so the count and the score
stay the same, and D3 holds. D3 therefore concerns how scores change when a
panel is extended. The Gini violates
D3, since a weakly bound kinase can raise its inequality. The ratio satisfies
it, because a weak addition cannot displace the primary target or lower the
reference off-target. $P_{\max}$ satisfies it too, since a weak addition leaves its
numerator unchanged and raises its denominator. The entropy satisfies D3 at
$\beta = \tau^*$, where weak binding carries zero weight. The gated entropy
satisfies D1 through its eligibility rule and D3 by Proposition 1 in Methods.
D3 concerns adding a target, not strengthening one already in the profile (so
strengthening one is allowed to raise the gated entropy's score). 
For example, with one kinase at $\mathrm{p}K_d = 7.0$ and fifty at $5.1$, the
score is $-5.502$. Raising one of the fifty to $6.0$ raises it to $-5.491$.

D4 asks that a score not rise when a compound binds one more kinase as
strongly as the others. For any compound that binds $m$ kinases at the same
value $a$, and the rest of the panel at the floor (such profiles are denoted
by $\mathbf{v}_m$ in Methods), the scores are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item active count: $-m$
  \item S-score with $\tau < a$: $-m/N$
  \item the entropies and the gated entropy, whose $m$ weights are equal:
        $-\log_2 m$
  \item $P_{\max}$: $1/m$
  \item Gini: $(N - m)/N$
  \item ratio $R_b(k)$: $a - \max(x_0, b)$ for $m \leq k$ and $0$ otherwise.
\end{itemize}
None of these values rises as $m$ grows, so all of these scores satisfy D4.
The values assume that floor-coded pairs have zero weight, which,
for the entropy and Gini, requires $\beta \geq x_0$. On Anastassiadis, their
sweeps also include baselines below the $20\%$ floor, where both fail D4: on
its 291 kinases, with $\beta = 0\%$ and targets at $60\%$, a second target
raises the Gini from $0.0068$ to $0.0135$. With floor-coded pairs kept at the
floor, the published entropy also fails D4 on panels of the datasets' sizes: on 363 kinases with targets at
$\mathrm{p}K_d = 7$, a second target raises its score from $-7.41$ to $-6.76$
bits. $P_{\max}$ satisfies D4 under either treatment, because its largest share
falls as $m$ grows.

D1 to D3 alone do not fix the direction of a score. The active count with
its sign reversed, which calls a compound more selective the more active
kinases it has, depends only on which targets lie above $\tau^*$, and a kinase
added at or below $\tau^*$ leaves it unchanged. It therefore satisfies D1 to
D3, as the gated entropy does, but it
rises with the number of equally strong targets ($+m$ on $\mathbf{v}_m$),
contradicting D4. The gated entropy without its non-binder gate also fails
D4, because floor-coded pairs carry weight, and adding equally strong
targets can concentrate the distribution. For the entropy, the gated entropy,
$P_{\max}$ and the Gini, Proposition 3 gives a conclusion stronger than D4. On
any
profile (not only one with equally strong targets), turning a floor-coded pair
into a target that is no stronger than the weakest weighted one does not raise the
score.

\emph{By construction: counterexamples.} The published entropy violates D3: with
floor-coded pairs given zero weight, a profile with one target at
$\mathrm{p}K_d = 6.01$ and fifty at $5.01$ scores $-5.35$ bits, and adding one
at $6.0$ raises this to $-5.19$. The entropy violates D3 at $\beta < \tau^*$.
Its hard cutoff gives a kinase just above $\beta$ a weight close to zero. A
profile with many such kinases therefore has high entropy. Adding one kinase
bound more strongly, but still below $\tau^*$, concentrates the distribution.
With
$\beta = 5.0$, a profile with one kinase at $\mathrm{p}K_d = 7.0$ and fifty at
$5.1$ scores $-4.89$ bits, and adding one at $6.0$ raises this to $-4.83$.
The gated entropy does not violate D3 on this profile: adding the kinase at
$6.0$ lowers its score from $-5.502$ to $-5.522$, because the exponential
weights give that kinase only $2.5$ times the weight of a kinase at $5.1$
($2.72$ against $1.11$). The entropy's hard cutoff gives it $10$ times the
weight ($1.0$ against $0.1$).

\emph{Empirical checks.} To additionally check empirically that Proposition 1 
holds, we tested D3
for the entropy and the gated entropy on the measured profiles. 
On the measured profiles of the active
compounds, adding a hypothetical kinase at $\tau^*$ never raises the entropy's
score on Davis, Klaeger or Metz at any baseline in their sweeps. On
Anastassiadis, at baselines below the $20\%$ floor ($33\%$ of the
compounds at $\beta = 0\%$, $16\%$ at $5\%$), it does. It never raises the gated
entropy's score on any dataset. Adding measured kinases bound below the
threshold to random 50- or 100-kinase subpanels of the same profiles raises the
entropy's score in at most $0.1\%$ of cases on any dataset, and never the gated
entropy's.

On the four datasets, we
compared the gated entropy's rankings with those of other scores, and tested
their sensitivity to $\tau^*$ and to noise. On the data, the gated
entropy's score falls as the number of active kinases rises (Spearman $-0.76$
to $-0.95$). Without the non-binder gate, the
correlation reverses ($r = +0.67$ on Klaeger), as its failure of D4 predicts.
Its rankings agree with the entropy's ($0.969$--$0.993$) and with those of the
S-score with $\tau = x_0$ ($0.964$--$0.996$), which also satisfies D1 to D4.
Moving $\tau^*$ from $5.5$ to $7$ ($40\%$ to $70\%$ on Anastassiadis), with
$T = \tau^* - x_0$ moved with it, changes which compounds are scored and how
they are ranked: over the compounds scored at both values, the Spearman
correlation with the default ranking is $0.85$ to $0.999$. The lowest values
are at $\tau^* = 5.5$ on Davis and Klaeger, where $T$ falls to $0.5$
$\mathrm{p}K$ units. 
With random noise added to the measured values, as described in Methods, D1
changed the status of at most 11 compounds in any draw. Over the compounds
scored both with and without noise, the gated entropy's ranking correlated
with its noise-free ranking at Spearman $0.972$ or higher, and the entropy's
at $0.983$ or higher. On these datasets, the gated entropy is not an empirical
improvement on simpler scores. Its difference from the entropy is in
construction: the entropy satisfies D3 at $\beta = \tau^*$, where binding below
the threshold has zero weight, and the gated entropy satisfies D3 while giving
that binding weight.

\emph{What the criteria determine.} 
The active count, the S-score with $\tau = x_0$ and the gated entropy satisfy
all four criteria, and so does every gated entropy with $T \geq \tau^* - x_0$.
Proposition 2 narrows the choice within one class of scores: those that weight
each target above the gate by the same continuous, nondecreasing function of
its distance above the gate and combine the weight shares with Shannon's
grouping property. In this class, D2 and D4 reject everything but the gated
entropies and the score that gives every target above the gate the same
weight, $-\log_2 n$ for $n$ such targets (the limit $T \to \infty$). D3 then
additionally retains only the gated entropies with $T \geq \tau^* - x_0$
(Proposition 1) and the equal-weight score. With the gate and $T$ fixed, the
score is determined up to a positive multiple. Taking the smallest admissible
$T$ is our choice. On Klaeger, the
rankings for $T = 1$, $1.5$ and $2$ agree at Spearman $0.985$ or higher, and
other variants are compared in Supporting Information, Section~S4.

\subsection{Recovery from Small Panels}
\label{sec:recovery}

On small random panels, some compounds can no longer be scored. At 50 targets,
$88\%$ of the compounds scored on the full panel are still scored on Davis,
$71\%$ on Klaeger, $77\%$ on Anastassiadis and $99\%$ on Metz
(Figure~\ref{fig:candidate}, top). The compounds lost are mostly those with few
active targets: on a 50-target Klaeger subpanel, a compound with one active
target stays scored with probability $0.23$, and one with ten with probability
$0.93$. High conditional agreement on a small panel can therefore coexist with
the loss of many narrowly active compounds. Coverage limits the recovery of pair
order. Keeping $90\%$ of clearly separated pairs takes 150 targets on Davis,
200 on Klaeger and Anastassiadis, and 40 on Metz for every score except the
ratio, the published entropy and $P_{\max}$, which need as many or more
(Figure~\ref{fig:candidate}, middle).

Among compounds scored on both panels, scores whose ranking depends mostly on
the few strongest targets recover slowest (Figure~\ref{fig:candidate},
bottom).
The S-score with $\tau = x_0$, the entropy with $\beta = x_0$ and the Gini
rank compounds almost as the share of targets with detected binding does
(Spearman above $0.97$ with the S-score at $\tau = x_0$ on every dataset). We
call these three the \emph{share-following scores}. The curves of the entropy
and Gini differ from the S-score's by at most $0.012$, so
Figure~\ref{fig:candidate} draws only the S-score with $\tau = x_0$.
They recover fastest on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis, reaching $p^*$ at
20, 50 and 30 targets, against 30, 80 and 50 for the S-score at $\tau^*$ and
the active count (which rank compounds identically on any one panel and share
one curve in Figure~\ref{fig:candidate}), and 40, 100 and 65 for the entropy with $\beta = \tau^*$. On
Metz, where untested targets are set to the floor, the S-score at $\tau^*$, the
active count and the entropy with $\beta = \tau^*$ recover fastest (50
targets, against 65 for the share-following scores). With bars of $0.80$,
$0.85$ or $0.95$ instead of $0.90$, these fastest scores still reach the bar no
later than any score outside their group. The published entropy and $P_{\max}$, with floor-coded pairs given zero weight, need
300 of 363 targets on Davis and 200 of 217 on Klaeger. On Metz, the published
entropy needs 150 of 172, while $P_{\max}$ needs more than the largest subpanel tested
(150). The ratio needs 200 of 217 targets on Klaeger and more than the
largest subpanel tested on the other datasets (300 of 363, 250 of 291 and 150
of 172). The published entropy gives weak binding a small positive weight, so
including weak binding does not by itself make a score recover quickly. The
gated entropy follows the share of targets with detected binding less closely
(Spearman $0.964$--$0.996$ with the S-score at $\tau = x_0$). Its mean Spearman
curve lies below that of the S-score with $\tau = x_0$ on every dataset, by up
to $0.069$, and it reaches $p^*$ at 50, 65, 30 and 80 targets on Davis,
Klaeger, Anastassiadis and Metz.

The value $p^*$ is a threshold on the mean: at $p^*$, $62$--$90\%$ of individual
subpanels exceed $0.90$ for the share-following scores (Supporting
Information, Section~S5). On
Davis, Anastassiadis and Metz, compounds whose score changes most when one
target is left out tend to have larger rank errors at 50 targets (Spearman
$r = 0.18$--$0.70$ for the entropy with $\beta = x_0$, the Gini and the gated
entropy). On Klaeger, this association is weak ($r \leq 0.13$).

\subsection{Scale of the Gated Entropy and Recovery}
\label{sec:results:scale}

The gated entropy satisfies D1 to D4 for any scale $T \geq \tau^* - x_0$. With
$T = 2(\tau^* - x_0)$, it reaches $p^*$ at 20, 50, 30 and 65 targets on Davis,
Klaeger, Anastassiadis and Metz, the values of the share-following scores,
against 50, 65, 30 and 80 with $T = \tau^* - x_0$. A larger scale gives the
targets more nearly equal weights, and the score then ranks compounds almost as
the S-score with $\tau = x_0$ does (Spearman $0.9968$--$0.9996$). Recovery
compares each score with its own full-panel ranking, so the faster recovery
does not show that the score with the larger scale measures selectivity better.
The other analyses keep $T = \tau^* - x_0$, the admissible scale at which the
score depends most on affinity differences. Supporting Information,
Section~S5, gives the same comparison for $T = 4(\tau^* - x_0)$.
