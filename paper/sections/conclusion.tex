\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Kinase inhibitor selectivity rankings depend on choices usually left implicit:
how affinities are weighted, which compounds are ranked, how pairs without
detected binding are treated, which targets the panel includes, and where the
activity boundary is placed. These choices change whether the common scores
agree, how much a ranking moves when the activity boundary moves, and how much
of a ranking a small panel retains. Selectivity analyses should state
them, apply an activity-eligibility rule, fix the activity boundary in advance
and report its effect, and report how many compounds remain scorable on small
panels. The gated entropy gives weight to binding below the activity threshold
and remains monotone under weak-target addition. Among scores that weight each
target by its distance above the activity boundary and satisfy Shannon's
grouping rule, the criteria leave only gated entropies whose width is at least
the distance between the floor and the activity threshold. On these data, the
gated entropy with that smallest width recovers rankings from small panels more
slowly than the share of targets with detected binding. With twice the width,
it recovers them as fast and ranks compounds almost as that share does.
