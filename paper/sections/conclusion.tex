\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Kinase inhibitor selectivity rankings depend on choices usually left implicit:
how affinities are weighted, which compounds are ranked, how pairs without
detected binding are treated, which targets the panel includes, and where the
activity boundary is placed. These choices change whether the common scores
agree, how much a ranking moves when the activity boundary moves, and how much
of a ranking a small panel retains. Selectivity analyses should state
them, apply an activity-eligibility rule, fix the activity boundary in advance
and report its effect, and report how many compounds remain scorable on small
panels. In particular, high agreement among compounds that remain eligible on
a smaller panel can conceal the loss of narrowly active compounds.

The formal contribution is a characterization within an explicit class of
distance-based weights and Shannon grouping: the proposed criteria retain
gated exponential entropies, including their equal-weight limit, and set a
sharp lower bound on their scale for monotonicity under weak-target addition.
The criteria do not select one score, and satisfying them does not establish
superior biological validity or empirical recovery. The gated entropy's scale
is another analysis choice that must be reported. The central practical
conclusion is therefore to interpret score agreement and small-panel recovery
together with the implementation, eligible compounds and retained coverage.
