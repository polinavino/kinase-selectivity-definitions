\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Kinase inhibitor selectivity rankings depend on choices usually left implicit:
how affinities are weighted, which compounds are ranked, how floor-coded
pairs are treated, which targets the panel includes, and where the
activity boundary is placed. These choices change whether the common scores
agree, how much a ranking moves when the activity boundary moves, and how much
of a ranking a small panel retains. Selectivity analyses should state
them, apply an activity-eligibility rule, fix the activity boundary in advance
and report its effect, as well as report how many compounds remain scorable on small
panels. Rank agreement can remain high even when compounds with few active
targets become unscorable on a smaller panel.

The gated entropy meets all four design criteria while weighting targets by
binding strength, including weak binding above the floor. Under the
assumptions of Proposition 2, the criteria allow only exponential or equal
weights. For exponential weights, criterion D3 sets the minimum scale at which adding
a weakly bound target cannot make a compound appear more selective.
On the datasets analyzed here (Davis, Klaeger, Anastassiadis and Metz), 
it has no recovery advantage over
simpler scores. Any scale at or above this minimum satisfies the criteria.
Increasing the scale reduces weight differences between targets and changes both
rankings and recovery from small panels, so the scale must be stated
when the score is reported.
