\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proposed four properties that a well-formed kinase inhibitor
selectivity measure should satisfy: a reliability threshold condition,
bounded gap sensitivity, distributional consistency, and monotonicity under
weak off-target addition. None of the four definitions assessed satisfies all
four. The one property all four violate is the reliability condition, a
reporting convention that any of them could adopt.

The properties come from characterizing the definitions on four datasets.
Rank instability within and between definition families is associated with
binding profile shape and has three structurally distinct sources. How the
definitions relate depends on how they are computed: on a common log-affinity
scale they split into two groups that measure different properties of binding
profiles (the S-score, entropy and Gini agree more with one another than with
the ratio), but under the association-constant form of the original
publication the entropy does not cluster with the S-score and Gini.

The candidate we propose, a gated, smooth-hinge effective-target number,
satisfies all four properties in the following ways:

\begin{itemize}
    \item[(i)] the reliability condition by a
reporting rule;
    \item[(ii)] bounded gap sensitivity vacuously;
    \item[(iii)] distributional consistency
because its activity boundary is
fixed at the assay floor (swept as a free parameter, that boundary moves the
ranking about as much as the entropy's baseline does); and
    \item[(iv)] monotonicity under weak
off-target addition by its functional form, which we prove.
\end{itemize}

The candidate recovers its full-panel ranking from about
a tenth to a quarter of the panel on Davis, Klaeger, and Anastassiadis, as the
entropy does, and variants of it satisfy the four properties on Davis and
Klaeger (Supporting Information, Section~S5). It ranks the compounds almost
identically to the entropy, so on these data it is
not an improvement on the entropy. The case for the properties therefore does not rest on these data:
they are requirements on the measure itself, and we propose them as checks
that any new selectivity measure should pass.

The main goal of this work is to identify criteria for judging
a candidate that could be considered a principled, universally adopted
choice of selectivity measure in the future. Such criteria must be justified
(which we argue they are),
and jointly satisfiable (which the candidate demonstrates).
Further properties may discriminate where these four do
not, and the candidate may separate from the entropy on panels whose active
fractions differ from the four studied here.
Establishing additional properties that uniquely characterize a selectivity
measure, as Shannon's axioms do for entropy, is a natural next step, for which
additional datasets may be required.

The immediate practical implication is that selectivity assessments should
report the sensitivity of rankings to parameter choice, and should explicitly
flag compounds with no binding above the activity threshold as unreliable.
The longer-term implication is that the field needs a principled basis for
choosing among selectivity definitions.
The need for a principled measure is not specific to selectivity.
As future and ongoing work, we are developing a general
measurement-theoretic framework for defining and comparing the competing
measures of one concept, reporting where they agree and which pairs they
leave undecided, with kinase selectivity as one instance alongside cases
as varied as biological age and variant effect prediction.
