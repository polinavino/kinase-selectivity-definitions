\subsection{Existing Definitions Against the Properties}
\label{sec:desiderata}

Table~\ref{tab:desiderata} summarizes how the four definitions and the candidate
measure fare against the properties D1--D4 defined in Methods. None of the four
definitions satisfies all four. D1 is the only property all four violate, and
the entropy comes closest overall: it satisfies D2 (vacuously) and fails D1,
D3, and D4.

\textit{D1.} None of the four definitions has a reliability condition, so each
assigns a score to the 16 Klaeger compounds with no binding above the activity
threshold. Their ranks are set by convention rather than by measured binding.
Pooled across the four definitions' parameter sweeps, their rank instability is
$\sigma = 72.9$ against $31.3$ for active compounds ($p < 0.001$, Mann--Whitney
$U$ test; Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}, left). The contrast is driven by Gini
($\sigma = 86.1$ versus $15.9$) and entropy ($17.1$ versus $12.5$), is marginal
for the ratio ($24.0$ versus $20.7$), and reverses for the S-score ($18.5$
versus $24.3$), whose score is exactly $0$ for every such compound. It is
largely a product of the convention for undefined entropy and Gini scores
(Methods): a zero-active compound's entropy and Gini are defined at only 1--4
of the 7 baselines swept (mean 3.2), and computed only over each compound's
defined configurations, zero-active compounds are slightly \emph{more} stable
than active ones (entropy: $\sigma = 9.8$ vs.\ $12.1$, $p = 0.085$; Gini:
$\sigma = 9.5$ vs.\ $13.0$, $p = 0.028$; 14 of the 16 compounds, the other two
being defined at fewer than two baselines;
\texttt{undefined\_score\_convention.py}). The case for D1 is therefore not that
these scores are noisier, but that they rest on an arbitrary choice that D1
removes. Any of the four definitions could satisfy D1 by withholding or
flagging such scores; for the ratio, our implementation's floor fallback is what
violates it (Methods, ``Ratio'').

\textit{D2.} The ratio violates D2 when its top two affinities are nearly tied.
On the 17 Klaeger compounds with a top$_1$--top$_2$ gap below $0.05$, mean
$R(1) = 0.02$ (the near-tied second kinase is treated as the off-target) against
mean $R(2) = 0.42$ (it is treated as a co-target;
\texttt{d2\_near\_tie\_sensitivity.py}). This is the Type~2 instability above,
where the top$_1$--top$_2$ gap is the strongest predictor of ratio instability
($r = -0.341$, $p < 0.001$). The S-score, entropy, Gini, and candidate have no
reference off-target and satisfy D2 vacuously; the S-score's sensitivity to
kinases near its threshold is a D3 matter.

\textit{D3.} Swept over $B = [x_0, \tau^*]$, the S-score, entropy, and Gini
each reverse the order of $8$--$23\%$ of pairs of scored compounds, on every
dataset (S-score $8.0$--$20.6\%$, entropy $8.8$--$22.6\%$, Gini
$9.1$--$22.4\%$; highest on Metz; \texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py}), far above
$\varepsilon = 1\%$. The candidate's emphasis baseline reverses at most
$0.72\%$, so every verdict in Table~\ref{tab:desiderata} is the same for any
$\varepsilon$ from $1\%$ to $7\%$. This is consistent with the
$n_\mathrm{active}$--instability correlation of Table~\ref{tab:predictors}:
compounds with few active kinases change their active sets as the boundary
moves.
Making $\beta$ an explicit parameter is what makes this testable: the published
originals leave it implicit ($K_a \to 0$ for the entropy, $0\%$ inhibition for
the Gini). The ratio has no baseline and satisfies D3 vacuously.

\textit{D4.} The S-score and Gini violate D4 by construction: both are computed
over the full panel of size $N$, so adding a kinase the compound does not bind
lowers the S-score's hit fraction and raises the Gini's inequality, inflating
the apparent selectivity. A compound can therefore be made to look more
selective simply by profiling it against more inactive kinases, which matters
whenever panels differ in size or composition, as between Davis and Klaeger.
The ratio satisfies D4, because a sub-threshold kinase falls at or below its
reference off-target. The entropy satisfies D4 only for additions at or below
its baseline $\beta$, which carry zero weight; a kinase between $\beta$ and
$\tau^*$ carries positive weight and can raise its score (``Why the Smooth
Hinge Matters'', below). These arguments refer to the selectivity-oriented $f$
(Methods), not to the raw entropy or S-score, which run the opposite way.

\subsection{The Candidate Measure}
\label{sec:candidate}

The candidate measure (Methods) is evaluated here to show that the four
properties can be met together. It is correctly oriented: its score falls with
the number of active kinases on every dataset (Spearman $-0.785$ to $-0.969$).
The non-binder gate is what makes this so. Without it, the non-binding kinases
at the floor dominate the profile and the score inverts, rising with the number
of active kinases ($r = +0.94$ on Klaeger).

Because the candidate is a local modification of the entropy, the two rank the
compounds they both score almost identically (full-panel Spearman $+0.999$ on
Klaeger and $+0.995$ on Metz). The properties do not act by reordering
compounds in the body of the distribution. They act on which compounds receive
a score at all (D1, which flags 16 of 222 Klaeger compounds), on whether the
ranking survives a shift in an analyst's parameter (D3), and on profiles that
these datasets rarely or never contain (D4). The case for the properties
therefore does not rest on these data. They are requirements on every binding
profile, not only on the profiles four datasets happen to contain, and we
propose them as checks that any new selectivity measure should pass. ``Why the
Smooth Hinge Matters'', below, gives a concrete case in which the entropy fails
one of them on a real dataset and the candidate provably does not.

The candidate satisfies D1 by its reliability gate and D2 vacuously. For D3,
its only free parameter is the emphasis baseline $\beta$, and sweeping it over
$B$ reverses $0.43$--$0.72\%$ of pairs, so it passes on every dataset
(\texttt{d3\_pair\_reversals.py}). Its activity boundary, however, is not
robust; it is fixed. Sweeping the gate $x_0$ itself over $B$ reverses
$8.5$--$22.5\%$ of pairs, against $8.8$--$22.6\%$ for the entropy's baseline,
and the two sweeps produce the same per-compound instability on Klaeger (mean
rank $\sigma = 7.2$ and $7.5$; Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}, right). The
candidate's D3 standing therefore comes entirely from declaring $x_0$ a fixed
constant, and Table~\ref{tab:desiderata} marks it $\checkmark^{\dagger}$. D4
holds by the proposition in Methods, and also on every compound in all four
datasets with one kinase added at $\tau^*$, the hardest case
(\texttt{candidate\_validation.py}; Supporting Information, Table~S4).

A usable measure must also be recoverable from realistic panels. With the
panel-size subsampling analysis above (same grid, 50 random subpanels per
size), the candidate reaches mean Spearman $\rho > 0.90$ with its full-panel
ranking at $p^* = 110$ kinases on Klaeger and $p^* = 80$ of 172 on Metz
(Figure~\ref{fig:candidate}). This matches the entropy on both datasets; on
Klaeger it is well ahead of the Gini ($290$) and the ratio ($343$), and on Metz
the S-score converges faster ($50$). These $p^*$ values score every compound,
including ones the D1 gate would refuse to score once their active kinases fall
outside a subpanel ($42\%$ of Klaeger compounds at 50 kinases, $27\%$ at 110,
at most $2\%$ on Metz). Restricting the comparison to compounds that D1 allows
to be scored on both the subpanel and the full panel moves the candidate, the
entropy, and the Gini all to $p^* = 80$ on Klaeger, with Metz unchanged. Part
of this gain is selection, since the gate removes the least-bound compounds,
which are also the hardest to rank. The gate and the smooth emphasis thus
neither cost nor buy convergence speed relative to the entropy.

The featured candidate is one member of a family of measures that satisfy
D1--D4 on these data: a per-compound quantile floor in place of the fixed floor
and R\'enyi orders up to $\alpha = 1$ do so on Davis and Klaeger, and smoothing
widths from $0.75$ to $2$ times $\tau^* - x_0$ on Klaeger (D4 is proved only
for the Shannon order, $\alpha = 1$). These variants, including how the R\'enyi
order affects panel recoverability, are reported in Supporting Information
(Section~S5).

\subsection{Why the Smooth Hinge Matters}
\label{sec:hinge}

The purpose of this subsection is to show that the candidate and the entropy
differ in principle, although they rank these four datasets almost identically,
and that the difference comes from the candidate's functional form rather than
from a reporting rule (D1) or a declared constant (D3). The property at stake
is D4.

\textit{The entropy can violate D4.} Its hard cutoff gives a kinase just above
the baseline $\beta$ an arbitrarily small weight. A profile with many such
kinases therefore has high entropy, and adding one kinase that is bound more
strongly, but still below $\tau^*$, concentrates the distribution and raises the
selectivity score. For example, with $\beta = 5.0$ and $\tau^* = 6.0$, a profile
with one kinase at $\mathrm{p}K_d = 7.0$ and fifty at $5.1$ has an
entropy-based selectivity of $-4.89$ bits, and adding one kinase at
$\mathrm{p}K_d = 6.0$ raises it to $-4.83$.
On Davis, Klaeger, and Metz, adding one kinase at $\tau^*$ never raises the
entropy's score for any compound at any baseline in their sweeps. On
Anastassiadis it does at low baselines: weighting each kinase by its raw
percent inhibition ($\beta = 0\%$), as the published percent-inhibition forms
do, it raises the score for $34\%$ of the 134 compounds with an active kinase,
and for $16\%$ at $\beta = 5\%$, although for none at $15\%$ or above
(\texttt{d4\_entropy\_vs\_candidate.py}).

\textit{The candidate cannot.} By the proposition in Methods, with
$T \geq 0.58\,(\tau^* - x_0)$ no sub-threshold addition can raise its score.
The condition on $T$ is sharp: just below it, a profile with one strong target
and enough kinases just above the floor violates D4. With $T = 1$ on Metz or
$T = 10$ on Anastassiadis, both below the bound, constructed profiles of this
kind violate D4 (by up to $0.014$ bits on Anastassiadis), although none of the
real compounds does. Setting $T = \tau^* - x_0$ on every dataset satisfies the
condition with room to spare, leaves Davis and Klaeger unchanged, and changes
the Metz and Anastassiadis rankings negligibly (Spearman $0.999$ against $T = 1$
and $T = 10$, respectively).

The two measures rank these datasets almost identically, yet they differ on a
question the rankings do not test: whether binding one more kinase below
threshold can make a compound look more selective. For the entropy it can, for
a third of the Anastassiadis compounds at the published forms' own baseline;
for the candidate it provably cannot.
