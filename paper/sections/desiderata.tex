\subsection{Existing Definitions Against the Properties}
\label{sec:desiderata}

Table~\ref{tab:desiderata} summarizes how the four definitions and the candidate
measure fare against the properties D1--D4 defined in Methods. None of the four
definitions satisfies all four, nor even D2 and D4 together: the ratio fails
D2, and the S-score, entropy and Gini fail D4.

\textit{D1.} None of the four definitions has a reliability condition, so each
assigns a score to the 16 Klaeger compounds with no binding above the activity
threshold.
Pooled across the four definitions' parameter sweeps, their rank instability is
$\sigma = 74.0$ against $31.2$ for active compounds ($p < 0.001$, Mann--Whitney
$U$ test; Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}, left). Within a single definition the
contrast is significant only for the Gini ($\sigma = 86.1$ versus $15.9$). It is
small and not significant for the entropy ($14.1$ versus $11.9$, $p = 0.25$) and
the ratio ($24.0$ versus $20.7$, $p = 0.08$), and reverses for the S-score
($18.5$ versus $24.3$, $p = 0.07$), whose score is exactly $0$ for every such
compound at the nine thresholds at or above $\tau^*$.
The pooled contrast persists with the undefined entropy and Gini
configurations dropped ($\sigma = 59.6$ against $30.6$) and with only the
S-score and ratio pooled ($66.0$ against $36.7$, both $p < 0.001$). The Gini's contrast results from the convention
for undefined scores (Methods). A zero-active compound's entropy
and Gini are defined at only 1--4 of the 7 baselines swept (mean 3.2). When instability is computed
only over each compound's defined configurations, ranking compounds at each
baseline among those defined there, zero-active compounds are \emph{more}
stable than active ones (entropy: $\sigma = 9.8$ vs.\ $12.8$, $p = 0.046$;
Gini: $\sigma = 9.5$ vs.\ $13.0$, $p = 0.028$). This uses 14 of the 16 compounds,
since the other two are defined at fewer than two baselines. The case for D1 is
therefore not that these scores are unstable within a definition, but that they
rest on sub-threshold values alone, which each definition treats differently.
Any of
the four definitions could satisfy D1 by withholding or flagging such scores.
For the ratio, the violation comes from our implementation's floor fallback
(Methods, ``Ratio'').

\textit{D2.} The ratio violates D2 when its top two affinities are nearly tied.
On the 17 Klaeger compounds with a top$_1$--top$_2$ gap below $0.05$, mean
$R(1) = 0.02$ (the near-tied second kinase is treated as the off-target) against
mean $R(2) = 0.42$ (it is treated as a co-target). This is the Type~2 instability above. The S-score, entropy, Gini, and candidate have no
reference off-target and satisfy D2 vacuously. The S-score's sensitivity to
kinases near its threshold is a D3 matter.

\textit{D3.} Swept over $B = [x_0, \tau^*]$, the S-score's threshold and the
entropy's and Gini's baseline each reverse the order of $8$--$12\%$ of pairs of
scored compounds on Davis, Klaeger and Anastassiadis, and $21$--$23\%$ on Metz,
far above $\varepsilon = 1\%$. So does the candidate's gate, with per-compound
instability similar to the entropy's baseline on Klaeger (mean rank
$\sigma = 7.2$ and $7.5$; Figure~\ref{fig:failure_modes}, right). The ratio's
floor fallback reverses $2$--$12\%$ of pairs on Klaeger and Anastassiadis,
depending on $k$. Each measure therefore satisfies D3 only with that parameter
fixed: the S-score's threshold at $\tau^*$, and every other at $x_0$. The
candidate's remaining parameter, its emphasis baseline, reverses at most
$0.72\%$ of pairs, so every verdict in Table~\ref{tab:desiderata} is the same for
any $\varepsilon$ from $1\%$ to $12\%$. The published originals leave the
baseline implicit ($K_a \to 0$ for the entropy, $0\%$ inhibition for the
Gini).

\textit{D4.} The S-score and Gini violate D4 by construction: both are computed
over the full panel of size $N$, so adding a kinase the compound binds only
weakly lowers the S-score's hit fraction and can raise the Gini's inequality,
inflating the apparent selectivity. This matters whenever panels differ in size or composition, as between Davis and Klaeger.
The ratio satisfies D4, because a sub-threshold kinase cannot displace the
primary target and cannot lower the reference off-target's affinity. The entropy satisfies D4 only for additions at or below
its baseline $\beta$ (``Why the Smooth Hinge Matters'', below).

\subsection{The Candidate Measure}
\label{sec:candidate}

The candidate measure (Methods) is correctly oriented: its score falls with
the number of active kinases on every dataset (Spearman $-0.783$ to $-0.969$).
The non-binder gate is what makes this so. Without it, the non-binding kinases
at the floor dominate the profile and the score inverts, rising with the number
of active kinases ($r = +0.93$ on Klaeger).

Because the candidate is a local modification of the entropy, the two rank the
compounds they both score almost identically (full-panel Spearman
$0.994$--$0.999$ on all four datasets). The properties do not act by reordering
compounds in the body of the distribution. They act on which compounds receive
a score at all (D1, which flags 16 of 222 Klaeger compounds) and on whether
adding a sub-threshold kinase can raise a score (D4).

The candidate satisfies D1 by its reliability gate, D2 vacuously, and D3 with
its gate fixed at $x_0$ (above). The activity threshold $\tau^*$, which sets its
D1 gate and $T$, is fixed too. Moving it from $5.5$ to $7$ ($40\%$ to $70\%$ on
Anastassiadis) changes which compounds are scored but barely changes how the
candidate ranks those scored at both values (Spearman $\geq 0.999$ on every
dataset). D4
holds by the proposition in Methods. It also holds on every compound in all
four datasets when one kinase is added at $\tau^*$, the strongest binding that
D4 allows (Supporting Information Table~S4).

A usable measure must also be recoverable from realistic panels. With the
panel-size subsampling analysis above, the candidate reaches mean Spearman $r > 0.90$ with its full-panel
ranking at $p^* = 50$ kinases on Davis and Anastassiadis (the smallest panel
tested) and 80 on Klaeger and Metz (Figure~\ref{fig:candidate}). These values
match the entropy on every dataset.
These $p^*$ values
apply the formula without the D1 gate, so they include compounds the gate would refuse to score (at 50
kinases, $9\%$ of Davis compounds, $42\%$ of Klaeger, $43\%$ of Anastassiadis,
and $2\%$ of Metz, counting those with no active kinase on the full panel).
Restricting the comparison to compounds that D1 allows to be scored on both the
subpanel and the full panel leaves the candidate's and the entropy's $p^*$
unchanged and brings the Gini to the same values (50 on Davis and
Anastassiadis, 80 on Klaeger and Metz). The Gini's gain comes from removing
compounds whose Gini is undefined on the subpanel, which the convention ranks
least selective (Methods).

The featured candidate is one member of a family of measures that satisfy
D1--D4 on these data: the R\'enyi orders $0.5$ and $1$ do so on Davis and Klaeger, and smoothing
widths from $0.75$ to $2$ times $\tau^* - x_0$ on Klaeger (D4 is proved only
for the Shannon order, $\alpha = 1$). These variants, including their panel
recoverability, are reported in Supporting Information
(Section~S5).

\subsection{Why the Smooth Hinge Matters}
\label{sec:hinge}

The candidate and the entropy differ in principle on D4, through the
candidate's functional form.

\textit{The entropy can violate D4.} Its hard cutoff gives a kinase just above
the baseline $\beta$ an arbitrarily small weight. A profile with many such
kinases therefore has high entropy, and adding one kinase that is bound more
strongly, but still below $\tau^*$, concentrates the distribution and raises the
selectivity score. For example, with $\beta = 5.0$ and $\tau^* = 6.0$, a profile
with one kinase at $\mathrm{p}K_d = 7.0$ and fifty at $5.1$ has an
entropy-based selectivity of $-4.89$ bits, and adding one kinase at
$\mathrm{p}K_d = 6.0$ raises it to $-4.83$.
On Davis, Klaeger, and Metz, adding one kinase at $\tau^*$ never raises the
entropy's score for a compound with an active kinase, at any baseline in their
sweeps. On Anastassiadis it does at baselines below the $20\%$ floor. With raw
percent inhibition as weights ($\beta = 0\%$), it raises the score for $34\%$ of
the 134 compounds with an active kinase, and for $16\%$ at $\beta = 5\%$, but
for none at $15\%$ or above. Compounds with no active kinase are left out
because D1 withholds their scores. Adding real kinases rarely does this: expanding random 50- or 100-kinase panels by 1 to 30 kinases that a
compound binds below threshold raises the entropy's score in at most $0.1\%$
of cases.

\textit{The candidate cannot violate D4.} By the proposition in Methods, no
sub-threshold addition can raise its score, and its condition on $T$ is sharp: just below it, a profile with one strong target
and enough kinases just above the floor violates D4. With $T = 1$ on Metz or
$T = 10$ on Anastassiadis, both below the bound, constructed profiles of this
kind violate D4 (by up to $0.014$ bits on Anastassiadis), although none of the
real compounds does. Setting $T = \tau^* - x_0$ (Methods) instead changes
the Metz and Anastassiadis rankings negligibly (Spearman $0.999$ against $T = 1$
and $T = 10$, respectively).
