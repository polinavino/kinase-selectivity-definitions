\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Datasets, Target Selection and Missing Values}
\label{sec:methods:data}

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes the four datasets. The Davis
dataset\cite{Davis2011} reports dissociation constants from a
competition-binding assay, and the Klaeger dataset\cite{Klaeger2017} apparent
dissociation constants from Kinobeads chemoproteomics in cell lysate. Both were
converted to $\mathrm{p}K_d = -\log_{10}(K_d / 10^9)$.\cite{DeepDTA} The
Anastassiadis dataset\cite{Anastassiadis2011} reports residual kinase activity
in a radiometric assay at $0.5~\mu$M inhibitor, which we converted to percent
inhibition ($100$ minus residual activity, clipped to $[0, 100]$). The Metz
dataset\cite{Metz2011} reports $\mathrm{p}K_i$ values from a
medicinal-chemistry campaign. Each score is computed on the native readout of
its dataset, and datasets are compared only through rank correlations computed
within each.

\paragraph{Target selection.} Selectivity across kinases needs one entry per
kinase, chosen without reference to any compound's affinities. The released
Davis panel lists 433 constructs. They include mutant, phosphorylation-state
and single-domain constructs of the same gene (12 for EGFR), 20 lipid kinases
and three non-human kinases, and for 26 of the 68 compounds the two best-bound
constructs are forms of one gene. We keep one reference construct per human
protein-kinase gene: the wild-type construct, or its catalytic or N-terminal
kinase domain where several domains are profiled. Genes profiled only as
mutants are removed. The 343 Klaeger targets are the proteins the Kinobeads
pulldown quantified, including complex subunits, such as cyclins, captured with
their kinases. We keep the 217 single-gene protein kinases that Klaeger et
al.\ annotate, except Q6ZSR9, which UniProt lists as an uncharacterized protein
without a kinase domain. This removes lipid kinases, four protein groups, and
BCR and TRRAP, which the source does not annotate as kinases and flags as
possibly co-enriched with ABL1 and CDK8. On Anastassiadis we keep the
first-listed of nine kinases profiled in two complexes or isoforms. The Metz
columns are already one human protein kinase each. The code repository lists
every target with the reason it was kept or removed, and Supporting
Information (Section~S1) compares the released panels.

\paragraph{Floor-coded and missing values.} Several kinds of pair receive the
same floor value $x_0$, which is not a measured detection limit. On Davis, the
released matrix assigns $\mathrm{p}K_d = 5.0$ ($K_d = 10~\mu$M) to pairs with
no detected binding, following DeepDTA;\cite{DeepDTA} Bosc et al.\ used nearly
the same value ($10.1~\mu$M).\cite{Bosc2017} On Klaeger, we kept the 5{,}123
drug--target pairs that the original authors classified as high confidence. We
coded at $\mathrm{p}K_d = 5.0$ three kinds of pair: those with no detected
binding, those whose fit was classified as low confidence, and the 194 measured
affinities weaker than $10~\mu$M. A low-confidence fit does not establish weak
binding: of the 405 such pairs on curated targets, 375 have a fitted
$\mathrm{p}K_d$ above 5. Giving all of these pairs the floor value is an
analysis convention. On Metz, pairs that were not tested are coded at
$\mathrm{p}K_i = 4.0$, the lowest value reported in the data. A median Metz
compound is measured against only 21 of the 172 kinases, so we kept the 704
compounds measured against at least 50 (median 64). On Anastassiadis, the
$1.1\%$ of missing entries were set to $0\%$ inhibition. There we place $x_0$
at $20\%$ as an operational cutoff for weak inhibition, since $54\%$ of the
entries lie between $0\%$ and $20\%$. A compound is active against a target
above the activity threshold $\tau^*$.

\subsection{Selectivity Scores and Their Implementations}
\label{sec:methods:defs}

A compound's profile is the vector $\mathbf{x} = (x_1, \ldots, x_N)$ of its
readouts across the $N$ panel targets, with higher values for tighter binding
or stronger inhibition. Every score is oriented so that higher values are more
selective (Table~\ref{tab:implementations}).

The S-score of Karaman et al.\cite{Karaman2008} is the fraction of the panel
bound above a threshold,
\begin{equation}
  S(\tau) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{1}[x_i > \tau],
\end{equation}
negated. They reported $K_d$ cutoffs of $3~\mu$M and $100$~nM, which lie
within our sweep of $\tau$. The selectivity entropy of Uitdehaag and
Zaman\cite{Uitdehaag2011} is the Shannon entropy
$H = -\sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i$ of the profile normalized to a distribution,
negated. In its published form, $p_i = K_{a,i} / \sum_j K_{a,j}$, with
association constants $K_{a,i} = 1/K_{d,i}$. To place all four definitions on
one scale, we also use a log-affinity form (on Anastassiadis, percent
inhibition), which weights each target by its readout above a baseline
$\beta$:
\begin{equation}
  p_i = \frac{\max(x_i - \beta,\, 0)}{\sum_j \max(x_j - \beta,\, 0)}.
\end{equation}
The baseline is a parameter we introduce; it has no published reference
values. Graczyk's Gini coefficient\cite{Graczyk2010} measures how unequally
binding is spread over the panel. Its published form uses percent-inhibition
values. We compute it on the same baseline-subtracted weights,
\begin{equation}
  G(\beta) = \frac{2\sum_{i=1}^{N} i \cdot w_{(i)}}{N \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_{(i)}} - \frac{N+1}{N},
  \qquad w_{(i)} = \max(x_i - \beta,\, 0)\ \text{sorted ascending},
\end{equation}
and on Anastassiadis also in its native form. The ratio compares the primary
target with the $k$-th ranked off-target,
\begin{equation}
  R_b(k) = x_{(1)} - \max(x_{(k+1)}, b),
\end{equation}
where $x_{(j)}$ is the $j$-th largest value. The level $b$ is the lowest coded
value ($5.0$ on Davis and Klaeger, $4.0$ on Metz and $0\%$ on Anastassiadis),
except in the recovery analysis, where it is $x_0$, and in D2, where it is
swept. At this level $R_b(k)$ equals Bosc et al.'s ranking score at rank
$k + 1$.\cite{Bosc2017} We cap $k$ at 5 because half of the Klaeger compounds
have fewer than five active kinases. Unless stated otherwise, the entropy and
Gini are in their log-affinity forms.

\paragraph{Conventions.} Where the entropy and Gini are undefined, we set both
to $0$. This ranks the compound as maximally selective under the entropy and
minimally selective under the Gini. $R_b(k)$ uses $b$ for a missing
off-target and so scores every compound, as Bosc et al.'s ranking score does.
Log-affinity weights with $\beta = x_0$ give floor-coded pairs no weight.
Association constants give them a small positive weight if they are kept at the
floor value. For the published entropy and $P_{\max}$ we give them no weight.
As sensitivity analyses we keep them at the floor value and, on Klaeger, use
every measured high-confidence affinity at its measured value, including those
weaker than the floor; neither of the first two treatments is free of
assumptions.

\paragraph{Comparators.} The active count, the entropy with $\beta = \tau^*$
and the S-score with $\tau = x_0$ (the share of the panel with detected
binding) are scored only for compounds with a target above $\tau^*$. The
partition index of Cheng et al.\cite{Cheng2010} is taken at the most strongly
bound target, as Uitdehaag and Zaman did.\cite{Uitdehaag2011} The CATDS score
of Klaeger et al.\ needs a designated target and Kinobeads dose--response
curves, which the other three datasets lack, so it is not compared across
datasets; a proxy is compared in Supporting Information (Section~S6).

\subsection{Ranking Comparisons and Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:methods:stability}

For each score and parameter value, compounds are ranked from most selective
(rank 1), with ties given their average rank. Ranks are always computed among
a stated set of compounds. Analyses of compounds with an active kinase rank
them among themselves, which applies D1 (below); analyses that compare
compounds with and without an active kinase rank all compounds. Agreement
between two definitions is the Spearman correlation between their median rank
vectors over each definition's parameter sweep: $\tau$ in steps of $0.25$
(11 values) and $\beta$ in steps of $0.25$ (7 values) on the $\mathrm{p}K$
datasets, $\tau$ in steps of $5\%$ (9 values) and $\beta$ in steps of $5\%$
(7 values) on Anastassiadis, and $k = 1, \ldots, 5$. Using mean ranks instead
changes no all-compound correlation by more than $0.08$.

A compound's rank instability is the standard deviation of its rank across a
sweep, within one definition or pooled over all four definitions' sweeps.
Pooling weights each definition by its number of parameter values, so the
S-score carries the most weight.
Profile features (the number of active kinases, the top$_1$--top$_2$ affinity
gap, the second-ranked affinity, and the standard deviation, range and
skewness of the active affinities) are correlated with within-definition
instability by Spearman correlation. A feature undefined for a compound with
too few active kinases is set to $0$, and a check excludes these compounds.
These associations are exploratory: a Bonferroni correction covers only the 24
tests per dataset of the feature table, and $p$-values are otherwise
uncorrected. Bootstrap intervals use 1{,}000 resamples of compounds.

To compare the effect of the dataset with that of the definition, we matched
46 compounds profiled in both Davis and Klaeger by PubChem CID, restricted both
panels to the 201 kinases they share (matched by gene symbol, with renamed
symbols mapped to current ones), and correlated each definition's
rankings between the datasets and the definitions' rankings within each
dataset. Analyses used Python 3 with NumPy, pandas and
SciPy;\cite{NumPy,pandas,SciPy} Supporting Information (Table~S9) names the
script behind every result.

\subsection{Evaluation Criteria and the Gated Entropy}
\label{sec:methods:criteria}

A selectivity measure is a function $f$ of a profile, defined for every panel
size $N$, with higher values more selective. Let $B = [x_0, \tau^*]$, the
interval between the floor and the activity threshold, where an analyst must
decide how much weak binding counts.

\textbf{D1 (activity eligibility).} $f(\mathbf{x})$ is undefined, or flagged as
not eligible, whenever $\max_i x_i \leq \tau^*$. A compound with no target
above $\tau^*$ has no target against which to judge selectivity. D1 is a
reporting rule and is assessed from each score's reporting convention. Bosc et
al.\ withheld ranks for such compounds under the S-score only;\cite{Bosc2017}
D1 applies the rule to every score.

\textbf{D2 (distributional consistency).} Let $\theta$ be the parameter that
places a score's activity boundary: the S-score's $\tau$, the entropy's and
Gini's $\beta$, the gated entropy's gate $x_0$ (with $\beta$ moved with it and
$T$ fixed), and the ratio's level $b$. A
pair of compounds \emph{reverses in $B$} if $f(\mathbf{x}; \theta) -
f(\mathbf{y}; \theta)$ is positive at some $\theta \in B$ and negative at
another. D2 requires that at most $\varepsilon = 1\%$ of the pairs of compounds
scored under D1 reverse in $B$, on every dataset and, for the ratio, for every
$k$. A score that fixes such a parameter, such as the active count, is assessed
with the parameter swept, so D2 concerns a family of scores. D2 is a
boundary-sensitivity diagnostic. Whether a score should satisfy it depends on
the use: a score whose question changes with the threshold can reasonably give
different rankings at different thresholds. Fixing the boundary defines the
analysis protocol; it does not remove the sensitivity, which D2 reports. The
S-score is evaluated at every observed value in $B$, where its value changes.
The gated entropy's gate is evaluated there and just below each observed value,
where its score jumps, and on 201 equally spaced values; the other parameters
are evaluated on the 201 values. Apart from the S-score, the percentages are
therefore estimates on a grid.

\textbf{D3 (monotonicity under weak-target addition).} For any $\mathbf{x}$ with
$\max_i x_i > \tau^*$, if $\mathbf{x}' = (x_1, \ldots, x_N, x_{N+1})$ with
$x_0 < x_{N+1} \leq \tau^*$, then $f(\mathbf{x}') \leq f(\mathbf{x})$. Otherwise a
compound can be made to look more selective by profiling it against more
kinases it binds weakly. D3 is assessed from each score's functional form. It
concerns adding a target, not strengthening one already in the profile.

We state no criterion for the ratio's choice of reference off-target, because
different references answer different questions.

\paragraph{The gated entropy.} For a profile with $\max_i x_i > \tau^*$
(undefined otherwise, as D1 requires), define
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \mathbf{1}[x_i > x_0]\; T \log\!\big(1 + e^{(x_i - \beta)/T}\big), \qquad
  p_i = \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j}, \qquad
  f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i p_i \log_2 p_i,
\end{equation}
with the emphasis baseline $\beta = x_0$ and the smoothing width
$T = \tau^* - x_0$ ($1$ $\mathrm{p}K$ unit on Davis and
Klaeger, $2$ on Metz, and $30$ percentage points on Anastassiadis). It is the
negative Shannon entropy of a gated, smoothed profile. The non-binder gate
$\mathbf{1}[x_i > x_0]$ gives floor-coded pairs no weight. The softplus
replaces the entropy's hard cutoff $\max(x_i - \beta, 0)$, which gives a
kinase just above $\beta$ a weight close to zero.

\textbf{Proposition.} With $\beta = x_0$ and
$T \geq (\tau^* - x_0)/\ln(2^e - 1) \approx 0.58\,(\tau^* - x_0)$, adding a
kinase with a value in $(x_0, \tau^*]$ never raises $f$. With
$T = \tau^* - x_0$ this holds for every $\beta \in B$.

The proof (Supporting Information, Section~S4) uses a lower bound on the
weights. Every gated target has weight above $m = T\ln 2$, and the added
target has weight at most $e\,m$. This bound on its share of the total weight
is enough for the entropy not to decrease. The bound on $T$ cannot be lowered.
The same minimum weight makes the score discontinuous at $x_0$: for a profile with
one target at $\mathrm{p}K_d = 7$, a second target at the floor leaves the
score at $0$, and one just above the floor lowers it by $0.80$ bits. To
measure the effect of noise near the gate, we perturbed the measured values
above the floor (normal noise with standard deviation $0.1$ and $0.3$
$\mathrm{p}K$ units), set values pushed below the floor to the floor, and left
floor-coded pairs fixed. On Anastassiadis every entry was perturbed, with
standard deviation $3$ and $9$ percentage points. Each setting used 50 draws,
and D1 was applied again to the noisy profiles.

\subsection{Recovery from Smaller Panels}
\label{sec:methods:recovery}

Subpanels of $m$ targets were drawn uniformly at random from the curated
targets, 50 per size, at $m = 10$, $20$, $30$, $40$, $50$, $65$, $80$, $100$,
$125$, $150$, $200$, $250$ and $300$ below the full panel, and every score
was computed on the same subpanels. D1 was applied to every score on every
subpanel. Coverage is the share of compounds scored on the full panel that are
still scored on the subpanel; it is the same for every score. Recoverability
is the share of clearly separated full-panel pairs (ranks at least a quarter
of the scored compounds apart) that are scored on the subpanel and keep their
order, with a pair tied on the subpanel counted as one half. Conditional
agreement is the Spearman correlation between subpanel and full-panel scores
over the compounds scored on both. Each score is compared with its own
full-panel ranking, and its clearly separated pairs are chosen from that
ranking, so recovery measures how much of a score's own ordering a subpanel
keeps, not which score is more accurate. We write $p^*$ for the smallest tested
size at which the mean conditional agreement over subpanels exceeds $0.90$, a
descriptive benchmark that we chose. As a
diagnostic, each compound's rank error at 50 targets is correlated with the
largest change in its full-panel score when one target is left out. Results
without the D1 gate, which mix ranking with the conventions for undefined
scores, are in Supporting Information (Section~S5).
