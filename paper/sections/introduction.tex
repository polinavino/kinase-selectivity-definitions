\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Kinase inhibitors are among the most intensely investigated compound classes
in drug discovery, with 94 FDA-approved agents and several hundred in
clinical development\cite{Roskoski2026}. The human kinome is comprised of more than
500 proteins with a conserved ATP-binding site\cite{Manning2002}, and
selectivity is a primary criterion in lead optimization\cite{Bhullar2018}.
Profiling assays measure a compound against a panel of tens to hundreds of
kinases, and the resulting profile is often reduced to one selectivity score.
Several scores are in use:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the S-score\cite{Karaman2008}, the fraction of the panel bound below an
        affinity cutoff
  \item the selectivity entropy\cite{Uitdehaag2011}, the Shannon entropy of the
        profile treated as a distribution
  \item the Gini coefficient\cite{Graczyk2010} of the profile
  \item the partition index\cite{Cheng2010}, the share of binding that falls on
        one target
  \item ratio-based measures, which compare the primary target with an
        off-target.
\end{itemize}
They are often used interchangeably, although each summarizes a different
aspect of the profile.

Earlier work established that these scores can disagree. Bosc et
al.\cite{Bosc2017} compared six scores on the Davis and Anastassiadis datasets,
computed several at more than one parameter value, compared them on compounds
and kinases shared between datasets, and found that compound rankings differ
substantially between them. They traced individual
disagreements to profile shape, such as a broad binder with one dominant
target. The KInhibition platform\cite{KInhibition2018} noted that conflicting
results across metrics are common, and introduced a composite score. A
later resource\cite{Gujral2026} from the same group offers a choice of scores.
Miljkovic and Bajorath\cite{Bajorath2018} found the concentration-dependent
selectivity score of Klaeger et al.\cite{Klaeger2017} broadly consistent with
activity data from ChEMBL. Karaman et al.\cite{Karaman2008} and Uitdehaag et
al.\cite{Uitdehaag2012} showed that the S-score and the entropy of individual
compounds vary when they are computed on random subpanels.

What has not been characterized systematically is how analysis choices alter
agreement between the scores. Such choices include: how affinities are weighted, 
which compounds are
ranked, how pairs without detected binding are treated, which assay targets
count as kinases, and where each score's parameter is set. It is also not
known which associations between profile shape and ranking instability persist
across these choices, or how much of a ranking a smaller panel retains once
some compounds can no longer be scored. 

This work adds a systematic account of these choices. We compare the S-score,
entropy, Gini and ratio on four datasets that span three assay technologies.
Each is curated to one reference target per human protein kinase, with the
entropy and Gini both in a common log-affinity form as well as in their published
forms. We vary the compounds ranked, the treatment of
pairs without detected binding and each score's parameter, and relate the
resulting instability to features of the binding profile. From these results
we state four criteria for a selectivity measure: activity eligibility,
consistency across placements of the activity boundary, monotonicity when a
weakly bound kinase is added, and orientation on profiles with equally strong
targets. We evaluate the existing scores and simple
comparators against them. We also give a gated, smooth-hinge entropy, prove
that it satisfies all criteria. We then show that, once its coefficients are fixed,
the criteria fix a unique measure. Stating such criteria
follows the axiomatic approach Shannon\cite{Shannon1948} used for entropy.
We measure how well each score's ranking is recovered
from random subpanels, and for how many compounds, then give recommendations
for reporting selectivity scores.
