\begin{abstract}
Kinase selectivity scores can rank the same compounds differently. We compare
four common scores across four profiling datasets. Agreement changes with
affinity weighting, the compounds ranked and the treatment of pairs without
detected binding. We relate ranking sensitivity to binding profile features.
We propose four criteria for evaluating selectivity scores. No score is
consistent across activity boundaries, so the boundary must be fixed by
protocol. We construct a gated entropy that gives weight to weak binding and
prove that adding a weakly bound target cannot make a compound appear more
selective. On these data, it ranks compounds almost as the share of targets
with detected binding does. Scores that follow this share recover full-panel
rankings from fewer targets than the ratio and the published entropy, but on
small panels many compounds with few active targets cannot be scored. These
findings support reporting analysis choices, ranking sensitivity and compound
coverage.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} binding selectivity,
cancer drug discovery, cheminformatics, kinase inhibition, research methodology.
