Kinase selectivity scores can rank the same compounds differently. We compare
four common scores across four profiling datasets. Agreement changes with
affinity weighting, the compounds ranked and the treatment of pairs without
detected binding. We relate ranking sensitivity to binding profile features.
Moving the activity boundary reorders compounds under every score, so the
boundary must be fixed by protocol. We propose four criteria for selectivity
scores and, with Shannon's grouping rule, derive from them a gated entropy with
exponential affinity weights. We prove the range of weighting for which adding
a weakly bound target cannot make a compound appear more selective. Scores that
follow the share of targets with detected binding recover rankings from fewer
targets than the ratio and the published entropy, but on small panels many
compounds with few active targets cannot be scored. These findings support
reporting analysis choices, ranking sensitivity and compound coverage.
