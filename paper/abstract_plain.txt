On four kinase profiling datasets, we asked which analysis choices change the
rankings given by the S-score, entropy, Gini and ratio. Agreement depends on
how affinities are weighted, which compounds are ranked, and how pairs without
detected binding are treated. Using log-affinities, the S-score, entropy and
Gini agree more with one another than with the ratio among active compounds;
the published entropy does not. Moving the activity boundary reverses 8-13% of
compound pairs for these three scores on three datasets and over 20% on the
fourth. No boundary parameter we tested meets our criterion of consistency
across placements, so the boundary must be fixed by protocol. Monotonicity when
a weakly bound kinase is added holds for simple measures and a gated entropy we
construct. On subpanels, scores that follow the share of targets with detected
binding recover rankings from fewer targets than the ratio and the published
entropy, but some compounds then cannot be scored.
