Kinase inhibitor selectivity is quantified using several competing
definitions, including S-score, selectivity entropy, Gini coefficient, and
ratio-based measures, which are often used interchangeably. Across four
profiling datasets spanning three assay technologies (68-704 compounds each),
we show that, with entropy and Gini computed on a common log-affinity scale,
these definitions split into two groups: the S-score, entropy and Gini agree
more with one another than with the fold-selectivity ratio. Rank instability
is associated with binding profile shape. We trace instability to three
sources: convention-dependent scores for compounds with no binding above the
activity threshold, near-tied top targets, and distribution-ratio disagreement
for promiscuous compounds, often with a dominant target. We motivate four
properties that a well-formed selectivity measure should satisfy. None of the
four assessed satisfies all of them. We then propose a measure that satisfies
all four when its activity boundary is fixed at the assay floor, recovering
its full-panel ranking from about a tenth to a quarter of a panel on three of
the four datasets.
