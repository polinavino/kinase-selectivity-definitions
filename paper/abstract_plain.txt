Kinase inhibitor selectivity is summarized by several competing scores,
including the S-score, selectivity entropy, Gini coefficient and ratio-based
measures, which are often used interchangeably. We asked which analysis choices
change the resulting compound rankings, on four profiling datasets spanning
three assay technologies, each curated to one reference target per human
protein kinase. Agreement between the scores depends on how affinities are
weighted, which compounds are ranked, and how pairs without detected binding
are treated. With entropy and Gini on a common log-affinity scale, the S-score,
entropy and Gini agree more with one another than with the ratio among
compounds with an active kinase. The entropy as originally published, with
association-constant weights, does not. Moving the activity boundary between
the floor and the activity threshold reverses 8-13% of compound pairs under the
S-score, entropy and Gini on three datasets, and over 20% on the fourth, whose
rankings largely follow how many kinases were measured. We state three criteria
for a selectivity measure: activity eligibility, consistency across placements
of the activity boundary, and monotonicity when a weakly bound kinase is added.
No boundary parameter we tested meets the second, so the boundary must be fixed
in the analysis protocol. Monotonicity holds for simple measures such as a
count of active kinases and for a gated, smooth-hinge entropy we construct,
which on these data ranks compounds almost as the share of targets with
detected binding does. On random subpanels, scores that rank compounds by this
share recover rankings from fewer targets than the ratio and the published
entropy, but some compounds can no longer be scored.
