Kinase inhibitor selectivity is quantified using several competing
definitions, including the S-score, selectivity entropy, Gini coefficient, and
ratio-based measures, which are often used interchangeably. We propose four
properties that a well-formed selectivity measure should satisfy: a
reliability threshold, bounded sensitivity to the reference off-target,
consistency across placements of the activity boundary, and monotonicity when
a weakly bound kinase is added. None of the four definitions satisfies all of
them, nor even the second and fourth together: the ratio fails bounded
sensitivity, and the others fail monotonicity. The properties are motivated by
four profiling datasets spanning three assay technologies. Moving the activity
boundary between the assay floor and the activity threshold reverses 8-12% of
compound pairs under the S-score, entropy and Gini on three of the datasets,
and over 20% on the fourth. Rank instability is associated with binding
profile shape, and the most unstable compounds have no binding above the
activity threshold, which the definitions score inconsistently, near-tied top
targets, or broad profiles, often with one dominant target. With entropy and
Gini on a common log-affinity scale, the S-score, entropy and Gini agree more
with one another than with the fold-selectivity ratio, most clearly on the two
pKd datasets, but not for the entropy as originally published. A gated,
smooth-hinge variant of the entropy satisfies all four properties, the second
vacuously and the third with its activity boundary fixed. It ranks these
datasets almost identically to the entropy but, unlike it, provably satisfies
monotonicity.
