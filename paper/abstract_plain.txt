Kinase inhibitor selectivity is quantified using several competing
definitions, including the S-score, selectivity entropy, Gini coefficient, and
ratio-based measures, which are often used interchangeably. We propose four
properties that a well-formed selectivity measure should satisfy: a
reliability threshold, bounded sensitivity to the reference off-target,
consistency across placements of the activity boundary, and monotonicity when
a weakly bound kinase is added. None of the four definitions satisfies all of
them. The properties are motivated by four profiling datasets spanning three
assay technologies (68-704 compounds each), on which rank instability is
associated with binding profile shape and has three sources: conflicting
scores for compounds with no binding above the activity threshold, near-tied
top targets, and distribution-ratio disagreement for promiscuous compounds.
With entropy and Gini on a common log-affinity scale, the S-score, entropy and
Gini agree more with one another than with the fold-selectivity ratio, a
grouping that fails for the entropy as originally published. A gated, smooth-
hinge variant of the entropy satisfies all four properties when its activity
boundary is fixed at the assay floor, ranks these datasets almost identically
to the entropy, and recovers its full-panel ranking from about a tenth to a
quarter of a panel on three of the four datasets.
