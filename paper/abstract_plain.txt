Kinase inhibitor selectivity is quantified using several competing
definitions: S-score, selectivity entropy, Gini coefficient, and ratio-based
measures, which are applied interchangeably without justification. Across four
profiling datasets spanning three assay technologies (68-704 compounds each),
we show that, with entropy and Gini computed on a common log-affinity scale,
these definitions split three to one: the S-score, entropy and Gini agree
closely while the fold-selectivity ratio agrees with none. Rank instability is
associated with binding profile shape. We trace instability to three sources:
convention-dependent scores for compounds with no binding above the activity
threshold, near-tied top targets, and distribution-ratio disagreement for
promiscuous compounds with a dominant target. We motivate four properties that
a well-formed selectivity measure should satisfy. None of the four assessed
satisfies all of them; the one property all four violate is a reliability
condition for compounds without binding above threshold. We then propose a
measure that does, recovering its full-panel ranking from about a third to a
half of a panel, depending on the dataset.
