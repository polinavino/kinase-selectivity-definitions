Reviewer 1 — Round 2 Review
Manuscript: "Definitional Instability in Kinase Inhibitor Selectivity: An
Empirical Characterization and Motivated Properties for a Well-Formed Measure"


====================================================================
BRIEF SUMMARY (as initially provided)
====================================================================

Reviewer 1 Comments to the Author

In the manuscript "Definitional Instability in Kinase Inhibitor Selectivity: An
Empirical Characterization and Motivated Properties for a Well-Formed Measure,"
the author uses four public kinase profiling datasets, spanning competition
binding, chemoproteomic, and functional activity assays, to examine whether
four common selectivity definitions rank compounds consistently. Varying the
unstandardized parameter of each definition, the author shows that S-score,
entropy, and Gini coefficient cluster closely, while fold-selectivity ratio
agrees with none. The author then relates rank instability to binding profile
features and identifies three sources of instability. These sources motivate
four properties that a well-formed selectivity measure should satisfy. While
none of the four definitions assessed satisfies all four properties, the
author proposes a gated, smooth-hinge effective target number and reports that
it recovers its full-panel ranking from roughly a third of the kinase panel.

The manuscript addresses a question of practical importance, since
practitioners routinely apply these definitions interchangeably, and the
expansion to 4 datasets and the transparent reporting of limitations,
including a null clinical outcome analysis, have strengthened it
considerably. However, the manuscript would benefit from significant
consolidation, as its principal findings are currently difficult to follow
through repeated results and accumulated qualifications. I recommend major
revision, with the main requests concerning the faithfulness of the entropy
and Gini implementations, the treatment of filtered, imputed, and undefined
entries, the formalization of the proposed properties, and evidence that the
candidate measure improves on selectivity entropy.

FULL REVIEW (as subsequently provided)
====================================================================

Manuscript title: Definitional Instability in Kinase Inhibitor Selectivity: An
Empirical Characterization and Motivated Properties for a Well-Formed Measure

Summary:
In the manuscript "Definitional Instability in Kinase Inhibitor Selectivity: An
Empirical Characterization and Motivated Properties for a Well-Formed Measure,"
the author uses four public kinase profiling datasets, spanning competition
binding, chemoproteomic, and functional activity assays, to examine whether
four common selectivity definitions rank compounds consistently. Varying the
unstandardized parameter of each definition, the author shows that S-score,
entropy, and Gini coefficient cluster closely, while fold-selectivity ratio
agrees with none. The author then relates rank instability to binding profile
features and identifies three sources of instability. These sources motivate
four properties that a well-formed selectivity measure should satisfy. While
none of the four definitions assessed satisfies all four properties, the
author proposes a gated, smooth-hinge effective target number and reports that
it recovers its full-panel ranking from roughly a third of the kinase panel.

The manuscript addresses a question of practical importance, since
practitioners routinely apply these definitions interchangeably, and the
expansion to 4 datasets and the transparent reporting of limitations,
including a null clinical outcome analysis, have strengthened it
considerably. However, the manuscript would benefit from significant
consolidation, as its principal findings are currently difficult to follow
through repeated results and accumulated qualifications. I recommend major
revision, with the main requests concerning the faithfulness of the entropy
and Gini implementations, the treatment of filtered, imputed, and undefined
entries, the formalization of the proposed properties, and evidence that the
candidate measure improves on selectivity entropy.

Major comments:

I recommend more clearly stating that the analysis concerns re-operationalized
forms of entropy and Gini, and to show the correlation matrix under the
published forms where it is computable.

On Davis, Klaeger, and Metz, entropy and Gini are computed on baseline
subtracted log-affinities rather than on association constants and
%inhibition, and Figure 1 shows that these operationalizations rank compounds
differently from the published forms (entropy 0.42 and Gini 0.11 on Davis,
-0.32 and -0.45 on Klaeger). The common scale is reasonable and is disclosed in
Methods. However, results involving entropy and Gini pertain to these
implementations rather than to the published definitions, and the central
claims would benefit from a clearer statement of this distinction.
Additionally, the author should also report the correlation matrix under the
original operationalizations as a robustness check, using the association
constant entropy on Davis, Klaeger, and Metz, computed on measured entries
only, and the native %inhibition Gini on Anastassiadis.

On Klaeger, entries failing the original authors' high-confidence annotation
were assigned pKd = 5.0; a true binding interaction removed by that filter is
excluded from the active set and alters the entropy, Gini, and S-score. The
author should report how many entries the filter removed, and the sensitivity
of Table 1 and the subsampling results to relaxing this filter. The Metz
inclusion rule is justified as retaining "profiles in which most entries
reflect a real measurement," but 50 of 172 kinases is 29%. Please restate the
threshold or report the coverage distribution across the retained Metz
compounds.

Entropy and Gini are undefined for any compound with no kinase above the
baseline beta, yet R(k) is computed for the zero-active compounds and
contributes to their pooled instability. The convention by which these
undefined scores were ranked across the parameter sweep is not stated. Please
state the convention and report the per-definition contrast under an
alternative, such as rank sigma computed only over configurations in which
score is defined.

The candidate measure satisfies D3 because its boundary between active and
inactive kinases is fixed at the assay detection floor, rather than by its
functional form. This rationale treats the floor as a fixed property of the
assay, which does not hold for every dataset, since the Metz floor is the
lowest measurement reported in the released data and the Davis and Klaeger
floors are values imputed for non-binding. When the floor itself is varied
across [4.5, 6.5], the author reports a comparable change in ranking for the
candidate measure and entropy, which suggests that the candidate measure
remains as sensitive to the placement of the activity boundary as entropy. The
author should define the quantity reported as rho ~ -0.93 and address whether
the floor can be regarded as fixed across the four datasets.

The right panel of Figure 6 appears to compare entropy's sensitivity to its
activity boundary with the candidate measure's sensitivity to its emphasis
baseline, which does not alter the active set. The 17-fold reduction annotated
on the panel may therefore reflect the parameter chosen rather than a property
of the measure. Please state which parameter was varied for the candidate
measure and present the floor sweep as the equivalent comparison.

The D3 verdicts for entropy and Gini rest on an empirical sweep over a finite
range of beta rather than on the formal definition, which is ambiguous as
written and, under its local reading, is satisfied by both measures for any
pair with distinct scores. The author should restate D3 so that the verdicts
follow from its definition and distinguish empirical verdicts in the
properties table from those derived from the functional form.

D1 is the only property that all four existing selectivity definitions
violate, and for entropy it is the only violation once D3 is read as formally
stated (see #6 above). The conclusion that none of the four satisfies all four
properties therefore depends largely on the absence of a reliability gate,
which is also the principal difference between the candidate measure and
entropy. Table 2 further records vacuous and substantive satisfaction with the
same symbol, although the text identifies the D2 verdicts for entropy and Gini
and D3 verdicts for the ratio and the candidate measure as vacuous or
dependent on a fixed parameter. Please state the conclusion more directly, and
I would suggest giving vacuous satisfaction its own symbol in Table 2.

The 5 most unstable Klaeger compounds (pg. 16) are described as having no
meaningful binding to any profiled kinase, yet AZD-8055, BMS-911543,
vatalanib, filgotinib, and ARRY-380 are selective inhibitors of mTOR, JAK2,
VEGFR2, JAK1, and HER2, respectively. Their zero-active status more plausibly
reflects poor expression or capture of these targets. Please revise the Type 1
description accordingly.

The manuscript would benefit from benchmarking definitional instability
against the ranking variability introduced by assay platform. Without new
data, compounds profiled in both Davis and Klaeger would allow the author to
report whether definitional choice changes relative rankings more or less than
platform choice, provided the overlap is sufficient to support the comparison.

All selectivity definitions considered, including the candidate measure, are
symmetric functions of the binding profile, so two compounds with identical
affinity distributions receive identical scores whether or not their
off-targets carry known clinical liabilities. None accounts for exposure,
although pg. 7 briefly acknowledges that occupancy at physiological
concentrations determines whether an interaction is pharmacologically
relevant. The author should discuss how these properties limit the use of any
single selectivity measure in the drug discovery decisions the Discussion
describes.

The motivated properties and candidate measure are introduced in standalone
sections after the Results, without a corresponding description in Methods,
and combine definitions, empirical verification, and variant measures. Several
Results paragraphs also retain layered qualifications from earlier revisions.
The author should introduce the properties and candidate measure in Methods,
report their evaluation in Results, and consolidate the qualifications so that
the principal findings are stated plainly.

Minor comments:

Unresolved cross-references remain in the rendered PDF. "Table
[tab:predictors]" appears several times, "Section sec:results:instability]"
appears on pg. 7, and "Section 4.2" on pg. 16 points to a numbered section in
an otherwise unnumbered manuscript.

The skewness and range of affinities across active kinases are undefined or
degenerate for compounds with fewer than three and fewer than two active
kinases, respectively, and Figure 3 shows vertical clusters at exactly zero
for both features. Please state how these compounds were handled and confirm
that the association between Gini instability and skewness does not depend on
that choice.

Figure 4 presents Davis compounds, whereas the accompanying text introduces
the instability taxonomy from Klaeger. The text on pg. 17 states that the most
stable compounds in both datasets are highly promiscuous, but the most stable
Davis compound in Figure 4 has two active kinases. The figure legend also
labels as "baseline" the line the caption describes as the assay floor. Please
reconcile the figure, caption, and text.

The word "predictable" in the Abstract, Conclusion, and graphical abstract
overstates in-sample correlations of 0.27 to 0.44 in magnitude. "Associated
with binding profile shape" would more accurately describe the evidence.

Several results, including the correlation-cutoff sensitivity, the CATDS and
Bosc et al. comparisons, and the candidate measure checks on Davis,
Anastassiadis, and Metz, are reported only in the code repository. Given that
the candidate measure was corrected after resubmission, these results should
appear in Supporting Information.
