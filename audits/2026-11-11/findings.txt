Audit of the kinase selectivity manuscript, November 11, 2026

The current LaTeX manuscript's principal numerical results reproduced. The
largest immediate risk is that the upload guide points to an obsolete Word
manuscript. I also found an overgeneralized cross-dataset recommendation, an
overstated reversal percentage, a clinical null-result wording issue, a small
population mismatch in the filter summary, and a reproducibility bug in the
raw-data fallback. None of the checks below established corruption of the
currently committed matrices or a failure of the gated-entropy proofs under
their stated assumptions.

The manuscript and existing analysis files were left unchanged. This directory
contains the audit report, a read-only verification script, and its JSON output.
The companion contribution_review.txt evaluates the clarity of the central
point, the contribution relative to the cited prior work, and support from
the body, with concrete recommendations for the abstract and conclusion.
The older AUDIT_FINDINGS.md concerns an earlier version and was used only as a
source of leads; its verdicts were not carried forward as current findings.

1. HIGH: The prescribed clean Word submission is a different, obsolete paper.

Location: paper/UPLOAD_GUIDE.txt:15-16;
paper/molecular_informatics_submission.docx; paper/build_docx.sh.

The upload guide names molecular_informatics_submission.docx as the clean main
document. Reading its actual document.xml shows the old title, "Definitional
Instability in Kinase Inhibitor Selectivity: An Empirical Characterization and
Motivated Properties for a Well-Formed Measure". It uses a softplus candidate,
the former D2 (bounded gap sensitivity), the former D3 (distributional
consistency), and a claim of recovering rankings from about a third to a half
of a panel. It lacks the revised exponential-weight derivation and the curated
217-target Klaeger and 363-target Davis panels. The other explicitly named
second-round Word file is also old.

The current main.pdf, supporting_information.pdf and LaTeX sources have the
new title and revised content. Thus agreement between source and PDF does not
make the named Word upload safe. Uploading it alongside the new figures,
tables and response would give reviewers mutually inconsistent versions.

Action: regenerate the clean Word document from the final LaTeX source, inspect
the resulting equations and tables, and generate any redline from that same
final version. Check the actual intended upload, rather than its filename.

2. MEDIUM: The cross-dataset robustness recommendation is broader than the
analysis that supports it.

Locations: paper/sections/discussion.tex:73-76;
paper/supporting_information.tex:813-816; claim_checks.py:243-252.

The recommendation says "Rank instability is not a guide to robustness across
datasets." SI says rank instability is not associated with cross-dataset rank
movement, citing |r| <= 0.18. Those numbers reproduce, but the diagnostic
correlates every definition's displacement with the SAME aggregated sigma over
all four definitions. It does not use that definition's within-sweep sigma.
It also uses the full, different target panels, and instability computed among
the original 68/222 compound populations. It is narrower than the matched
compound/target analysis immediately preceding it in SI and does not establish
an absence of useful associations under other instability definitions.

Keeping the published displacement calculation unchanged and using S-score
instability within the S-score sweep gives Davis r = 0.3270, nominal p = 0.0265.
Ranking the 46 matched compounds among themselves before computing their
within-definition instabilities gives Davis S-score r = 0.3361, p = 0.0224.
These exploratory results do not survive an eight-test Bonferroni correction
and do not establish a general predictive relationship. They do establish
that the |r| <= 0.18 statement is specific to the aggregate definition.

Action: say "In this 46-compound comparison, aggregated rank instability was
only weakly associated with cross-dataset rank displacement (|r| <= 0.18)."
Specify the populations and panels. If retaining a recommendation about
within-definition parameter sensitivity, evaluate that quantity directly on
matched panels and eligible compounds, with uncertainty and multiplicity
reported. The existing null result supports caution, not a general no-guide
conclusion.

3. MEDIUM: "For every score ... at least 13.7%" silently maximizes over the
ratio's off-target parameter.

Location: paper/sections/results.tex:245-248;
outputs/d3_pair_reversals.txt; d3_pair_reversals.py.

The manuscript says that for every score, moving the boundary reverses at least
13.7% of pairs on at least one dataset. The ratio reaches 13.71% only at k = 5
on Klaeger. For R_b(1), the largest fraction across all four datasets is 3.00%:
Davis 0.00%, Klaeger 3.00%, Anastassiadis 2.13%, Metz 0.04%. R_b(2), R_b(3),
and R_b(4) also never reach 13.7%. Moreover, the immediately preceding text
correctly says the published entropy and P_max have no boundary parameter.

The script's actual summary is more precise: every swept PARAMETER in Table S3
reaches at least 13.71% somewhere, taking the maximum over k for the ratio.
The main text changes that quantifier to every score.

Action: write "Every boundary parameter studied reverses compound pairs; the
maximum over datasets, and over k for the ratio, is at least 13.7% for each
parameter family." Alternatively retain the actual ranges already reported
at results.tex:155-163 and omit the universal percentage. This does not affect
the conclusion that fixing the boundary is consequential.

4. LOW TO MEDIUM: Clinical nonsignificance is summarized as absence of an
association.

Location: paper/sections/discussion.tex:108-111;
paper/supporting_information.tex, Section S7;
clinical_outcomes_correlation.py.

The main text says the selectivity rankings "were not associated" with serious,
fatal or hospitalization report proportions. The actual result is that none
of 12 correlations was statistically significant in 46 drugs. The largest
observed association, hospitalization versus Gini rank, is r = 0.1931 with
p = 0.1984. An illustrative paired percentile bootstrap over drugs (3,000
resamples; seed 42) gives a 95% interval of [-0.1393, 0.4858]. That interval
does not exclude moderately positive associations. This bootstrap is an audit
diagnostic, not a replacement for a prespecified clinical analysis.

The manuscript appropriately describes FAERS proportions as proportions of
reports. I did not find a current claim that they estimate patient incidence,
and the dropped FDA-label discontinuation data are not used by this analysis.

Action: change to "We detected no statistically significant associations in
this exploratory 46-drug analysis." Retain the report-based interpretation and
avoid interpreting the null as clinical equivalence or evidence that
selectivity cannot matter for adverse events.

5. LOW: The main-text filter-sensitivity summary omits its fixed-population
condition.

Locations: paper/sections/results.tex:134-136;
paper/supporting_information.tex:130-135;
klaeger_filter_sensitivity.py:174-182.

The main text reports a change of at most 0.01 in active-compound correlations
when low-confidence fits are restored. SI correctly specifies the 200 compounds
active under BOTH filters. The analysis deliberately fixes this intersection.
Restoring the fits actually increases the eligible population from 200 to 202,
adding Brivanib_alaninate and TG-100115. Using each filter's own eligible set
changes the S-score/ratio correlation from 0.37778 to 0.39093 (delta 0.01315),
versus a maximum change of about 0.006 on the fixed intersection.

Action: carry the SI's qualifier into the main text: "among the 200 compounds
active under both filters." The scientific effect remains small. This is an
estimand/population clarification, not a consequential reversal of the result;
0.013 also rounds to 0.01 at two decimal places.

6. MEDIUM FOR REPRODUCIBILITY: The Anastassiadis raw-data fallback silently
bypasses target curation.

Location: additional_datasets_analysis.py:127-140;
curate_panels.py, Anastassiadis section.

When anastassiadis_matrix.csv exists, the analysis uses the correct curated
178 x 291 panel. If it is absent, load_anastassiadis() reconstructs ALL 300 raw
columns, writes them under the same filename, and immediately analyzes them
without the nine duplicate-complex/isoform removals. That file then persists
for other scripts, which assume this filename denotes the curated matrix.

This did not affect the current reruns: the committed curated file exists,
matches the raw data on retained columns, and is reproduced by curate_panels.py.
The fallback is nevertheless an advertised path for regenerating missing data
and can silently produce results inconsistent with Methods.

Action: have the fallback create the released matrix and call a shared curation
function before writing/using the curated matrix, or fail with a clear request
to run curation. Validate the panel's expected target identities, not just its
shape, at the analysis boundary.

Verification performed

Fourteen scripts were run successfully in an isolated temporary copy. All
their printed numerical output matched the committed outputs (absolute paths
to generated figures were normalized for the comparison):

  additional_datasets_analysis.py
  bosc_catds_comparison.py
  claim_checks.py
  clinical_outcomes_correlation.py
  curate_panels.py
  d3_pair_reversals.py
  d4_entropy_vs_candidate.py

  instability_by_family.py
  klaeger_filter_sensitivity.py
  metric_fidelity_robustness.py
  original_operationalization_robustness.py
  panel_recovery.py

The Klaeger, Anastassiadis and Metz matrices were independently rebuilt from
their raw spreadsheets and compared against the committed curated matrices.
Maximum absolute discrepancies were approximately 1.8e-15, 1.4e-14 and zero,
respectively. Rerunning target curation reproduced all three curated panels
and all three curation tables byte for byte. Davis curation was checked
against its released matrix; acquisition from the upstream repository was
not repeated.

The recovery benchmarks reproduced: S-score at the floor reaches the chosen
mean-Spearman 0.90 bar at 20/50/30/65 targets (Davis/Klaeger/Anastassiadis/Metz),
the default gated entropy at 50/65/30/80, and twice its scale at 20/50/30/65.
These are conditional agreement with each score's own full-panel ranking.
The current manuscript correctly explains coverage loss, the chosen mean
benchmark, and why recovery does not establish better biological selectivity.

I checked the algebra and assumptions of Propositions 1-3 and reproduced their
numerical examples and adversarial checks. I did not identify a counterexample
to the stated propositions. Proposition 2 explicitly assumes distance-based
weights and Shannon grouping; it does not establish uniqueness among all
possible selectivity measures. The T lower bound is a universal guarantee over
arbitrary profile length, rather than a demonstrated optimal scale for a fixed
finite kinome. These restrictions are substantially stated in the paper.

Primary-source spot checks confirmed the Bosc window/ranking definitions and
its S-score-specific withholding convention, the radiometric Anastassiadis
assay, the cited 2026 review's count of 94 FDA-approved inhibitors, and the
existence of the Binder paper's cited correction:

  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5217660/
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3230241/
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41587611/
  https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.6c02488

The audit was not an exhaustive verification of every bibliography entry,
every raw experimental measurement, or upstream chemical identity. It did not
redownload the public datasets or refresh FAERS; refreshing a live clinical
snapshot would change the object being checked. No manuscript edits or
submission regeneration were performed.

To reproduce the independent checks saved here:
  python3 audits/2026-11-11/verify.py

The script prints the evidence in verification.json and does not execute the
analysis scripts' top-level logging/writes. The 14 full reruns were performed
separately in a temporary copy to preserve the reviewed repository state.
