Contribution and argument audit, November 11, 2026

Verdict: the body supports a useful empirical contribution and a separate,
conditional mathematical contribution. The current manuscript does not yet
give them a consistent hierarchy. Its title foregrounds analysis choices,
its abstract lists several contributions, and most of its conclusion focuses
on the gated entropy's scale. A reader can reasonably finish unsure whether
the main advance is an analysis of ranking protocols or a new score.

A clear central point supported by the body would be:

  Apparent agreement and robustness of kinase selectivity rankings depend on
  how profiles are weighted, coded and selected for scoring. On smaller
  panels, agreement among the compounds still eligible can conceal the loss
  of narrowly active compounds.

This is more informative than "scores disagree" or "analysis choices matter."
It identifies what changes, explains why the usual evidence for agreement or
robustness needs qualification, and gives the reader a practical consequence:
report the scoring protocol and retained compound coverage together.

The strongest contribution, with its supporting evidence

1. Demonstrate which analysis choices change the interpretation of score
agreement, using explicit alternative implementations on four curated panels.

Results, "Analysis Choices Change Agreement Between Scores," supplies direct
comparisons: published versus log-affinity entropy, two treatments of
floor-coded pairs, all versus active compounds, and released versus curated
targets. Table 3 and Figure 1 show that the apparent grouping of the scores
is contingent on those choices. On Klaeger, for example, all-compound Gini
agrees more with the ratio than with the S-score, whereas active-compound
Gini agrees more with the S-score. On Metz, agreement largely follows
measurement coverage. These are concrete changes in interpretation, rather
than a generic warning that parameters can change numbers.

The contribution should be described as controlled within-dataset sensitivity
comparisons across several choices. The body does not provide a full factorial
decomposition of every choice, estimate their independent causal effects, or
validate a universal ordering of their importance.

2. Evaluate small-panel rankings jointly with compound eligibility and pair
recovery, rather than conditional rank correlation alone.

Results, "Recovery from Small Panels," and Figure 3 directly support this
contribution. At 50 targets, only 71% of the full-panel-eligible Klaeger
compounds remain eligible. A compound with one active target remains scored
with probability 0.23, compared with 0.93 for one with ten. Thus conditional
agreement favors a surviving population depleted of narrowly active compounds.
The paper measures coverage, conditional agreement and recovery of separated
pairs separately. The finding that some scores reach a correlation benchmark
quickly is useful only alongside those coverage results.

This is a strong practical extension relative to the random-subpanel studies
described in the Introduction. The paper should lead with the distinction
between recovering the order of survivors and retaining the compounds that
were originally to be ranked. It should not present fast self-recovery as
evidence of better biological selectivity; the body correctly avoids that.

The formal contribution and the role it can support

The formal contribution is a characterization of a class of scores and a
sharp monotonicity bound: within the stated distance-based weighting class,
with Shannon grouping, the criteria select exponential/equal weights, and
weak-target-addition monotonicity requires T >= tau* - x0 for the default gate.
Methods Propositions 1-3, SI S4 and the worked counterexamples support this.

This is not supported as a claim that the gated entropy is the best selectivity
score. The body explicitly says it is not an empirical improvement on simpler
scores (results.tex:388-392). Simpler counts also meet all four criteria. The
default gated entropy recovers rankings more slowly than the floor S-score;
doubling its scale matches recovery while making the rankings nearly identical
to that simpler score (results.tex:480 onward).

That does not make the formal result empty. Its contribution is to show what
these chosen requirements imply, and what their satisfaction does not settle.
It can serve the paper's main argument by separating a construction guarantee
from empirical recovery and biological validation. It should be introduced
as a second contribution with that purpose, rather than as the solution to
the empirical sensitivity findings.

Specific weaknesses in the current argument

A. The abstract is an inventory rather than a hierarchy.

Location: paper/sections/abstract.tex:2-14.
It moves from score comparisons to profile associations, boundary changes,
criteria, derivation, proof, panel recovery and reporting. It does not identify
which result changes the reader's understanding most. "We relate ranking
sensitivity to binding profile features" reports an activity without its
outcome, and gives an exploratory secondary analysis equal rhetorical status
with the main findings.

Revision: state the central empirical finding first, give one concrete example
or the coverage consequence, then introduce the formal result as a separate
contribution. If space is tight, omit the generic profile-association sentence.

B. The final Introduction paragraph has competing endpoints.

Location: paper/sections/introduction.tex:49-70.
It begins with a systematic analysis of choices, spends most of its latter
half explaining the new entropy and its scale, and only then returns to the
subpanel question. The novelty relative to earlier comparisons is spread
across this paragraph and the preceding gap paragraph.

Revision: state the contribution in two explicit parts: (i) a comparison
protocol that separates implementation, eligibility and coverage effects;
(ii) a conditional characterization of affinity-weighted scores under the
proposed criteria. Describe profile associations as supporting diagnostics.
The earlier work already established score disagreement and some parameter
and panel dependence, so those facts alone should not be the novelty claim.

C. The phrase "From these results we state four criteria" skips a normative
step.

Location: paper/sections/introduction.tex:55-58.
The observations motivate proposing requirements. They do not establish that
boundary locality and weak-target-addition monotonicity are necessary for
every useful meaning of selectivity. D2 in particular privileges dependence
on membership above a cutoff, whereas affinity concentration is also a
legitimate objective discussed in the paper. Passing the criteria and
recovering one's own ranking are distinct evaluations.

Revision: say "Motivated by these sensitivities, we propose four design and
reporting criteria and examine their consequences." Keep the existing scope
restrictions close to the derivation. Do not imply the data prove that all
applications must adopt this particular set of criteria.

D. The conclusion gives the secondary contribution the final emphasis.

Location: paper/sections/conclusion.tex:12-24.
After summarizing the empirical message, it spends its remaining text on the
gated entropy and ends by asking users to report its scale. This makes the
paper read as if the main endpoint is adoption of that score, despite the
body's finding of no empirical improvement and the broader title.

Revision: compress the formal result to one or two sentences, and end on the
principal contribution: score agreement and small-panel robustness must be
interpreted with the implementation, eligible compounds and coverage stated.
The scale comparison can remain a worked example of that general point.

E. The strongest empirical distinction should appear earlier in Results.

Locations: results.tex:410 onward; results.tex:231-409.
The coverage/recovery contribution follows a long criteria-and-candidate
section. For a paper whose central question is how analysis choices affect
rankings, readers encounter the most actionable consequence of those choices
only after a substantial formal detour.

Revision: consider the sequence (1) agreement changes with implementation and
eligibility, (2) boundary sensitivity, with profile associations compressed,
(3) panel recovery and coverage, (4) criteria and the formal characterization.
Keep the candidate scale experiment with the fourth part and link it back to
recovery. This is an editorial recommendation, not a requirement to rerun the
analyses or an assertion that the present order is invalid.

F. Exploratory side analyses should not carry the main contribution.

Profile-feature correlations are modest, population-dependent, and only the
ratio-gap association remains significant on both pKd datasets after the
reported correction. They support examples and diagnostics, rather than a
general predictive account of instability. The clinical analysis is ancillary
and its null result does not validate or invalidate the scoring framework.
The current SI placement for much of this material is appropriate.

Suggested contribution statement

  We distinguish changes in selectivity rankings caused by score
  implementation and activity eligibility, and evaluate recovery from
  smaller panels jointly with the compounds retained for scoring. Across
  four datasets, these choices change apparent agreement between scores,
  while high agreement on small panels can coexist with the loss of
  narrowly active compounds. Separately, we characterize an affinity-weighted
  entropy family under explicit design assumptions and show that satisfying
  those assumptions does not confer an empirical recovery advantage.

This statement is supported by the existing body. It does not require a new
analysis to make it true, subject to correcting the narrower overclaims in
findings.txt. It does require the abstract, contribution paragraph and
conclusion to share the same emphasis.

Novelty scope: this assessment distinguishes the contribution from the prior
work actually discussed and spot-checked during the audit. It is not an
exhaustive priority search establishing that no earlier publication made a
similar distinction. The relevant comparison paper is Bosc et al. (2017):
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5217660/
